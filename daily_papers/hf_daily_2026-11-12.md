## 1. Tree-Ising: Repairing Language Models with Bit-Level Ising Edits
**Upvotes:** 👍 77 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.14210`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14210) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14210.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14210)

### Abstract:
> Weight-space programming, introduced in recent MIT work by Takehi et al., enables a frontier LLM agent to read a network's weights and write a repair. Their GPT-6 Astra agent corrected the reasoning loops of Qwen3.5-0.8B without gradients, using 158 agent turns, 89.7 million tokens, and 50 forward-pass requests. Our method, Tree-Ising, casts this repair as a sparse discrete optimization problem over the model's own BF16 weights. Forward statistics define an objective that weakens a loop direction, strengthens a concluding direction, and preserves the layer's output on productive tokens. Within a state-tree representation, where lossless BF16 storage acts as a special case, the admissible edits are sign flips and exponent shifts that preserve all shared group exponents and mantissas. We decompose this objective exactly into independent QUBO (Ising) problems, one per output row, which we solve on a GPU using simulated annealing or quantum-inspired simulated bifurcation. On the agent's two layers, Tree-Ising achieves 54.6% on MATH-500, outperforming the agent's edit (51.7%) under the same evaluation protocol, while the base model scores 7.9% as reported. The simulated bifurcation solver yields a statistically indistinguishable repair at 55.1%. Both solvers outperform the agent edit across all five math benchmarks, with statistically significant improvements on MATH-500 and GSM8K after correcting for multiple comparisons. Our approach modifies 13% to 17% fewer weights and reduces BF16 bit flips by a factor of 2.2 to 2.3. It requires only four forward-pass requests and zero LLM tokens, with the annealing recipe costing about $0.90 in GPU time. On Qwen3.5-2B, with layers and dose selected using only the base model, the same recipe increases greedy GSM8K accuracy from 21.6% to 69.4%. As a proof of concept, we execute an eight-variable repair subproblem using QAOA on an IBM Heron processor.

## 2. Few-Step Streaming Video Generation via Condition Shifting
**Upvotes:** 👍 70 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.14033`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14033) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14033.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14033)

### Abstract:
> Few-step sampling reduces the cost of streaming video generation. Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD) supervises few-step generators with a separate fake-score network, which adds training memory and computation and must track the evolving generator. We extend APEX to streaming video by using condition shifting to estimate the velocity field of the generated distribution within the generator. The original text condition selects generation, while a shifted condition selects fake estimation using the same parameters. This removes the independent fake network while retaining S2-Avatar's frozen reference teacher, Self-Replay Forcing, and perceptual regularization. We evaluate APEX on 180-second videos using three backbone evaluations per block and a one-step spatial Refiner, with a 70-case human study and a 320-case StreamAV-Bench comparison. Compared with S2-Avatar, APEX achieves higher video instruction fulfillment on the Progressive track (3.856 versus 3.831) and higher visual aesthetics across both tracks (0.641898 versus 0.632606). Human raters favor APEX most strongly on instruction following, with +30.0% net preference, followed by consistency and video quality at +4.3% each. Motion quality favors S2-Avatar, audiovisual synchronization is predominantly tied, and usability is unchanged. Separate measurements under matched training settings show 4.4% lower peak allocated GPU memory per GPU and 9.2% shorter mean iteration time than DMD.

## 3. World In-Context Learning for Agentic Robot Manipulation
**Upvotes:** 👍 66 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.12885`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12885) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12885.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12885)

### Abstract:
> Trained action policies write manipulation skill into network weights, tying what is learned to the distribution it is learned from. In-context learning (ICL) avoids this coupling but has not matched trained policies, because a demonstration recorded in another scene carries no feedback from the world the robot currently faces. We observe that what an agent learns in context is semantic, recording task steps and object roles rather than visual appearance, so experience gathered in one world transfers to another. Based on this insight, we propose World In-Context Learning (WICL), a paradigm in which a frozen model starts from a demonstration, rehearses the task in a world such as a simulator, and writes the feedback into its context rather than its weights. We build RoboScript, a modular infrastructure that implements WICL for agentic manipulation. From a single demonstration, RoboScript learns each task through rehearsal in simulation, freezes the learned knowledge as a Task Memory, and deploys the frozen memory on new scenes with data isolation that ensures every deployment input is available on a real robot. On all 50 tasks of RoboTwin 2.0, RoboScript reaches 83.4% mean success across clean and randomized settings with one demonstration per task and no gradient updates, exceeding the strongest trained action policy by over 20 points. The method generalizes across visual distributions, improves through reflection, scales with the foundation model, and transfers to a physical dual-arm robot through cross-embodiment memory adaptation. With a world in the loop, a robot can learn a manipulation task in its context as well as it learns one in its weights. All code is available at https://github.com/ArcheOne-AI/RoboScript

## 4. Don't Just Trust Your Memory: Evidence-Linked Memory Reduces Memory-Induced Degradation in LLM Agents
**Upvotes:** 👍 58 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.14320`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14320) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14320.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14320)

### Abstract:
> External memory enables LLM agents to improve from experience by distilling trajectories of past tasks into concise lessons for new tasks. However, memory can also lead to memory-induced degradation, where an agent fails on tasks it would solve without memory, often because it follows a lesson that is incorrect, or misapplies or misreads a valid one. This is because lessons are inferred from trajectories labeled only with task success or failure, yet are typically provided without the trajectories they came from, leaving the agent no way to check them. To address this, we introduce WikiTrace, an evidence-linked memory system designed to let agents check each lesson against its original trajectory. Specifically, motivated by the finding that a trajectory often still holds the evidence needed to catch an erroneous lesson, WikiTrace stores each lesson as a concise insight linked to a summary of why its trajectory succeeded or failed and to the raw trajectory itself. Then, on a new task, the agent starts from all insights and, whenever one lacks details needed to act, conflicts with another, or comes from a failed task, descends through its links with terminal tools to fill in missing details or correct the insight before acting. We validate WikiTrace on five agentic benchmarks, showing that it consistently outperforms existing memory approaches and a baseline given the same insights alone, while also reducing memory-induced degradation. Our code is available at https://github.com/yukyeongleee/wikitrace

## 5. Infrathon: Benchmarking Language Agents for Real-World, Long-Horizon AI Infra Optimization
**Upvotes:** 👍 35 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.11995`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11995) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11995.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.11995)

### Abstract:
> In this work, we study whether frontier agent can continually improve real-world AI infrastructure over long horizons. We introduce Infrathon, a benchmark of diverse, long-horizon AI infrastructure optimization tasks across different levels. We evaluate six models across 150 runs with 24-hour budgets, totaling over 10,000 GPU-hours. Our results show that model rankings vary with the evaluation horizon, while 135 of the 150 runs achieve further improvements during the second half of their budgets. These findings indicate that shorter evaluations can overlook both optimization gains and changes in relative model performance. We also find agents can discover solutions that outperform human-designed baselines: Claude Opus 5.5 increases the mean KV-cache compression ratio from 6.82% with the human-designed Quant-VideoGen codec to 19.86%. Analysis of agent trajectories identifies task violations, unreliable testing, and premature completion as barriers to sustained improvement. Together, Our Infrathon framework provide resources for constructing long-horizon tasks, evaluating frontier agents, and discovering good infrastructure solutions.

## 6. dNaViT: Discrete Codes are General-Purpose Visual Representations
**Upvotes:** 👍 33 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.12426`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12426) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12426.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12426)

### Abstract:
> Discrete autoregressive modeling, notably through next-token prediction, has become a cornerstone of modern language models. Extending this paradigm to vision allows visual and language models to converge on a unified framework, thereby fostering shared methodology and infrastructure. This work investigates a central question: how far can a finite discrete vocabulary go in representing the richness of the visual world? We present dNaViT, a Discrete Native-resolution Vision Transformer that produces discrete visual primitives analogous to language tokens. Built upon a frozen pretrained encoder, dNaViT applies lightweight residual modulation with a dual-branch objective to inject reconstruction-aware cues into semantic features. By leveraging grouped product quantization, it scales discrete capacity without inflating spatial sequence lengths, while a balanced training recipe maintains both semantic and pixel-level information. Consequently, dNaViT functions as a versatile discrete visual tokenizer, enabling unified encoding across downstream tasks alongside high-fidelity image decoding. Extensive evaluations show that dNaViT matches or exceeds task-specific baselines across 12 visual understanding benchmarks, 4 generation benchmarks, and an image editing benchmark. These findings demonstrate that a compact discrete vocabulary can serve as a universal visual currency and unlock the full potential of fully unified, native multimodal methodology.

## 7. MMDM: Measuring Massive Multitask Decision Making
**Upvotes:** 👍 26 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.14309`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14309) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14309.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14309)

### Abstract:
> System-1 Decision Models directly make structured probabilistic decisions over query-specified options given a state in a single forward pass, without autoregressively generating reasoning trajectories or textual responses. Recent evaluations cover diverse tasks but often rely on existing benchmarks, where prior exposure to test material can obscure generalization. Moreover, real-world decision making often involves long contexts, large option spaces, and complex logical dependencies, challenges that existing benchmarks do not adequately capture together. Thus, we introduce MMDM (Massive Multitask Decision Making), a benchmark of 5,448 high-quality questions across 16 task categories, combining newly constructed tasks with source-grounded recompositions and rewrites for single-select, multi-select, and soft-label decision making in fresh contexts. It covers realistic scenarios such as agent state tracking, tool and skill selection, preference judgments, and rubric scoring, with option spaces ranging from 2 to 1,024 and inputs extending to 64,817 tokens. With MMDM, we conduct a comprehensive quantitative evaluation of 28 decision models, showing that many perform well on common knowledge yet struggle with interdependent options, constrained decisions, and multi-select tasks. For example, Perplexity Decider-V1.2 reaches 78.75% Hard Single-select Accuracy on world knowledge, but only 53.95% on option logic and 47.95% on constraint planning, while the best Hard Multi-select Accuracy across all models is 36.81%. These results highlight joint reasoning, constraint-aware selection, and complete multi-select recovery as key priorities for decision model development, with MMDM providing a testbed for measuring this progress.

## 8. DexForesight: Grounding Dexterous Policies with Implicit Spatial Foresight
**Upvotes:** 👍 24 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.11166`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11166) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11166.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.11166)

### Abstract:
> Dexterous manipulation requires rich spatial foresight over both the surrounding scene and the robot hand. Existing world-action models often acquire such predictive knowledge through explicit future prediction. We introduce DexForesight, a framework that learns spatial foresight implicitly by injecting dynamics-aware world representations into the latent space of a dexterous policy. During training, DexForesight learns an action-conditioned spatial world model from offline scene depth, kinematic hand geometry, and demonstration trajectories. The world model predicts 3D hand-point motion and visual feature changes, capturing both articulated hand dynamics and scene changes under demonstrated actions, including the motion of fingers occluded from the cameras. We then freeze the world model and align policy queries with its predictive world queries, transferring the learned spatial dynamics into policy latents and using them to condition action generation. At deployment, only the policy is retained, operating on RGB observations, language, and robot state without depth input or explicit future prediction. On 11 DexJoCo simulation tasks, DexForesight improves the overall success rate of the base policy from 49.4% to 60.5%. On three real-robot dexterous manipulation tasks, DexForesight achieves 58.3% success, compared with 50.0% for the strongest baseline. Project page https://dexforesight.github.io/

## 9. Embodied First Exam
**Upvotes:** 👍 23 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.12177`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12177) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12177.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12177)

### Abstract:
> Early successes on agentic robotics point to a potential paradigm shift toward using coding agents to control robots. Existing robotics benchmarks are built around visuomotor policy learning, leaving it open whether coding agents can reliably control diverse robots under physical-world constraints. We therefore introduce the Embodied First Exam, a unified, comprehensive, and agent-native evaluation framework where coding agents can choose their own control approach: direct control, code to control, or train a policy to control. We hope it serves as a first exam for coding agents before they enter the physical world, with 1,156 tasks in 35 suites on 47 robots, including 9 new suites that go beyond pick-and-place to spatial puzzles, dexterous manipulation, multi-robot interaction, dynamic whole-body control, and sim-to-sim engineering. Each task can be run in up to three evaluation settings. The Standard Setting mimics the real world: the agent receives only sensor observations and, by default, cannot reset the environment. Its Realtime variant keeps the environment running while the agent reasons. The Privileged Setting gives the agent access to the full simulator state and unlimited resets, allowing it to search for an oracle solution for training purposes. Success rates in this setting serve as an approximate upper bound. On the 456 core tasks, the strongest of eight agents, GPT-6 Astra in Codex, achieves a success rate of 34% in the Standard Setting, leaving much room for improvement. To see where agents fail, we label the stage at which failed attempts go wrong and compare failures across agents, settings, and stage categories.

## 10. One Table, Many Queries: Benchmarking Generalist Tabular Models Across Generation, Imputation, and Prediction
**Upvotes:** 👍 22 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.13001`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13001) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13001.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13001)

### Abstract:
> Generation, imputation, and prediction query different aspects of the same tabular distribution, but are usually evaluated separately. This leaves unclear which capabilities they share, how their training objectives interact, and whether one model can perform all three well. We introduce the One Table Benchmark (), spanning 56 datasets, 46 registered methods, and 20 capability axes under aligned data splits and explicit task contracts. We compare task ranks on aligned support, train five backbones under all seven non-empty objective combinations on five datasets with three seeds, and contrast fixed-model profiles with specialist portfolios. Rank comparisons connect imputation and prediction through numerical recovery and dependence preservation, and link generation to both through predictive utility; distributional fidelity follows different, sometimes opposing, rankings. Objective interventions show the largest pooled gains from including the recipient's own objective. Generation has the broadest coverage of positive pooled auxiliary effects, while prediction shows potential to improve imputation and synthetic-data utility, with responses that vary across backbones. Cross-addition effects reveal that objective benefits need not add and depend on the objectives already present. Fixed-model comparisons expose corresponding utility--fidelity trade-offs: some generalists surpass specialist portfolios on utility, while fidelity continues to favor specialization. Frozen foundation models show strong prediction and numerical point imputation, together with promising synthetic-data utility, highlighting their potential across all three tasks. These findings motivate unified tabular models that share useful conditional information while improving categorical reconstruction and faithful joint sampling.
