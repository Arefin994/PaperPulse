## 1. The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents
**Upvotes:** 👍 386 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.36077`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36077) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36077.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36077)

### Abstract:
> Multimodal intelligence can be viewed as human-guided co-evolution between worlds and agents through interaction. However, scaling this process faces two bottlenecks: constructing heterogeneous, verifiable environments is costly, and manual Harness iteration cannot keep pace with growing task diversity and complexity. A key insight is that tasks with different modalities and surface forms can share the same underlying capability requirements. We therefore introduce The Trinity, a recursive self-improvement framework coupling world generation with Harness evolution through three roles: Creator, Explorer, and Evolver. Drawing on verified execution trajectories, Evolver shifts the basis of task organization from surface categories to shared requirements, reorganizing the original task tree into a hierarchical capability tree. We introduce Capability-Driven Real2Sim2Real: for heterogeneous tasks at the same node, Creator abstracts their common requirements into simplified, verifiable simulated worlds for capability learning, while evaluation on original tasks measures transfer. Along this capability tree, Explorer progressively evolves reusable capability modules through selective inheritance, forming a Harness tree whose validated updates accumulate across iterations within a fixed topology. The resulting Harnesses specialize in different capabilities, motivating a Capability-Aware Harness Router that uses public task information to select compatible module configurations from the tree. Our evolved Harnesses improve mean performance by 34 points across 28 training benchmarks and 26 points across 18 unseen benchmarks, including two real-world embodied tasks.

## 2. Multiscale Policy Optimization for Long-Horizon LLM Agents
**Upvotes:** 👍 380 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.35013`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35013) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35013.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35013)

### Abstract:
> Long-horizon LLM agents interact with their environments through complete assistant turns while generating each action token by token. We observe that critic values vary primarily across turns, motivating optimization anchored at turns with finer credit allocation among tokens. We propose Multiscale Policy Optimization (MPO), combining Multiscale Advantage Estimation (MAE) and turn-level importance sampling to align credit assignment and policy updates at the turn scale. Extensive experiments across search, tool-use, and automation tasks with 4B and 30B-A3B models demonstrate consistent gains over strong RL baselines.

## 3. Efficient Streaming Audio-Visual Target Speaker Extraction for Real-World Acoustic Scenes
**Upvotes:** 👍 150 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.32749`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32749) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32749.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32749)

### Abstract:
> Streaming audio-visual target speaker extraction (AVTSE) is essential for latency-sensitive applications. However, current causal systems face two coupled gaps. First, standard benchmarks fix the number of speakers at two and ignore the near-/far-field interference, reverberation, and sudden noise of real scenes. Second, high-performance causal architectures are too heavy for edge deployment. Lightweight alternatives try to reclaim capacity by repeatedly invoking a small separator, which offsets the savings. To address the first gap, we release RealSSA, a realistic AVTSE benchmark consisting of RealSSA-Sim and RealSSA-Real. RealSSA-Sim provides controllable simulated scenes with 1--5 dynamic near-field interfering speakers; scenes with far-field interference additionally include 10--15 far-field interfering speakers. RealSSA-Real provides a held-out real-recorded evaluation set. To address the second gap, we propose Falcon, a causal AVTSE method. Its Separator performs multi-scale time-frequency separation in a single encoder--decoder pass. We train Falcon with an encoder-space speaker contrastive loss. This loss suppresses near-field leakage at zero inference cost and transfers across backbones. Falcon achieves state-of-the-art extraction quality on RealSSA-Sim, LRS2-2Mix, LRS3-2Mix, and VoxCeleb2-2Mix. Compared with the causal AV-TFGridNet baseline, it cuts parameters by 91.9%, computation by 20.2 times, and GPU inference latency by 11.1 times. Code and demo are available at https://cslikai.cn/Falcon/.

## 4. PDRVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Model with Physical Dynamics Representations
**Upvotes:** 👍 149 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.37381`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37381) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37381.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37381)

### Abstract:
> Vision--Language--Action (VLA) models generate robot actions from visual observations and language instructions, but their understanding of physical dynamics remains limited. Video world models can capture scene dynamics, yet generating future videos introduces high latency into the control loop. We introduce PDRVLA, whose lightweight Physical Dynamics Module extracts physical tokens from vision--language features to condition action generation without online video generation. The module learns through direct prediction of future RGB, depth, and optical-flow features, with auxiliary supervision through a frozen pretrained video world model. Training relies entirely on publicly available data, expanded through human-to-robot synthesis, subtask annotation, and embodied chain-of-thought (ECoT) annotation. Across four simulation benchmarks, PDRVLA achieves success rates of 99.3% on LIBERO, 89.9% on LIBERO-Plus, 93.6% on RoboTwin~2.0, and 81.5% on SimplerEnv. Ablations show improved future prediction with world-model supervision and evaluate how subtask conditioning, future object-pose supervision, and ECoT training affect action success.

## 5. VibeVideoBench: Evaluating Multimodal Agents on Code-Driven Video Generation
**Upvotes:** 👍 89 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.35864`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35864) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35864.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35864)

### Abstract:
> Diffusion video models produce striking clips, but they cannot reliably render an exact formula, a correct chart, a rule-consistent game or narration synchronized to on-screen events. A different route is emerging: multimodal agents that write code to render every frame and synthesize every sound. We introduce VibeVideoBench, a benchmark for evaluating agents on code-driven video generation. Its 239 self-contained tasks in English and Chinese span 12 categories, from data stories and music-driven pieces to classroom lessons and paper explainers; each asks an agent, running in its native harness inside a sandbox with no image or video generation model, to deliver a finished MP4 with sound within one hour. Evaluation is hierarchical and evidence-based: program checks of the delivery spec, about 13k task-specific rubric checks traced to the requirements they enforce, and five quality dimensions anchored per task, scored by three VLM judge panels. Across nine frontier agent systems and 2,151 scored rollouts, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 rank first and second with both the judges and human annotators, and the strongest system satisfies every core requirement on 76.3% of tasks. Delivery is a second axis: counting missing videos as zero changes the ranking, and three quarters of the lost videos are final renders that started too late, ran too slowly or never started, a failure of how agents plan their hour. Agents build their own rendering pipelines and inspect their own frames, yet how much they look does not predict quality. The judges' ranking of the systems closely matches blind human preferences.

## 6. Can Discrete Tokens Serve as Semantically Lossless Representations of Visual Signals?
**Upvotes:** 👍 60 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.37662`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37662) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37662.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37662)

### Abstract:
> Discrete visual tokens represent vision in a discrete form analogous to language, offering a native interface for multimodal modeling under a shared next-token prediction objective. However, quantization error is widely assumed to cause unavoidable semantic loss, fundamentally limiting the semantic fidelity of discrete visual tokens and positioning continuous visual representations as an intrinsic upper bound. Using multimodal understanding as a proxy for semantic fidelity, we revisit this assumption and find quantization error unreliable, as discrete representations with substantially larger errors can achieve better understanding. Instead, the effective bit budget of discrete indices emerges as a key determinant of downstream performance. However, existing quantizers eventually saturate as additional codebooks contribute progressively less semantic information. As one approach to alleviating this saturation, we introduce Factorized Vector Quantization, which quantizes complementary, fixed-dimensional views of the visual feature with symmetric parallel codebooks. It continues to improve at larger bit budgets, ultimately matching continuous visual representations in multimodal understanding. Trained on industrial-scale multimodal data, our model achieves state-of-the-art performance among discrete-representation models, outperforming UniAR and Emu3-Chat by 22.8% and 59.2%, respectively, on a nine-metric average. To our knowledge, it is also the first discrete-representation model competitive with strong continuous-representation models such as Qwen3-VL, including on fine-grained OCR tasks. We further identify the key principles and a practical recipe enabling this competitiveness. These results and insights support the potential of discrete visual representations to underpin large-scale native multimodal foundation models. Code and models are available at https://github.com/hustvl/FVQ

## 7. OmniShowcase: Omnidirectional Subject-Consistent Video Generation from Multi-View Images
**Upvotes:** 👍 47 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.35684`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35684) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35684.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35684)

### Abstract:
> Generating videos of a specific subject from a few reference images remains challenging when the camera reveals surfaces that are not visible in the input. Early subject-to-video (S2V) methods condition on a single reference image and must therefore infer the subject's unseen appearance and geometry as the viewpoint changes. To address this limitation, recent methods extend S2V to multiple reference views, providing more information about the subject from different viewpoints. However, they do not explicitly specify the 3D structure that connects these observations. In addition, existing training data do not guarantee that viewpoint changes preserve the same underlying 3D subject. We present OmniShowcase, built on two components: (i) a fully automated data synthesis pipeline that produces training videos along diverse camera trajectories, with viewpoint variation driven by underlying subject geometry, even spanning full 360-degree orbits; and (ii) a geometry-conditioning method that provides the video model with a turntable of surface-normal maps reconstructed from the reference views without requiring temporal alignment with the generated frames. We construct Showcase-4K, a corpus of 3D-asset-based videos covering twelve camera motions across 88 trajectory configurations with reconstruction-derived surface-normal conditioning, and ShowcaseBench, a held-out evaluation set with the source meshes and camera parameters used to render each video. Experiments show state-of-the-art subject identity and 3D consistency while maintaining competitive video quality and motion. Project page: justin4ai.github.io/OmniShowcase

## 8. Truncate or Not? A Scaling Study of Looped Transformers with Truncated Backpropagation
**Upvotes:** 👍 30 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.26030`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26030) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26030.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26030)

### Abstract:
> Looped transformers reuse a shared block of layers to increase computational depth without increasing the number of trainable parameters. They are trained with backpropagation through time (BPTT). To save training compute, BPTT is often truncated to the last few loops, but truncation yields biased gradients that may weaken the benefit of looping. In this work, we study the value of truncation in looped transformer training in a systematic way: we jointly vary model size, training tokens, and forward and backward loop counts across nearly 600 training runs and fit a unified scaling law that captures the effects of looping and truncation through effective parameters. Despite the training-compute savings of truncation, our fitted law favors full BPTT when forward and backward loop counts are jointly optimized. In matched-compute runs, full BPTT through fewer loops consistently outperforms truncated settings of the same compute. The law further shows that at fixed model size, the compute-optimal loop count and training-token budget both grow with compute. When model size is also free, the law identifies two fully trained loops as the best looped setting. Experimentally, this setting achieves lower validation loss than the compute-optimal non-looped model with about 27% fewer non-embedding parameters and only 8% more training compute.

## 9. Zooming without Zooming v2: From Seeing Details to Connecting Evidence for Generalizable Multimodal Perception
**Upvotes:** 👍 28 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.37037`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37037) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37037.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37037)

### Abstract:
> Multimodal perception forms the foundation of multimodal intelligence. In particular, spatial perception and multimodal long-context understanding are two pivotal tasks that underpin embodied AI and multimodal agents. However, synthesizing targeted, high-quality training data for these tasks remains difficult and costly. Our key insight is that these tasks share an underlying capability, which we call connecting evidence: identifying relevant visual facts, binding them to the correct entities, recovering their relations, and composing them into an answer. Crucially, targeted training data are far easier to synthesize for this capability than for these tasks themselves. To strengthen this capability, we propose EvidenceConnect, an easy-to-implement data synthesis framework that uses zoomed views of related regions within a single image to generate questions requiring comparison, aggregation, or chaining of visual evidence. To ensure quality, a reference answer is retained only when independent VLMs agree on it, and region-ablation tests discard questions whose answer survives the removal of selected regions. We then train base VLMs on the resulting data with RLVR, obtaining Zooming without Zooming v2 (ZwZ2). Although the synthesized data are not tailored to any downstream benchmark, ZwZ2 improves fine-grained and spatial perception, with further gains on embodied spatial reasoning, multimodal long-context understanding, multimodal agent tasks, and general visual reasoning. These results establish EvidenceConnect as an effective framework for supervising evidence connection and suggest that strengthening this shared capability leads to more generalizable multimodal perception.

## 10. EmbeddingGemma 2: Unified and Efficient Multimodal Representations
**Upvotes:** 👍 26 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.37364`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37364) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37364.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37364)

### Abstract:
> We present EmbeddingGemma 2, an open multimodal embedding model which natively represents text, images, audio, video, and combinations thereof in a unified space. Our approach introduces improvements such as simplified per-layer embeddings and a multi-stage curriculum with modality and objective progression which ensure strong quality across unimodal, cross-modal, and mixed-modal evaluations. Notably, in comprehensive embedding benchmarks across all modalities, EmbeddingGemma 2 establishes a new Pareto frontier, outperforming both specialist models and models two to twelve times larger. Furthermore, our design offers flexibility for resource-constrained environments, supporting selective modality encoder loading, adjustable vision token budgets, truncatable embedding dimensions, and quantized weights. We present ablations analyzing key decisions and release EmbeddingGemma 2 to promote further research.
