## 1. RuleTrek: Benchmarking Agentic Rule Reasoning in Complex Scenarios
**Upvotes:** 👍 62 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.19698`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19698) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19698.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19698)

### Abstract:
> Large language models (LLMs) increasingly tackle complex real-world tasks in specialized domains. However, identifying and correctly applying domain-specific rules remains challenging when scenario information is distributed across multiple files. Existing rule-reasoning benchmarks typically represent scenarios using only a few factual statements, simplifying the identification and application of relevant rules. To address this gap, this paper introduces RuleTrek, a multi-domain benchmark for agentic rule reasoning in complex scenarios. Its rule-grounded construction pipeline extracts structured rule graphs, generates and incrementally composes rule-specific sub-scenarios while ensuring consistency and solvability. To systematically evaluate agentic rule reasoning in complex scenarios, RuleTrek assesses three capabilities in interactive workspaces: Rule Identification (RI), Rule Deduction (RD), and End-to-End Rule Reasoning (E2E). Experiments on 11 frontier LLMs show that the highest RI and RD scores are only 62.47\% and 60.43\%, respectively, while E2E scores range from 26.97\% to 55.29\%. These results demonstrate that current LLM agents face substantial challenges in rule reasoning over complex scenarios. We make our code and dataset publicly available at https://github.com/yuan247/RuleTrek{RuleTrek}.

## 2. Many Right Answers, One Training Example: Removing Responder Noise from GRPO for LLM Recommenders
**Upvotes:** 👍 39 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.17771`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17771) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.17771.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.17771)

### Abstract:
> A recommendation request on Reddit draws several replies, each naming different titles. When an LLM recommender is trained on such threads with reinforcement learning (RL), which replies should a generated list be rewarded against? The state-of-the-art recipe, Rank-GRPO, picks one reply per training example, so the advantage mostly records which reply was drawn (85% of its variance) rather than how good the list is. We argue that RL should train on the request, not on each answer, and propose ReMaRk: it scores every list once against the union of all replies' titles and samples each request equally. This makes RL cheaper. On seven backbones and three datasets, ReMaRk reaches the validation recall of 2,000 per-reply RL steps in 25 to 53% of the steps and leads at that budget; trained until neither improves, it is never significantly behind Rank-GRPO and in four of seven pairs reaches Rank-GRPO's best with fewer GPU-hours. The same reward helps on ProtoQA, where about 100 people answer each question, which points to a simple rule for RL with several valid human answers: reward them jointly.

## 3. Apertus 1.5: From Open Text Models to Open Multimodal Reasoners
**Upvotes:** 👍 34 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.15542`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15542) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.15542.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.15542)

### Abstract:
> Language models are becoming infrastructure, and infrastructure built in the open, by public institutions, on documented data, is the alternative to dependence on a few providers; Apertus shows this is viable at scale. Apertus 1.5 extends the fully open Apertus family from text to multimodal reasoning. Continued pretraining of the Apertus 1.0 models, on 4T additional tokens for the 8B and 2T for the 70B, adds image and audio understanding and extends the context window fourfold to 262,144 tokens; multimodality enters through the tokenizer, the language-model head remains restricted to text, and the architecture is unchanged. Supervised finetuning, long-context finetuning, reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, and preference alignment complete the released models. Thinking is a flag of the chat template rather than a separate model, and lifts mathematical reasoning by up to 20 points on the 8B model. The released models improve over their predecessors on nearly every benchmark; among fully open models of its class, the 8B leads on knowledge, instruction following, and cultural benchmarks, and its instruction following surpasses all compared models, including open-weight ones. On image and audio understanding, a first for the family, the models are competitive with fully open multimodal models, strongest on document understanding and long-form and lower-resource speech recognition, while specialized vision and audio models remain ahead on perception and transcription of English and other high-resource languages. As in Apertus 1.0, we train only on openly available data, respect crawler opt-outs retroactively, and exclude restrictively licensed material. We release the model weights, data preparation and training recipes, and evaluation suites under permissive licenses; openness extends to values, with the Apertus charter guiding alignment stages published alongside the models.

## 4. CLASHarness: Benchmarking Multi-Agent Conflict in Computer Use via a Controllable and Verifiable Generation Pipeline
**Upvotes:** 👍 27 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.19128`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19128) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.19128.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.19128)

### Abstract:
> Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly deployed in multi-agent systems to automate complex computer-use workflows, yet existing benchmarks mostly assume consistent, cooperative environments. In contrast, real-world collaboration rarely runs on consistent information: peer or sub-agents frequently report outdated states, issue contradictory instructions, or compete over shared permissions and files, forcing agents to check others' outputs and verify ground truth in the environment. Evaluating such conflict resolution remains challenging: manual authoring cannot scale across complex systems, while unconstrained LLM generation produces narrative drift, answer leakage, and unsatisfiable rubrics. We introduce CLASHarness (Conflict-Loaded Agent Scenario Harness), a controllable framework for generating verifiable multi-agent conflicts in computer use, with a benchmark of 600 multi-round tasks. By combining hierarchical scenario planning with an eight-step verified micro-pipeline, CLASHarness pre-commits decisive visual facts before rendering and compiles hybrid code and semantic graders directly from executed reference trajectories, guaranteeing every task is solvable by construction. Evaluating 15 frontier models on CLASHarness reveals three core bottlenecks. First, when sub-agent outputs conflict with the environment, weaker models blindly follow misleading information, whereas stronger models inspect visual evidence to verify ground truth. Second, resolving conflicts is much harder than finding them: while agents can spot inconsistencies, actively fixing system state drops task success significantly. Third, coordination protocols remain a universal bottleneck: even frontier models fail over 58% of closing actions, often forgetting to release shared locks or hand off tasks. CLASHarness provides a scalable, verifiable foundation for building reliable multi-agent systems.

## 5. The Thinking Leakage in Reinforcement Learning for LLMs
**Upvotes:** 👍 21 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.14270`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14270) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14270.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14270)

### Abstract:
> Hybrid reasoning models such as the Qwen3 series switch between a THINK mode and a NO-THINK mode through a few chat-template tokens. A popular recipe trains such models with reinforcement learning in NO-THINK mode and reports improved reasoning performance. We provide evidence that much of this gain can be explained by thinking leakage: the gain comes largely from partially reactivating the reasoning abilities of the original model's THINK mode instead of learning enhanced reasoning behavior. We train Qwen3-1.7B, 4B, and 8B with GRPO in NO-THINK mode. As accuracy improves, responses become longer and more reflective, are increasingly judged by the original model to resemble its own thinking, and the model's internal activations shift toward its THINK mode. We then investigate at the level of weights, activations, and attention heads: scaling the RL update further elicits THINK-mode behavior and approaches THINK-mode accuracy but does not exceed it; in Qwen3-8B, replacing a low-dimensional THINK-related activation subspace of the RL-trained model with its original-model value removes most of the RL gain, and the reverse exchange transfers a considerable fraction of the gain to the original model; and the RL-induced shift passes through the attention heads that read the empty think block. We further identify a possible mechanism in the GRPO training signal: responses favored by the advantage-weighted update are more THINK-like, and a gradient step computed from this signal moves the model toward its THINK state. Thus, gains in NO-THINK mode can largely reflect reactivation of reasoning abilities already present in the original model.

## 6. LabInstruct: Benchmarking Situated Instructional Video Generation for Lab Procedures
**Upvotes:** 👍 16 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.14020`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14020) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14020.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14020)

### Abstract:
> Self-driving laboratories (SDLs) aim to automate the full experimental loop, from scientific decision-making to physical execution. Ideally, AI-generated plans could be carried out directly by robotic systems, but reliable automation remains difficult in complex, open-world laboratory environments, where experiments often involve fine-grained manipulation, long-horizon procedures, and substantial variation across tasks and setups. Humans therefore remain an important execution interface between AI-generated plans and physical experiments, creating a need for clear and effective human-facing experimental guidance. Because laboratory procedures are inherently visual, spatial, and dynamic, video is particularly well suited to communicating apparatus configurations, manipulation actions, temporal dependencies, and state changes. Recent advances in video generation now make it possible to synthesize experimental demonstrations directly from an initial workspace image and a natural-language instruction. However, whether such models can reliably communicate real laboratory procedures has not been systematically studied. We introduce LabInstruct, a benchmark for situated instructional video generation in real laboratories. LabInstruct contains 204 tasks across 5 scientific disciplines, with real reference executions and structured annotations of objects, actions, contacts, and state transitions. Evaluating 8 frontier image-to-video models, we find that visually plausible generations frequently remain procedurally incorrect, revealing a substantial gap between visual realism and the reliability required for experimental instruction.

## 7. APEX-Harm: Evaluating Agent Safety in Shared Professional Workspaces
**Upvotes:** 👍 15 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.13623`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13623) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13623.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13623)

### Abstract:
> Professional agents increasingly work through command-line interfaces and multiple tools over shared workspaces containing files, messages, and business applications. These environments create safety risks that are difficult to capture with attacks placed at a single, known read location. We introduce APEX-Harm, a benchmark for evaluating prompt and script injection against long-horizon agents performing finance, consulting, and legal tasks in large workspaces. Each task contains 115 to 328 files, together with email, calendar, chat, document, spreadsheet, PDF, and code-execution tools exposed through MCP servers. The benchmark includes six attack categories covering static and dynamic injections, task-specific script replacement, and attacker-provided generic scripts, and evaluates attack success, exposure, and task performance. We evaluate 12 frontier models on 180 task instances per model. Opus 5 and Sonnet 5 have the lowest overall attack success rates (8% and 20%), while task-specific script replacement and attacker-provided generic script attacks remain substantially more effective, averaging 81-84% and 73% attack success, respectively. Gemini 3.8 Flash achieves the highest mean task score (0.877) while still exhibiting a 33% attack success rate. These results show that completing the requested task is not sufficient evidence of safe behavior: an agent can produce a correct deliverable while also performing an unauthorized action in the workspace.

## 8. SwarmRSI: Recursive Self-Improvement through Evolving Scientific Agent Swarm
**Upvotes:** 👍 14 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.20431`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20431) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20431.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20431)

### Abstract:
> Recursive self-improvement (RSI) in scientific research aims to improve agents' research capabilities through their own experimental experience. Scientific agent swarms can combine evidence and implementations from different research directions to guide their agents' subsequent experiments. However, poor experimental results can leave unclear whether to revise a hypothesis or its implementation, while overall program scores can hide components useful to other agents. To address these challenges, we introduce SwarmRSI, a framework that evolves scientific agent swarms for recursive self-improvement. We devise Feedback-Guided Swarm Mutation to refine hypotheses and their implementations using experimental feedback from other agents. We also propose Complementary Swarm Recombination to reuse retained hypotheses and complementary program components across agents, allowing partial results to support new research. Recursive Swarm Evolution integrates both mechanisms into a feedback loop, using new experimental evidence to guide subsequent research. Experiments on OpenProblems, NatureBench and MLE-Bench Lite demonstrate SwarmRSI's state-of-the-art (SOTA) performance. Further evaluations show that accumulated research experience improves performance on held-out tasks. Biologists also reproduce its programs for follow-up biological analyses.

## 9. Speculative Forcing for Efficient Autoregressive Video Generation
**Upvotes:** 👍 11 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.22347`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22347) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22347.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22347)

### Abstract:
> Few-step autoregressive video generators enable streaming generation, yet they run every token through the final denoising step, regardless of how much refinement it needs. We find this uniform computation is often unnecessary: many tokens already closely match their eventual full-step outputs in the initial steps, while others require refinement until the end. We introduce Speculative Forcing, a framework that treats intermediate clean estimates as drafts and predicts when individual tokens can be safely accepted early. Once accepted, a token is frozen as its final output and undergoes no further denoising, while its cached keys and values remain available as context for the remaining tokens. We explore two alternative verifier designs: a training-free one based on similarity to clean content and stability across steps, and a learned one that reads the frozen generator's intermediate features to estimate each token's remaining gap to its full-step output. Across short-video, long-video, and world generation, Speculative Forcing preserves the quality of full-step sampling while substantially reducing computation. On LongLive, it matches the four-step generator with ~1.7 denoising steps per token, running over 1.5x faster at 41.7 and 37.8 fps. On Lingbot-World 2.0, where camera motion continually changes the visible content, it maintains quality with ~2.5 denoising steps per token and runs 1.35x faster at 24.5 fps.

## 10. Intern Lumina U2: A Multi-Codebook Diffusion Large Language Model for Omni-Visual Understanding and Image Generation
**Upvotes:** 👍 11 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.20818`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20818) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20818.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20818)

### Abstract:
> Unified multimodal generation covers discrete, continuous, and hybrid paradigms. Discrete approaches with single-codebook visual tokenizers align naturally with text modeling but have limited representational capacity. Continuous and hybrid approaches retain richer visual representations, but they model text and images in heterogeneous spaces with different training objectives, potentially introducing optimization conflicts between modalities with misaligned representation spaces. These choices expose a central question: how to efficiently model high-capacity visual representations alongside textual content in a shared optimization space within a single backbone. We present Intern Lumina U2, to our knowledge the first diffusion language model to represent multi-codebook visual tokens in a fully discrete space. Each visual position is represented by a group of codes from complementary codebooks, increasing its representational capacity while preserving compatibility with discrete text modeling. At the input, the embeddings are concatenated at each position; at the output, a lightweight multi-codebook autoregressive head predicts the codes within each position sequentially, while the diffusion backbone processes all positions in parallel. Built on the unified 4D representation of AToken, Intern Lumina U2 supports image, video, and 3D inputs within a single backbone and achieves leading performance among unified models in visual understanding, image generation, and image editing. We further build a full-stack training, inference, and evaluation system on both Ascend and NVIDIA platforms to verify the cross-platform effectiveness of our model. We also improve the inference efficiency through a stability-aware sampling strategy, which combines confidence-plateau-guided unmasking approach with drift-triggered cache refreshing mechanisms, accelerating image generation by 4.45x.
