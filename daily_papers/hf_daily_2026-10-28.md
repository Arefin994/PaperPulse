## 1. AV-FABLE: Audio-Visual Manipulation Forensics in Long-Form Videos
**Upvotes:** 👍 39 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.33413`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33413) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33413.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33413)

### Abstract:
> Localized AI manipulations in long-form videos often affect brief events within otherwise authentic content, making video-level classification insufficient for forensic analysis. Existing audio-visual deepfake benchmarks largely focus on short talking-head clips, while long-form temporal forensics remains predominantly visual. We introduce AV-FABLE, a dual-timeline benchmark for sparse manipulations in open-scene long-form audio-visual videos. AV-FABLE contains 16,650 pristine--manipulated twin pairs (998 hours) created with 27 editing configurations, with separate audio and visual timelines that enable modality-specific temporal localization and expose errors hidden by joint-timeline evaluation. On AV-FABLE, zero-shot prompting of audio-visual large language models (AV-LLMs) yields near-chance detection and poor temporal localization. In contrast, supervised readouts of their frozen perception features recover strong forensic signals, suggesting manipulation evidence is encoded in these representations but not effectively exposed through language outputs. Motivated by this observation, we propose NFSR(Native Forensics via Separated Readouts), which reads frozen AV-LLM features with separate audio and visual temporal decoders. NFSR achieves the best audio-visual localization performance in-domain and across all three out-of-distribution settings, including transfer to held-out videos from an external long-video forensic benchmark. AV-FABLE, code, and model weights will be publicly released.

## 2. ChronoReplay: One Answer Interface Is Not Enough for Streaming Spatial Intelligence
**Upvotes:** 👍 34 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.34283`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34283) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34283.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34283)

### Abstract:
> Training a spatial VLM to answer which side an object lies on does not require it to preserve the metric displacement that determines that answer, while supervision for a distance estimate may leave categorical relations largely unconstrained. When post-training emphasizes one readout, improvements can therefore concentrate on that interface while transfer to others remains weak or even degrades; we call this observed specialization interface lock-in. ChronoReplay addresses it by replaying heterogeneous spatial supervision as chronologically ordered episodes, retaining source-native targets, and balancing supervision across families. Under the same observation access, a single ChronoReplay checkpoint improves OVO-S-Bench, OST-Bench, and UCS-Bench together, including a 13.41-point gain on OST-Bench, and achieves the highest three-benchmark mean among the supervision settings compared here. Same-data controls further separate the ingredients of this transfer: answer serialization redistributes gains across OST and UCS, family-balanced learning improves over ordinary joint SFT and natural turn weighting, and chronological replay exceeds a shifted-order control by about 2.41 OST points across two paired seeds. Adaptive frame selection then changes evidence access while keeping the trained model fixed, providing an additional inference-time gain at lower visual cost. Streaming spatial performance depends on what distinctions supervision preserves, how heterogeneous histories are organized during learning, and what causal evidence inference makes available at test time.

## 3. Hy-Nomas: Towards Free-form Interactive World Generation
**Upvotes:** 👍 29 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.36729`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36729) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36729.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36729)

### Abstract:
> Interactive world generation requires models to follow successive commands while preserving relevant history and action consequences. We present Hy-Nomas, a video world model with an 8B diffusion backbone that supports free-form text instructions and continuous camera control across multiple interaction turns. It generates variable-length segments conditioned on current inputs and cached history. Our central design aligns structured video captions with action-aware causal attention: descriptive context remains accessible across segments, while action instructions are restricted to their intended temporal windows. A vision-language Prompt Enhancer (PE) translates user requests and multimodal interaction histories into structured conditions for the next segment. A multi-stage curriculum progressively introduces causal generation, text interaction, camera control, and training on model-generated histories, followed by diffusion distillation. In human evaluation, Hy-Nomas shows superior performance over existing world models. We release model weights, code and benchmarks, together with a lightweight adaptation workflow, to support reproducible research on interactive world generation.

## 4. Rollback: Experience Backtracking in World Models for Policy Improvement
**Upvotes:** 👍 28 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.36068`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36068) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36068.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36068)

### Abstract:
> Action-conditioned world models offer a scalable alternative to costly real-world interaction for improving robot policies. However, existing world-model-based methods largely treat world models as forward-only surrogates of the physical environment, leaving untapped a capability no physical environment affords: rewinding to past decision points to explore counterfactual futures. We introduce Rollback, a policy improvement paradigm that turns world models from passive simulators into counterfactual engines: it rolls back to critical decision points, branches into alternative actions, and contrasts their outcomes to attribute success to individual decisions. This concentrates imagination where it matters most and distills counterfactual outcomes into fine-grained, action-level supervision without a separately learned value model. We build a scalable training system that unifies distributed world-model search with asynchronous real-robot execution, forming a self-reinforcing cycle: real-world experience continually sharpens the world model, and a sharper world model in turn drives further policy improvement. We term the world-model side of this cycle test-time physics grounding, a capability that hand-built physics simulators lack: while the world model serves as the training environment, real transitions from the policy it trains correct its dynamics through a lightweight LoRA pipeline. Experiments on three challenging real-world tasks show that Rollback improves success rates over the strongest baseline by an absolute 10.67%, 11.11%, and 11.85% on block extraction, ball tossing, and tabletop curling, respectively, while achieving more stable improvement throughout training. Although initialized from teleoperated demonstrations far below human level, Rollback surpasses the average success rate of human players on both ball tossing and tabletop curling within 8 hours of training.

## 5. Representation Redistribution in Sparse Autoencoders
**Upvotes:** 👍 25 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.21240`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21240) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21240.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21240)

### Abstract:
> Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) decompose language model activations into sparse features, but prompt transformations designed to preserve the underlying request can redistribute activity across them. A feature change observed on one transformed prompt need not recur, and even a recurring change need not affect a downstream computation. We introduce the redistribution sensitivity score (RSS), which identifies large feature responses that recur in a consistent direction, and evaluate the resulting supports on variants excluded from selection. Across 27 pretrained SAEs spanning eight language models, the tested prompt transformations produce a reproducible redistribution profile on these excluded variants. We then separate recurring representation change from signed readout contribution and decision failure. For a fixed linear readout, an exact decomposition shows how response magnitude, readout coefficients, directional alignment, and the clean margin determine downstream consequences. In two frozen safety readouts, combining RSS support erosion with the full clean margin yields recurrent failure AUROCs of 0.935 and 0.958 using median supports of 20 and 19 features, compared with 0.965 and 0.964 using full erosion. RSS therefore provides a way to identify recurring sparse representation changes and quantify their contribution to a downstream readout without equating representation change with failure. Code is available at https://github.com/OpenSAE/Redistribution-Sensitivity-Score

## 6. OvisOCR3 Technical Report
**Upvotes:** 👍 24 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.33066`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33066) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33066.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33066)

### Abstract:
> We introduce OvisOCR3, a 0.8B model for a broad range of OCR-related tasks. OvisOCR3 unifies document parsing, general OCR, key information extraction (KIE), document figure captioning, and cross-page table reconstruction in a single model. We train the model on real and synthetic data through continued pretraining and use domain-balanced mid-training to balance supervision across tasks with different data volumes and response lengths. Multi-teacher on-policy distillation transfers expertise from five domain-specific 4B teachers into the shared model, followed by mixed-task reinforcement learning with domain-specific rewards. We compare OvisOCR3 with general-purpose vision-language models and specialized OCR models on public and in-house benchmarks covering all five task families. In these comparisons, it achieves an overall score of 96.74 on OmniDocBench v1.6 and the highest overall scores on PureDocBench, Real5-OmniDocBench, and MDPBench. On OCRBench v2, it ranks first, scoring 65.9 and 61.3 on the English and Chinese splits, respectively. It also outperforms the baselines on OCRBench and all four KIE benchmarks. On DocFigCapBench, it achieves the highest localization F1 and a caption score close to that of Qwen3.8-Max. For cross-page tables, it leads in Relation F1 on CrossTabBench and truncated-table TEDS on MPDocBench-Parse. These results demonstrate that a compact 0.8B model can achieve strong performance across diverse OCR-related tasks. OvisOCR3 is available at https://huggingface.co/ATH-MaaS/OvisOCR3.

## 7. LociArt: A Unified Paradigm for Free-Style Local Video Stylization
**Upvotes:** 👍 23 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.36456`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36456) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36456.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36456)

### Abstract:
> Video stylization typically transforms entire scenes, while local artistic editing requires precise region--style association, spatial control, and temporal consistency. We introduce LociArt, a unified paradigm for free-style local video stylization that explicitly specifies where to stylize and what style to apply while preserving non-target content. At its core, GRNStyle builds on Generative Refinement Networks (GRN) and introduces a polar style-aware mask that embeds style identities as equal-norm directions within selected regions. Complementary representation--geometry conditioning combines encoded mask features with raw-mask spatial support, integrating spatiotemporal context with geometric control. To provide paired supervision, we develop an automated pipeline combining global stylization, video object removal, matting, and compositing, and construct the LociArt-100K Dataset with 100K local stylization pairs. We further establish the LociArt Benchmark, comprising 80 source videos and 84 target masks covering 168 foreground/background stylization cases, with a Style Prototype Similarity (SPS) evaluation scheme for regional style quality and complementary measures of non-target preservation and temporal consistency. Experiments show that GRNStyle outperforms existing methods in regional style quality and spatial control while maintaining strong temporal consistency. Qualitative examples further illustrate joint stylization of multiple regions with the same or different learned styles, despite training only on single-region, single-style pairs. Together, these components provide a unified foundation for learning and evaluating controllable local video stylization. Project page: https://higherhu.github.io/lociart/.

## 8. ThinkWorld: World Imagination Reinforces Chain-of-Dynamics Reasoning
**Upvotes:** 👍 19 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.37666`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37666) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37666.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37666)

### Abstract:
> Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have made substantial progress in robotic manipulation, but their generalization remains constrained by the diversity and coverage of action-labeled robot demonstrations. Human videos provide much broader behavioral and environmental experience, while learning from them faces a fundamental trade-off between scalable data and explicit action grounding. We introduce ThinkWorld, a VLA learning framework that grounds action reasoning in world dynamics and strengthens it through reinforcement learning with world imagination. ThinkWorld represents each action thought as a Chain-of-Dynamics (CoD), a sequence of discrete motion tokens that captures the intermediate dynamics connecting the current world to a future state and serves as a common action representation across embodiments. We first initialize CoD with action-labeled demonstrations, then predict the future induced by each CoD and reinforce those whose predictions better match the observed outcome. This enables videos without action annotations to provide direct supervision from observed state transitions, allowing human and robot experience to refine the same reasoning space. Experiments in simulation and on real robots demonstrate effective human-to-robot transfer and strong generalization to unseen tasks and environments.

## 9. On the SPOT: Enhancing Speculative Decoding for Diffusion Language Models via Commit-Order Prediction
**Upvotes:** 👍 18 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.22543`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22543) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22543.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22543)

### Abstract:
> Diffusion language models (DLMs) promise parallel text generation, but in practice face an unresolved speed-quality trade-off. Exact decoding fills in only one masked position per model pass, while faster multi-token decoding schemes are lossy. Speculative decoding is the standard lossless acceleration for autoregressive models. However, it does not transfer directly to DLMs, which must choose both what to generate and where. We show that this order is the missing piece: the model's own predictions from the previous pass already give the right token at over 90% of positions, but not which position comes next. We introduce Speculating Positions Order Trajectory (SPOT), a 0.62M-parameter model that reads only the target's output distributions, predicts this order and proposes a tree of candidate continuations, which the target verifies in a single pass by state equality. Coupled-noise match, which shares Gumbel noise between drafter and target, keeps this verification exact at any temperature. This approach also inspired us to introduce SPOT-Sets for dynamic threshold decoding, where a pass commits several positions: SPOT-Sets drafts whole commit sets together with their tokens. Our comprehensive experiments show that SPOT cuts target passes by up to 6.8times and reaches up to 3.4times the throughput of standard decoding on SDAR-8B, matching or beating the calibrated and training-free drafting baselines in every task and budget at T=0 and T=1. In addition, we show that SPOT's learned drafter transfers across models, demonstrating successful transfer across six different DLMs; with a light calibration, the SPOT-Sets drafter transfers as well. On top of dynamic threshold decoding, SPOT-Sets improves the throughput of SDAR-8B by 59% on average over the threshold decoder alone. Project page: https://eliezer318.github.io/SPOT-dLLM/

## 10. Evolving to Match Capabilities: Improving Harness Self-Evolution via Skill Adaptation
**Upvotes:** 👍 17 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.33024`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33024) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33024.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33024)

### Abstract:
> Harness self-evolution offers a practical route for LLM agents to improve from execution experience without updating model parameters. Its effectiveness depends on whether models can successfully act on the guidance provided by their harnesses. However, we find that harness benefits are not uniform across model capabilities: skills that improve stronger models can provide little benefit or even degrade performance for weaker ones. Across seven models and three benchmarks, we identify 103 task-skill pairs where skills improve GPT-5.6-luna by 31.1 points but degrade Gemma-4-E4B by 8.8 points. In 86% of failed weak-model runs, the violated requirement is already stated in the skill. This motivates skill adaptation: reformulating existing skills to match the executor's capabilities and make their guidance easier to execute. To achieve this, we diagnose failures in tool execution, context management, instruction following, and verification, and distill these diagnoses into a fixed meta-skill that guides adaptation during harness self-evolution. Guided by the meta-skill, a capable external rewriter, Kimi-K3, adapts skills without execution evidence, improving the three weak executors by 5.1-14.1 points over the original skills. We then incorporate this adaptation into a five-round self-evolution loop, where each model revises its own skill from execution trajectories and verifier feedback. Across seven executors, from Gemma-4-E4B to Kimi-K2.5, the meta-skill consistently improves final scores by 2.0-5.1 points over the same loop without the meta-skill. Our findings provide practical design guidance for developing more effective harness self-evolution methods.
