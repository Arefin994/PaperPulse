## 1. Don't Just Trust Your Memory: Evidence-Linked Memory Reduces Memory-Induced Degradation in LLM Agents
**Upvotes:** 👍 67 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.14320`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14320) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14320.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14320)

### Abstract:
> External memory enables LLM agents to improve from experience by distilling trajectories of past tasks into concise lessons for new tasks. However, memory can also lead to memory-induced degradation, where an agent fails on tasks it would solve without memory, often because it follows a lesson that is incorrect, or misapplies or misreads a valid one. This is because lessons are inferred from trajectories labeled only with task success or failure, yet are typically provided without the trajectories they came from, leaving the agent no way to check them. To address this, we introduce WikiTrace, an evidence-linked memory system designed to let agents check each lesson against its original trajectory. Specifically, motivated by the finding that a trajectory often still holds the evidence needed to catch an erroneous lesson, WikiTrace stores each lesson as a concise insight linked to a summary of why its trajectory succeeded or failed and to the raw trajectory itself. Then, on a new task, the agent starts from all insights and, whenever one lacks details needed to act, conflicts with another, or comes from a failed task, descends through its links with terminal tools to fill in missing details or correct the insight before acting. We validate WikiTrace on five agentic benchmarks, showing that it consistently outperforms existing memory approaches and a baseline given the same insights alone, while also reducing memory-induced degradation. Our code is available at https://github.com/yukyeongleee/wikitrace

## 2. Scaling Mathematical Proof Supervision from Structured Textbooks
**Upvotes:** 👍 41 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.40394`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40394) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40394.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40394)

### Abstract:
> Mathematical supervision for language models is typically flattened into isolated problem-solution pairs, discarding the curricular organization and evidential structure of the original sources. We introduce TOME-Math (Textbook-Oriented Mathematical Evidence), a large-scale corpus that converts 2,118 textbooks spanning the core areas of university-level mathematics subjects into approximately 380K structured proof-supervision records while preserving chapter order, proposition-proof links, dependency references, and source provenance. Leveraging these structural signals, we explore curriculum learning across multiple mathematical subjects and characterize scaling patterns with supervision volume and source breadth. At full scale, fine-tuning on TOME-Math raises the unweighted mean score across our held-out evaluations and established public benchmarks by 16.7 percentage points over the base model, while exceeding all three public instruction-data baselines evaluated in aggregate. Our findings highlight structured textbooks as both a scalable source of mathematical supervision and an empirical framework for studying the impact of data organization on mathematical reasoning. We will release the corpus to facilitate further research on structured mathematical supervision.

## 3. One Table, Many Queries: Benchmarking Generalist Tabular Models Across Generation, Imputation, and Prediction
**Upvotes:** 👍 40 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.13001`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13001) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13001.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13001)

### Abstract:
> Generation, imputation, and prediction query different aspects of the same tabular distribution, but are usually evaluated separately. This leaves unclear which capabilities they share, how their training objectives interact, and whether one model can perform all three well. We introduce the One Table Benchmark (), spanning 56 datasets, 46 registered methods, and 20 capability axes under aligned data splits and explicit task contracts. We compare task ranks on aligned support, train five backbones under all seven non-empty objective combinations on five datasets with three seeds, and contrast fixed-model profiles with specialist portfolios. Rank comparisons connect imputation and prediction through numerical recovery and dependence preservation, and link generation to both through predictive utility; distributional fidelity follows different, sometimes opposing, rankings. Objective interventions show the largest pooled gains from including the recipient's own objective. Generation has the broadest coverage of positive pooled auxiliary effects, while prediction shows potential to improve imputation and synthetic-data utility, with responses that vary across backbones. Cross-addition effects reveal that objective benefits need not add and depend on the objectives already present. Fixed-model comparisons expose corresponding utility--fidelity trade-offs: some generalists surpass specialist portfolios on utility, while fidelity continues to favor specialization. Frozen foundation models show strong prediction and numerical point imputation, together with promising synthetic-data utility, highlighting their potential across all three tasks. These findings motivate unified tabular models that share useful conditional information while improving categorical reconstruction and faithful joint sampling.

## 4. SteerDistill: Quantizing LLM Agents via Closed-Loop Teacher Steering
**Upvotes:** 👍 32 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.13410`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13410) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13410.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13410)

### Abstract:
> Low-bit quantization reduces the inference cost of LLM agents but can compromise task success. Examining failed rollouts, we find that quantized agents overlook task requirements or environmental conditions, producing environment transitions that diverge from successful full-precision references. Correcting these behavioral divergences remains difficult: offline data may miss the student's failure contexts, while its own rollouts may provide few correct responses in those contexts. We introduce SteerDistill, a quantization-aware distillation method in which a full-precision teacher guides the student to revise failed responses and iteratively refines its guidance using feedback from student execution. This closed-loop teacher steering produces locally verified behavioral corrections. We internalize these corrections by training the student without guidance to match the steered student's predictions at corrected turns and the full-precision teacher's at other turns, enabling unassisted inference. Across five agentic benchmarks and three models, SteerDistill exceeds the strongest quantized baseline by 1.67--2.18 points in average score under W4A16 and stays within one point of BF16. On τ^2-Bench, it outperforms QeRL by 1.84 percentage points in success rate using 67% fewer training GPU hours and produces shorter successful trajectories than the quantized baselines.

## 5. MACaQ: Fast, Model Agnostic Calibrated Quantization for Low-Precision Weights and Activations
**Upvotes:** 👍 30 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.10649`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10649) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10649.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10649)

### Abstract:
> Weight-activation quantization can reduce the memory and compute cost of neural-network inference, but recent methods often require expensive optimization and architecture-specific propagation of basis transforms across layers. We introduce MACaQ, an MXFP4 W4A4 post-training quantization method that treats every linear layer independently. MACaQ applies a per-channel scale and a block-Hadamard transform online to each layer's activations, while absorbing the corresponding inverse transforms into its weights. Retaining these transforms online avoids cross-layer dependencies and enables a simple streaming implementation of the quantization process, in which only one layer must reside in memory at a time. To improve over round-to-nearest quantization, we introduce Flip-Refinement, a Hessian-aware coordinate-descent procedure that greedily moves individual FP4 codes to adjacent representable values. Despite executing the activation transforms at inference time, MACaQ adds only 2.3% end-to-end overhead over pure MXFP4 on Qwen3-32B in vLLM. MACaQ reaches lower perplexity than DuQuant++ and MR-GPTQ, and already matches their best perplexity on Llama-3-8B in 11 times less quantization time. We further apply MACaQ without architecture-specific modifications to an image diffusion model, illustrating the generality of its layer-local design and show quantization of a 122B parameter model in 64 minutes, using only 32.5GB GPU RAM.

## 6. Incentivizing Self-Learning in Large Language Models via Reinforcement Learning
**Upvotes:** 👍 28 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.39671`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39671) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.39671.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39671)

### Abstract:
> Large language models (LLMs) draw on parametric knowledge and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to solve knowledge-intensive tasks. Yet problem solving in unfamiliar domains calls for tightly coupled knowledge acquisition and reasoning, beyond factual retrieval and aggregation or learning solely from a preassembled context. We study self-learning -- autonomously retrieving, studying, and applying knowledge from long-form reference materials on the fly. To investigate this capability, we formulate a textbook-based mathematical problem-solving task. LLMs must navigate textbook exposition, learn and apply relevant principles, and construct proofs for exercises, with reference solutions withheld. We introduce a BookNavigator interface to support this human-inspired study process and incentivize self-learning through reinforcement learning (RL) with fine-grained rewards. Experiments show that our method improves performance across in-domain and out-of-domain textbooks and subjects, raising Intern-S2-Preview-35B's overall proof accuracy from 51.5% to 79.5% and outperforming RL-trained textbook-free and fixed-retrieval baselines. These gains extend to complex long-context benchmarks involving different task types, with improvements over the base model of 1.7 and 2.3 percentage points on BrowseComp-Plus and CL-bench, respectively. Together, these findings suggest that RL can foster a domain-agnostic self-learning strategy, highlighting the potential of self-learning from long-form corpora to strengthen broader problem-solving capabilities on complex, long-context tasks.

## 7. Embodied First Exam
**Upvotes:** 👍 27 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.12177`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12177) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12177.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12177)

### Abstract:
> Early successes on agentic robotics point to a potential paradigm shift toward using coding agents to control robots. Existing robotics benchmarks are built around visuomotor policy learning, leaving it open whether coding agents can reliably control diverse robots under physical-world constraints. We therefore introduce the Embodied First Exam, a unified, comprehensive, and agent-native evaluation framework where coding agents can choose their own control approach: direct control, code to control, or train a policy to control. We hope it serves as a first exam for coding agents before they enter the physical world, with 1,156 tasks in 35 suites on 47 robots, including 9 new suites that go beyond pick-and-place to spatial puzzles, dexterous manipulation, multi-robot interaction, dynamic whole-body control, and sim-to-sim engineering. Each task can be run in up to three evaluation settings. The Standard Setting mimics the real world: the agent receives only sensor observations and, by default, cannot reset the environment. Its Realtime variant keeps the environment running while the agent reasons. The Privileged Setting gives the agent access to the full simulator state and unlimited resets, allowing it to search for an oracle solution for training purposes. Success rates in this setting serve as an approximate upper bound. On the 456 core tasks, the strongest of eight agents, GPT-6 Astra in Codex, achieves a success rate of 34% in the Standard Setting, leaving much room for improvement. To see where agents fail, we label the stage at which failed attempts go wrong and compare failures across agents, settings, and stage categories.

## 8. FINCOSMOS: An Open-World Evaluation Paradigm for Agents in Finance
**Upvotes:** 👍 25 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.07464`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07464) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07464.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07464)

### Abstract:
> Language agents increasingly work for users on the open Web, and finance is where trusting them is hardest. The data must come from the Web, the strategy lives in the user's head, and correctness depends on time, so an agent can fail silently: a strategy the user never asked for, unverified data, or a backtest that uses the future yields a report as convincing as a correct one. Existing benchmarks close the world somewhere, fixing the task, the data, or the timeline, and thereby remove these failures instead of measuring them. We introduce FINCOSMOS, an open-world evaluation paradigm for agents doing financial work. A simulated user holds a hidden strategy sampled from a compositional grammar and reveals it only when asked. The agent receives no data, so it must acquire data from the Web, build its own backtest under a data cutoff, and deliver verifiable results. Grading needs no reference answer. Programs verify point-in-time correctness and recompute every reported metric, the delivered strategy is checked against the hidden specification, and a run is accepted only when the user is satisfied and none of 15 veto checks fails. Across four coding agents and a model without tools in 150 sessions, no session is accepted: every session that the simulated user signed off fails a program check, and agents raise only 54--64\% of the details they were not told. Human experts attribute 88.33\% of failures to the agents. With FINCOSMOS feedback, an agent with persistent memory learned all ten tasks.

## 9. The Surprising Effectiveness of Deleting Weights in LLM Post-Training
**Upvotes:** 👍 21 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.10866`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10866) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10866.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10866)

### Abstract:
> How little of a pretrained large language model (LLM) has to change for it to acquire new capabilities? We find that zeroing fewer than 0.05% of its weights, with no other modification, matches full fine-tuning (FFT) at scale across three LLM post-training regimes: reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards, supervised fine-tuning, and on-policy distillation. Our method, Deletion Tuning, learns a binary mask over the gate projection of transformer blocks. Two practical advantages follow: the trained mask is hundreds to thousands of times smaller than the corresponding FFT checkpoint, and Deletion Tuning forgets far less, which addresses a central challenge in continual learning. Under reinforcement learning, its prior-task accuracy stays near the base level while FFT, LoRA, and random sparse fine-tuning lose up to 5.4 points. To understand why masking alone suffices, we analyze the geometry of the learned weight changes under reinforcement learning using stable rank, spectrum drift, and principal-weight overlap. Compared with LoRA and random sparse fine-tuning, Deletion Tuning is closest to FFT across the three measures.

## 10. Read Less, Solve More: Token-Efficient Sparse Reading of External Content for AI Agents
**Upvotes:** 👍 19 | **arXiv ID:** `2608.22237`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.22237) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.22237.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.22237)

### Abstract:
> Web-facing AI agents repeatedly access webpages, documents, repositories, and tool responses, yet reading interfaces often expose entire objects when only sparse evidence is needed. This over-reading increases token and latency costs and can dilute relevant evidence. We present SparseRead, a reusable reading control layer that governs content admission without modifying the agent model. Its Read Intent Protocol tracks evolving needs and source coverage; Adaptive Reader Backends select how evidence is located and exposed; and the Budgeted Read Gate bounds cumulative raw exposure per episode with faithful full-view fallback. A unified Reader interface supports interchangeable evidence acquisition across heterogeneous resources. Across six models, including Claude Opus 5, and five scenarios, SparseRead reduces total tokens by up to 92.9% and wall time by up to 89.0%, preserving or improving task quality in 97% settings. On Web deep-search tasks, it reduces tokens by 31.0--43.0% and task completion time by 23.0--37.0%. Ablations validate the three designs, while integration with three agent frameworks demonstrates portability.
