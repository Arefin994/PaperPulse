## 1. SplatAnything: Gaussian Spacing Normalization for Feed-forward 3DGS across Arbitrary Scenes
**Upvotes:** 👍 64 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.08264`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08264) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08264.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08264)

### Abstract:
> We identify a key limitation of existing feed-forward 3DGS methods: Gaussian attributes are typically predicted in a radial-distance-normalized space that canonicalizes a global radial statistic of the reconstructed geometry, while the spacing between neighboring Gaussian centers can still vary substantially across scene scales and camera configurations. This mismatch induces systematic shifts in Gaussian-scale targets, leading to ambiguous supervision. To address this, we propose SplatAnything with Gaussian Spacing Normalization (GSNorm), which canonicalizes a robust scene-level statistic of Gaussian spacing to provide a more consistent spatial reference for Gaussian prediction. To rigorously assess our method, we further establish a systematic benchmark spanning diverse datasets, input sparsity levels, and challenging extrapolated viewpoints. Extensive experiments demonstrate that SplatAnything achieves state-of-the-art performance, with particularly strong gains under substantial variations in scene scale and viewpoint.

## 2. Confucius4-R2T2: A Stable-Prefix Streaming ASR Model for Real Real-Time Transcription
**Upvotes:** 👍 57 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.06043`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06043) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06043.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06043)

### Abstract:
> Streaming speech recognition is increasingly consumed by machines, such as translators and voice agents, that act on each token as it appears. A token that is later revised costs wasted work or a wrong action. In this work, we present Confucius4-R2T2 (short for Real Real-Time Transcription), a stable-prefix streaming ASR model that never modifies its earlier output while maintaining high accuracy and low latency. R2T2 is obtained by post-training Qwen3-ASR-1.7B on 1,500 hours of Chinese and English audio while freezing its pre-trained audio encoder and projector. The adaptation is based on the concept of Longest Stable Prefix (LSP), a new notion that we introduce in this work. We prove that LSP is monotone under the prefix order, consistent with the full-audio output, and maximal among labels that equal a whole earlier output. Learning from LSP labels converts a full-context model into a stable-prefix streaming one without any architectural change. Extensive evaluations show that R2T2 brings streaming recognition close to the base model's full-context quality while delivering stable text with low emission lag. Its gains extend beyond the post-training languages, demonstrating a practical path from full-context ASR to stable streaming. We open-source the model weights and code at https://huggingface.co/netease-youdao/Confucius4-R2T2 and https://github.com/netease-youdao/Confucius4-R2T2.

## 3. P^2-MOPD: Accelerating Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation via Parameter Priors
**Upvotes:** 👍 45 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.03953`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03953) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03953.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.03953)

### Abstract:
> Multi-teacher on-policy distillation consolidates expert capabilities by supervising student-generated trajectories, whereas task arithmetic offers a training-free alternative through linear parameter combinations. We view the linear combination of student and expert parameters as a single differentiable model and propose P^2-MOPD, which jointly optimizes the mixing coefficients and the student weights under the same on-policy distillation objective. Unlike task arithmetic, which stays within the span of the frozen student and expert parameters for any choice of merging coefficients, P^2-MOPD keeps the student trainable, so the experts act as parameter priors without confining it to this span. Across four domains at 1.7B and 4B scales, P^2-MOPD achieves faster and more balanced recovery than MOPD from the same initialization, surpassing its final average recovery in half the training steps. At the end of training, our 1.7B model reaches 94.7% average recovery, versus 80.9% for MOPD.

## 4. PCBRouteAgent: How Close Are LLM Agents to Becoming PCB Routing Engineers?
**Upvotes:** 👍 34 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.08175`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08175) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08175.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08175)

### Abstract:
> Automating printed circuit board (PCB) routing requires coordinating board-level connectivity decisions with geometric execution under strict design rules. Although recent agents can interact with native routing tools, reliable routing on complex boards remains challenging. We present PCBRouteAgent, a multimodal PCB routing agent inspired by how human engineers coordinate global strategy with local execution. A Global Routing Planner combines board images and structured electrical and geometric information to specify connection topology, routing order, corridors, and layer transitions. A Detailed Routing Executor realizes these guides through KiCad's native routing engine and performs bounded local recovery. Unresolved failures are returned as structured feedback, enabling targeted guide revisions while preserving accepted routes. We evaluate PCBRouteAgent on 247 synthetic and real-world boards from four PCBWorld-Bench test splits. It achieves clean-pass rates of 100%, 94.9%, 100%, and 40% on D2, D3-A, D3-B, and D3-C, respectively. On the three real-world subsets, PCBRouteAgent outperforms the best evaluated baseline in clean-pass rate by 13.9, 36, and 10 percentage points, respectively.

## 5. LowRAM: Low-Rank Keys and Amortised Values for Composable KV Cache Compression at Test-Time
**Upvotes:** 👍 30 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.07025`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07025) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07025.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07025)

### Abstract:
> Autoregressive decoding relies on the KV cache, whose memory footprint grows linearly with context length and caps batch size and throughput. Existing post-training methods exploit redundancy across tokens, layers, and feature dimensions, or lower numerical precision. We identify a complementary axis: within each attention head, values are largely predictable from their corresponding keys. We exploit this with value cache amortisation, which fits a lightweight per-head predictor at test time and stores residuals only for the tokens it reconstructs worst, giving 4times near-lossless value compression. The predictor can be initialised by meta-learning for substantially reduced prefill overhead, or fitted from scratch in settings that allow a one-off cost, such as multi-query inference. We pair value amortisation with cross-layer low-rank key compression to produce LowRAM, an asymmetric compression pipeline that stays close to lossless across three model families. By reconstructing only a selected subset of KV pairs at each decoding step, LowRAM can keep decoding overhead minimal and achieve up to 5times higher peak decode throughput at 4times compression. LowRAM further composes with quantisation and eviction, reaching 26.9times compression at 94.5% of baseline performance on long contexts. These results show that treating keys and values as structurally distinct enables efficient, aggressive, and composable KV cache compression.

## 6. LongBanana: An Expert-Verified Benchmark for Long-Context Multi-Reference Image Synthesis
**Upvotes:** 👍 29 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.06856`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06856) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06856.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06856)

### Abstract:
> We introduce LongBanana, to our knowledge the first expert-designed and expert-verified benchmark for long-context multi-reference image synthesis in the age of agent-driven image generation. The task requires retrieving dispersed visual evidence, rejecting distractors, binding entities and relations, and composing one coherent final image from multiple references. LongBanana contains 90 carefully designed tasks organized by a 3-by-3 taxonomy; each task includes about 32 reference images, 20 constraints, and 50 evaluation items, yielding 4,670 expert-specified atomic evaluation items in total. Its graph-grounded evaluation design instantiates each task with reference images and an entity-relation graph, then converts node, edge, and whole-graph obligations into checklist items for Content Fidelity, Relational Binding, and Global Constraint Satisfaction. Reviewed expert labels include a 15% double-labeled subset with 89.4% exact agreement and 0.83 quadratic weighted kappa. We further introduce LongBanana-Gen, an agent-harness framework for long-horizon image generation; experiments show that minimal agent harnesses improve over direct generation, while a ReAct-Plan-Exec-Reflect (RPR) workflow yields harness-dependent gains by strengthening evidence grounding and repair. Together, LongBanana provides an auditable testbed for measuring dense visual evidence use and a shared target for developing stronger agent-driven image-generation systems. Code and data are available at the project page: https://long-banana.com/.

## 7. Toward the Era of Agentic Embodied Intelligence
**Upvotes:** 👍 28 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.09124`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09124) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09124.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.09124)

### Abstract:
> Foundation models increasingly guide robot actions, yet sustained autonomy requires efficient control and failure recovery. We introduce A2-MEAS, an embodied multi-agent framework built around code as a scaling interface. Learned correction codes combine corrective language, spatial evidence, execution history, and action proposals. MetaBrain provides training supervision and runtime guidance. A correction encoder and adapter absorb this knowledge into policy weights during training, while fresh codes adapt behavior with frozen weights at deployment. The action model, A2-WLA, adds motion-aware history and a latent world model to a vision-language-action policy. Surprisingly, this simple design with action pretraining and task specialization reaches 0.5755 Q-score on the 50-task BEHAVIOR 2026 public subset before online correction, 2.21x the 2025 winner's reported 0.2605 across different benchmark editions and training configurations. On all 100 BEHAVIOR 2026 public tasks, A2-MEAS achieves 0.6411 Q-score and 45.40% task success. On RoboTwin 2.0, A2-WLA achieves 93.98% average success, rising to 95.05% with the complete system. Across 12 real-robot tasks in five household settings, A2-WLA achieves 57.92% average success versus 26.67% for pi0.5. In long-horizon pour-over coffee preparation, a history-conditioned adapter raises post-failure completion at two filter-handling steps from 14% to 94% and from 8% to 82%, compared with A2-WLA retries. Our training infrastructure delivers 427.1 samples per second, 4.03x the throughput of a pi0.5 reference implementation on matched hardware. We will release the system, models, training scripts, and infrastructure to support efficient embodied learning.

## 8. LLMs Don't Know What They Know: Benchmarking Temporal Self-Knowledge for Financial Backtesting
**Upvotes:** 👍 27 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.06808`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06808) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06808.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.06808)

### Abstract:
> Financial backtests of large language models (LLMs) are meaningful only if the model did not already know the outcomes it is asked to predict. Practitioners guard against this by trusting a vendor's stated knowledge cutoff, by asking the model what it knows, or by instructing it to answer as of a past date. To verify whether any of these safeguards can be trusted, we introduce FinDendro, a benchmark of 2,226 dated U.S. facts from 1999 to 2026, covering monthly macroeconomic series, month-end stock prices and Federal Reserve decisions. Each fact yields four prompts: a value question, Recall and Predict questions that pair each candidate value with a claim of knowing or guessing without a declared date, and a Predict question with a historical declared date. Controls for persistence and dominant outcomes keep chance accuracy near 0.27. Results show that for most frontier models, self-reports understate knowledge. GPT-5 answers 76% of controlled facts correctly but claims to know 38%, and answers 61% of the facts it says it does not know. Knowledge claims can persist beyond the measured knowledge boundary, by up to seven quarters, and a declared date removes most of the claims of prior knowledge while leaving accuracy unchanged. Backtests inside a model's training window therefore cannot be certified leak-free by self-report, instruction or stated cutoff. These findings motivate behavioral measurement of temporal knowledge boundaries, and FinDendro provides a benchmark and procedure for selecting backtest periods based on demonstrated factual knowledge.

## 9. The Last Mile to the Real World: Agent-Native Primitives as Reusable Building Blocks for Solving Real-World Tasks
**Upvotes:** 👍 27 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.07461`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07461) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07461.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07461)

### Abstract:
> How can an agent handle a real-world task end to end? Frontier models with their own harnesses still fall short on real-world benchmarks: on Terminal-Bench 4.0, GPT-5.6 Sol with Codex resolves 37.3% of the tasks and Claude Opus 5 with Claude Code 51.8%. These tasks fail at the step from reaching a resource to applying it, which we call the last mile to the real world. Existing approaches put the resource within reach but leave the competence to apply it with the caller, so each harness rebuilds that competence. We introduce agent-native primitives, reusable units that equip a resource with a resident LLM that applies it in context and communicates with other primitives, and HEELO (Harness Engineering around Evolving Agent-Native Primitives for the Last Mile to Real-WOrld Tasks), a harness engineering framework that builds each task's harness from a library of such primitives. HEELO composes the primitives a task needs into a task-specific graph, verifies the result against evidence from the environment, revises the primitives a failure implicates, and admits new or improved primitives after validation. We instantiate HEELO with eight primitive families, each built for one kind of task, and evaluate them on more than twenty benchmarks. Dev-Primitives raise Terminal-Bench 4.0 resolution from 37.3% under Codex to 55.5% on the same backbone, Harness Primitives composed per task raise Terminal-Bench 2.0 Pass@1 from 60.0% for the seed harness to 72.2%, Tool Primitives raise ToolBench pass rate from 55.8% to 75.1%, and Navigation Primitives raise GAIA-Text-103 accuracy from 45.6% to 58.3%. On the 46 real-world tasks of our heelo.dev platform, Claude Opus 5.5 with Claude Code and GPT-6.1 Sol with Codex complete 9 each on their own and 39 and 35 when connected to HEELO through our MCP server, with 1.9times and 2.3times less time and 1.55times lower cost per completed task.

## 10. Beneath the Surface: What We Overlook When We Talk About Agent Evolution
**Upvotes:** 👍 23 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.07624`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07624) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07624.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07624)

### Abstract:
> Agent harness evolution enables recursive self-improvement (RSI) without updating model weights. We examine whether higher benchmark scores yield reliable, efficient, and transferable improvement. We evaluate four methods across harnesses, general and coding tasks. Repeated evaluations, controlled agent expansion, component interventions, and admission replays reveal recurring patterns. All sixteen evolved agents gain previously uncovered tasks while losing previously covered ones. In one general-task case, reliability rises by 5.2 points despite losing ten previously covered tasks. Most reliability gains also increase deployment cost. Specific changes usually outperform generic reminders, but their benefits can change when combined. The highest-scoring agent does not yield the largest mean improvement in any of our four pools, and stricter admission can discard useful alternatives. Transfer selectively preserves gains: coverage usually falls on the general-task target across both harness types and paradigms, but rises on the coding target with the outer-layer-only harness. These findings motivate preservation-aware evaluation, tested decision rules, and explicit applicability conditions. As a constructive follow-up, EvoAudit turns these diagnostics into revisions of select, analyze, mutate, and gate, achieving the best performance.
