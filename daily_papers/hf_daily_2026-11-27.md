## 1. Mixture-of-Loops Transformer
**Upvotes:** 👍 66 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.34923`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34923) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34923.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34923)

### Abstract:
> Looped Transformers boost expressivity by reusing shared layers across loops, yet at the cost of efficiency. Pushing their expressivity-efficiency frontier requires rethinking the core mechanism connecting adjacent loops: What should be passed as a Transformer loops? We introduce Mixture-of-Loops Transformer (MoLT), whose Mixture-of-Loops Attention (MoLA) uses keys and values (KV) as memory for selective reuse across depths and loops. Under loop residual, MoLT-R retains the residual stream and reads local KV across loops for expressivity. Under loop KV, MoLT-KV instead restarts from embeddings and reads current-loop local KV with preceding-loop memory KV at earlier positions for efficiency. MoLT-KV eliminates same-position cross-loop dependence and, paired with loop forcing for parallel training, unlocks single-traversal Mixture-of-Loops (MoL) decoding beyond the training loop horizon. To balance efficiency and quality, we introduce U-shaped MoLT (U-MoLT), which separates memory construction from prediction readout and skips unused decoder traversals without changing predictions. In 400B-token pretraining, U-MoLT outperforms OLMo3 across 700M/1.5B dense and 3B MoE models, achieving 1.7x validation cross-entropy (CE) token efficiency at 700M while improving 3B MoE 10-domain perplexity by 0.75 and 10-task accuracy by 2.1 percentage points. In 100B-token continued pretraining of Qwen3.5-0.8B-Base, our U-MoLT adaptation lowers CE on all 11 domains over the matched baseline with 1.4x mean token efficiency. At inference, U-MoLT with MoL-Hybrid-2 matches non-looped OLMo3 latency at 996.6 vs. 1,000.6 ms on S-size (~113M) models. Code is available at https://github.com/hustvl/MoLT

## 2. Stable Gated DeltaNet-2: Understanding and Mitigating Recurrent State Instability
**Upvotes:** 👍 63 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.34051`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34051) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34051.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34051)

### Abstract:
> Linear attention offers an efficient solution for long-sequence modeling via linear complexity and fixed-size recurrent states. Gated DeltaNet-2 (GDN-2) introduces independent channel-wise control over erasure and writing, enhancing state-update expressiveness and in-context retrieval, yet it suffers from training failures during long-context training. We trace these failures to the asymmetry between reading and modification directions during erasure, which yields non-normal erase operators whose directional gains accumulate across tokens, triggering exponential state growth even without new writes. To resolve this, we propose , employing symmetric erasure to ensure non-expansive state propagation and decay-regulated residual writing to preserve writing flexibility. Theoretically, we establish an O(T) upper bound on recurrent state growth while retaining chunkwise parallel computation. Experiments on ~1.6B parameter models show that  achieves stable training alongside improved long-context retrieval. Compared to GDN-2, it boosts the 128K average RULER score by 6.80 points in the recurrent setting and average accuracy across nine downstream tasks by 0.62 points in the hybrid setting.

## 3. OS-Omni: Benchmarking Generalist Computer-Using Agents on Long-Horizon Workflows Across Platforms
**Upvotes:** 👍 57 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.34232`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34232) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34232.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34232)

### Abstract:
> Computer-using agents are increasingly expected to complete realistic user goals rather than isolated GUI actions. However, existing benchmarks typically isolate web, mobile, or desktop environments, leaving it unclear whether a single generalist agent can execute long-horizon workflows that span multiple operating systems and persistent application states. We introduce OS-Omni, a live cross-platform benchmark and execution environment for evaluating computer-using agents across Windows, Ubuntu, macOS, iOS, Android, and Web environments. OS-Omni is released in versioned waves of 300 tasks each; this paper focuses on the first release, v1, which contains 48 tasks per platform and 12 cross-platform workflows that pair a desktop with an Android phone. OS-Omni provides reproducible initialization, platform-specific adapters, trajectory logging, and executable final-state evaluators, and includes controlled service-backed applications for workflows involving account, cart, budget, calendar, message, and other persistent state. Human studies show that OS-Omni tasks require 67.54 interaction steps, substantially exceeding OSWorld references of 15.0 steps. On v1, the strongest evaluated agent, Claude Opus 5.5, reaches 68.5% average success, trailing the 77.3% human reference by 8.8 points; the gap is uneven across platforms, with frontier models exceeding humans on Linux but trailing them by more than 25 points on iOS and macOS. These results indicate that current agents remain far from platform-robust, long-horizon computer use, especially when success requires sustained state tracking and durable final-state changes.

## 4. ProxyGenBench: Benchmarking Proxy-Guided Video Generation
**Upvotes:** 👍 33 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.34457`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34457) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34457.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34457)

### Abstract:
> Recent video generators produce realistic videos, yet controlling how scenes unfold remains challenging. Structural proxies, such as animated 3D blockouts, guide generation by specifying scene layout, motion, camera behavior, and event order. However, these coarse inputs leave details unspecified and may contain artifacts that should not be reproduced. Successful proxy-guided generation must therefore Preserve intended structure, Complete missing content and correct proxy artifacts, and Bind text, image, and audio conditions to the right entities and events. We introduce ProxyGenBench to study these interacting requirements, with 312 evaluation cases from 60 scenes across three scene tasks and controlled variations in proxy fidelity, text detail, and multimodal inputs. We evaluate five video generators using verified, case-specific checklists. Experiments show that proxies improve spatiotemporal control, but greater proxy detail can coincide with poorer completion, while strong structural adherence does not ensure correct binding.

## 5. Let Vision-Language Models Generate Images through Their Representations
**Upvotes:** 👍 27 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.31237`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31237) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31237.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31237)

### Abstract:
> Recent unified multimodal models aim to combine visual understanding and generation within a single model, yet effectively integrating the two capabilities remains challenging. Leveraging pretrained vision-language models offers a way to build on already learned multimodal knowledge, but making this knowledge effectively accessible to visual generation remains nontrivial. We find that a central challenge lies in the heterogeneous visual representations used for understanding and generation. To address this challenge, we introduce Integral, which converts a pretrained vision-language model into a unified multimodal model by constructing a shared visual representation space across the two capabilities. Integral implements this design through two complementary components. Abstraction Fusion constructs shared visual tokens by aggregating hierarchical representations from the frozen vision encoder, while Block-wise MoT introduces a lightweight generative stream that interacts with the vision-language model only at block boundaries. Our approach effectively preserves the pretrained understanding capability while extending it to strong visual generation. Controlled comparisons further demonstrate that sharing visual representations between understanding and generation substantially improves generative learning compared with using a separate visual representation space, despite comparable reconstruction fidelity. These results suggest that representations learned by pretrained vision-language models already provide an effective foundation for visual generation.

## 6. DocSEAM: Bidirectional Cross-Page Encoding for Multi-Page Document Understanding
**Upvotes:** 👍 27 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.34420`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34420) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34420.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34420)

### Abstract:
> Multi-page document vision-language models encode each page independently, creating a feature-level discontinuity at page boundaries: visual tokens are formed without access to content on adjacent pages, and the language model must reason over these page representations formed in isolation. We introduce DocSEAM (Document Selective Enrichment via Anchored Masking), a lightweight module inserted between a pretrained vision encoder and the language model that injects the inductive bias of local cross-page context into visual representations. For each page, DocSEAM forms a local triplet with its two neighbors, applies center-anchored masked self-attention with directional salience gates to fuse information from neighbor pages, and returns only the enriched center-page tokens, leaving the visual token count unchanged. After a supervised cross-page warm-up and multi-page fine-tuning with DocSEAM frozen, our 9B-parameter model achieves state-of-the-art results among the evaluated open-weight models of up to 10B parameters on five multi-page document VQA benchmarks. We further show that under controlled re-pagination, DocSEAM reduces sensitivity to boundary placement and improves cross-page span localization

## 7. Entangle: Scaling Human-Video In-Context World-Action Models across Unseen Tasks and Contexts
**Upvotes:** 👍 26 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.28732`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28732) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28732.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28732)

### Abstract:
> In-context learning (ICL) enables foundation models to perform unseen tasks from context without parameter updates. In robotic manipulation, a human video provides the context that specifies how a task should be performed. However, scaling human-video ICL on world-action models (WAMs) faces two challenges: task-level human-robot pairs are not temporally aligned with robot actions, and standard action losses allow policies to rely on initial-scene or task-identity shortcuts rather than tracking the context. We present Entangle, an in-context world-action model that maps a single human video and current robot observations to future visual states and executable actions. To scale temporally aligned training data, we convert recorded robot videos into matched human contexts via video-to-video generation, supplemented by real paired recordings. For model training, we combine variable-length video windows with an intra-task contrastive objective that distinguishes the paired action trajectory from other executions of the same task, preventing task-identity shortcuts. Without task-specific fine-tuning, Entangle improves steadily as aligned training data scales, reaching an average score of 83.5/100 across six unseen tasks on an Astribot S1 robot, and achieves a 69.5% average success rate on seven unseen RoboTwin tasks, outperforming Zero-WAM by 22.6 percentage points. Under Context-OOD, although trained only on scene-aligned pairs, Entangle transfers context-specified behaviors across rearranged layouts and substituted tools. Project page at https://entangle-robotics.github.io/

## 8. DRBench Pro: A Knowledge-Centric Benchmark for Professional Deep Research Agents
**Upvotes:** 👍 22 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.34852`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34852) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34852.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34852)

### Abstract:
> Deep research agents gather information from multiple sources and synthesize it into reports on complex, open-ended questions. Existing benchmarks assess report quality along several dimensions, but lack a dedicated knowledge-centric evaluation of which research directions a report covers and how deeply it explores them. We introduce DRBench Pro, a benchmark that makes structured knowledge coverage the primary evaluation target. Our automated pipeline builds local knowledge trees from Wikipedia and uses sampled subgraphs to generate research queries and evidence-grounded rubrics. Each rubric corresponds to a relation in the tree, preserving the structure of the knowledge being evaluated. Agents receive only the research query. This structure allows us to measure breadth across major research directions and depth along related knowledge chains, with their geometric mean giving overall coverage. The benchmark includes 144 cases across 24 domains and 44,102 rubrics. Evaluating 16 deep research agents reveals different strengths in breadth and depth, with the best-performing agent reaching 57.04% overall coverage. Rubric judgments agree closely with human annotations, and agent rankings remain stable across the tested metric configurations.

## 9. StateWeaver: Multi-Agent Coordination of Story-State Transitions for Long-Horizon Video Generation
**Upvotes:** 👍 21 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.34290`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34290) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34290.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.34290)

### Abstract:
> Modern video foundation models generate high-quality short clips, but preserving long-video consistency and correctly depicting event-driven state changes remain challenging. Existing agentic workflows face these challenges because continual planning and repair accumulate context, while reference updates remain limited. We introduce StateWeaver, which combines Active States and Hierarchical Collaboration to connect review-driven visual reference updates with cross-shot state inheritance. Active States translate state discrepancies identified during review into concrete visual asset requirements that guide reference construction, validation, and regeneration, preserving identity and already-correct content while repairing target attributes. Confirmed generation results are linked to the corresponding asset states, story time, and reference versions for verification and reuse by subsequent shots. Hierarchical Collaboration isolates execution contexts by task, clip, and repair round, and uses hierarchical memory to organize this process across multiple sets. Across multiple benchmarks, StateWeaver outperforms existing methods and produces long videos with sustained cross-shot consistency under predefined and autonomously planned storyboards.

## 10. Playfulness as Feedback for Agentic Game Development
**Upvotes:** 👍 20 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.33457`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33457) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33457.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.33457)

### Abstract:
> Coding agents can generate runnable games from natural-language descriptions. However, a runnable game does not guarantee an excellent gameplay experience for players. In our view, a playful game should provide 1) reliable core functionality, 2) appealing visual design and assets, and 3) rich gameplay that supports exploration and progression. How agents can improve these aspects of playfulness remains underexplored. In our work, we propose Play2Evolve, a recursive game development framework with three stages: Initialization, Refinement, and Evolution. Using playfulness as feedback, agents playtest the game and diagnose issues in its functionality, visual presentation, and gameplay. Their feedback guides bug fixes and asset improvements. It also helps agents propose new gameplay and aesthetic goals for continued game evolution. To enrich game materials, we combine a video generation model with code to create appealing static and dynamic assets and integrate them into interactive gameplay. Finally, Play2Evolve achieves state-of-the-art performance among the evaluated methods, scoring 60.05 on GameCraft-Bench compared with 50.81 after Game Initialization and 52.46 for Play2Code. Human overall-quality ratings reach 3.67, compared with 2.38 and 2.43, respectively. Our code will be released at https://github.com/showlab/Play2Evolve.
