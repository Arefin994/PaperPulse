## 1. The Trinity:Capability-Driven Recursive Self-Improvement for Multimodal Agents
**Upvotes:** 👍 534 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.36077`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36077) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36077.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36077)

### Abstract:
> Multimodal intelligence can be viewed as human-guided co-evolution between worlds and agents through interaction. However, scaling this process faces two bottlenecks: constructing heterogeneous, verifiable environments is costly, and manual Harness iteration cannot keep pace with growing task diversity and complexity. A key insight is that tasks with different modalities and surface forms can share the same underlying capability requirements. We therefore introduce The Trinity, a recursive self-improvement framework coupling world generation with Harness evolution through three roles: Creator, Explorer, and Evolver. Drawing on verified execution trajectories, Evolver shifts the basis of task organization from surface categories to shared requirements, reorganizing the original task tree into a hierarchical capability tree. We introduce Capability-Driven Real2Sim2Real: for heterogeneous tasks at the same node, Creator abstracts their common requirements into simplified, verifiable simulated worlds for capability learning, while evaluation on original tasks measures transfer. Along this capability tree, Explorer progressively evolves reusable capability modules through selective inheritance, forming a Harness tree whose validated updates accumulate across iterations within a fixed topology. The resulting Harnesses specialize in different capabilities, motivating a Capability-Aware Harness Router that uses public task information to select compatible module configurations from the tree. Our evolved Harnesses improve mean performance by 34 points across 28 training benchmarks and 26 points across 18 unseen benchmarks, including two real-world embodied tasks.

## 2. Multiscale Policy Optimization for Long-Horizon LLM Agents
**Upvotes:** 👍 375 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.35013`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35013) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35013.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35013)

### Abstract:
> Long-horizon LLM agents interact with their environments through complete assistant turns while generating each action token by token. We observe that critic values vary primarily across turns, motivating optimization anchored at turns with finer credit allocation among tokens. We propose Multiscale Policy Optimization (MPO), combining Multiscale Advantage Estimation (MAE) and turn-level importance sampling to align credit assignment and policy updates at the turn scale. Extensive experiments across search, tool-use, and automation tasks with 4B and 30B-A3B models demonstrate consistent gains over strong RL baselines.

## 3. ChronoReplay: One Answer Interface Is Not Enough for Streaming Spatial Intelligence
**Upvotes:** 👍 347 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.34283`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34283) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34283.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34283)

### Abstract:
> Training a spatial VLM to answer which side an object lies on does not require it to preserve the metric displacement that determines that answer, while supervision for a distance estimate may leave categorical relations largely unconstrained. When post-training emphasizes one readout, improvements can therefore concentrate on that interface while transfer to others remains weak or even degrades; we call this observed specialization interface lock-in. ChronoReplay addresses it by replaying heterogeneous spatial supervision as chronologically ordered episodes, retaining source-native targets, and balancing supervision across families. Under the same observation access, a single ChronoReplay checkpoint improves OVO-S-Bench, OST-Bench, and UCS-Bench together, including a 13.41-point gain on OST-Bench, and achieves the highest three-benchmark mean among the supervision settings compared here. Same-data controls further separate the ingredients of this transfer: answer serialization redistributes gains across OST and UCS, family-balanced learning improves over ordinary joint SFT and natural turn weighting, and chronological replay exceeds a shifted-order control by about 2.41 OST points across two paired seeds. Adaptive frame selection then changes evidence access while keeping the trained model fixed, providing an additional inference-time gain at lower visual cost. Streaming spatial performance depends on what distinctions supervision preserves, how heterogeneous histories are organized during learning, and what causal evidence inference makes available at test time.

## 4. DTCA: Denoising Token Credit Assignment for Reinforcement Learning in Diffusion Language Models
**Upvotes:** 👍 255 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.35201`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35201) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35201.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35201)

### Abstract:
> Diffusion language models (DLMs) generate text through iterative denoising, enabling bidirectional context modeling and parallel token refinement. Recent studies have applied reinforcement learning (RL) to DLMs and improved their reasoning performance. However, the iterative denoising process makes credit assignment more challenging, as final outcome rewards provide limited information about how different tokens contribute to the final output. In this paper, we propose Denoising Token Credit Assignment (DTCA), a framework for token-level credit assignment in reinforcement learning for diffusion language models. DTCA estimates token contributions by measuring the improvement in prediction confidence of remaining masked positions after token commitment and groups these signals according to part-of-speech (POS) categories. These contribution weights are used to select informative denoising timesteps and assign token-level learning signals during RL training. DTCA further calibrates these learning signals according to token-level entropy changes induced by policy updates. Experiments on LLaDA-8B-Instruct show that DTCA consistently improves mathematical reasoning performance across different generation budgets, with gains of up to 9.0 points on MATH500 and 5.3 points on GSM8K over the base model.

## 5. VibeVideoBench: Evaluating Multimodal Agents on Code-Driven Video Generation
**Upvotes:** 👍 173 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.35864`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.35864) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.35864.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.35864)

### Abstract:
> Diffusion video models produce striking clips, but they cannot reliably render an exact formula, a correct chart, a rule-consistent game or narration synchronized to on-screen events. A different route is emerging: multimodal agents that write code to render every frame and synthesize every sound. We introduce VibeVideoBench, a benchmark for evaluating agents on code-driven video generation. Its 239 self-contained tasks in English and Chinese span 12 categories, from data stories and music-driven pieces to classroom lessons and paper explainers; each asks an agent, running in its native harness inside a sandbox with no image or video generation model, to deliver a finished MP4 with sound within one hour. Evaluation is hierarchical and evidence-based: program checks of the delivery spec, about 13k task-specific rubric checks traced to the requirements they enforce, and five quality dimensions anchored per task, scored by three VLM judge panels. Across nine frontier agent systems and 2,151 scored rollouts, GPT-6 Astra and Claude Opus 5.5 rank first and second with both the judges and human annotators, and the strongest system satisfies every core requirement on 76.3% of tasks. Delivery is a second axis: counting missing videos as zero changes the ranking, and three quarters of the lost videos are final renders that started too late, ran too slowly or never started, a failure of how agents plan their hour. Agents build their own rendering pipelines and inspect their own frames, yet how much they look does not predict quality. The judges' ranking of the systems closely matches blind human preferences.

## 6. Memory Tax: Dissecting the Net Value of Agent Memory
**Upvotes:** 👍 146 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.34073`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.34073) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.34073.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.34073)

### Abstract:
> Memory is increasingly used in long-horizon agents, yet the net value of building memory over direct retrieval from raw history remains largely unclear. Through a large-scale evaluation across multiple benchmarks and model backbones, we find many dedicated memory systems underperform naive RAG in many cases, indicating a negative net value. Taking RAG as a reference, we dissect the net value of memory into the gains and losses over direct retrieval, termed memory benefit and memory tax. These gains and losses arise because memory process changes how the raw information is organized and accessed. We view such changes as the double-edged deviations from the raw history. They are originally designed and introduced to benefit the task, for example by reorganizing facts or distilling preferences, but some may also remove or obscure evidence, giving rise to memory tax. Since memory benefit reflects the expected gains of its design, we then focus more on the overlooked memory tax, study how it arises from the system and evaluation side, and generalize the concept to more application scenarios of memory. We hope the notion of memory tax can provide a new lens and shift more attention from standalone performance to the net value of memory, informing more reliable memory designs and evaluations. We will open-source the code and results to the community. Project Page: https://hexuan21.github.io/MemoryTax/

## 7. PDRVLA: A Vision-Language-Action Model with Physical Dynamics Representations
**Upvotes:** 👍 143 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.37381`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37381) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37381.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37381)

### Abstract:
> Vision--Language--Action (VLA) models generate robot actions from visual observations and language instructions, but their understanding of physical dynamics remains limited. Video world models can capture scene dynamics, yet generating future videos introduces high latency into the control loop. We introduce PDRVLA, whose lightweight Physical Dynamics Module extracts physical tokens from vision--language features to condition action generation without online video generation. The module learns through direct prediction of future RGB, depth, and optical-flow features, with auxiliary supervision through a frozen pretrained video world model. Training relies entirely on publicly available data, expanded through human-to-robot synthesis, subtask annotation, and embodied chain-of-thought (ECoT) annotation. Across four simulation benchmarks, PDRVLA achieves success rates of 99.3% on LIBERO, 89.9% on LIBERO-Plus, 93.6% on RoboTwin~2.0, and 81.5% on SimplerEnv. Ablations show improved future prediction with world-model supervision and evaluate how subtask conditioning, future object-pose supervision, and ECoT training affect action success.

## 8. Can Test-Time Memory Evolution Outperform Test-Time Training for Scientific Discovery?
**Upvotes:** 👍 117 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.32046`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32046) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32046.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32046)

### Abstract:
> Recent test-time training (TTT) frameworks have enabled open-weight large language models (LLMs) to outperform inference-only frameworks powered by frontier models on scientific discovery tasks. However, TTT introduces additional computational cost for online weight updates during the discovery process, making computational efficiency an important consideration when scaling to larger models. This motivates us to explore an efficient test-time adaptation method for scientific discovery. We introduce Test-Time Memory Evolution (TTME), a plug-and-play test-time adaptation method, as an alternative to TTT. TTME continually updates the external memory by distilling the experience to guide subsequent discovery without updating model weights. To have a clean comparison, we evaluate TTME and TTT under the same discovery framework, replacing only the test-time adaptation method. Our evaluation shows that TTME outperforms TTT on all five mathematical tasks, while reducing computational cost by 82.8-84.6% in FLOPs and 47.7-58.4% in runtime. These results establish TTME as an effective and computationally efficient alternative to TTT in the evaluated discovery settings.

## 9. Hy-Nomas: Towards Free-form Interactive World Generation
**Upvotes:** 👍 107 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.36729`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36729) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36729.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.36729)

### Abstract:
> Interactive world generation requires models to follow successive commands while preserving relevant history and action consequences. We present Hy-Nomas, a video world model with an 8B diffusion backbone that supports free-form text instructions and continuous camera control across multiple interaction turns. It generates variable-length segments conditioned on current inputs and cached history. Our central design aligns structured video captions with action-aware causal attention: descriptive context remains accessible across segments, while action instructions are restricted to their intended temporal windows. A vision-language Prompt Enhancer (PE) translates user requests and multimodal interaction histories into structured conditions for the next segment. A multi-stage curriculum progressively introduces causal generation, text interaction, camera control, and training on model-generated histories, followed by diffusion distillation. In human evaluation, Hy-Nomas shows superior performance over existing world models. We release model weights, code and benchmarks, together with a lightweight adaptation workflow, to support reproducible research on interactive world generation.

## 10. Can Discrete Tokens Serve as Semantically Lossless Representations of Visual Signals?
**Upvotes:** 👍 89 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.37662`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37662) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37662.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.37662)

### Abstract:
> Discrete visual tokens represent vision in a discrete form analogous to language, offering a native interface for multimodal modeling under a shared next-token prediction objective. However, quantization error is widely assumed to cause unavoidable semantic loss, fundamentally limiting the semantic fidelity of discrete visual tokens and positioning continuous visual representations as an intrinsic upper bound. Using multimodal understanding as a proxy for semantic fidelity, we revisit this assumption and find quantization error unreliable, as discrete representations with substantially larger errors can achieve better understanding. Instead, the effective bit budget of discrete indices emerges as a key determinant of downstream performance. However, existing quantizers eventually saturate as additional codebooks contribute progressively less semantic information. As one approach to alleviating this saturation, we introduce Factorized Vector Quantization, which quantizes complementary, fixed-dimensional views of the visual feature with symmetric parallel codebooks. It continues to improve at larger bit budgets, ultimately matching continuous visual representations in multimodal understanding. Trained on industrial-scale multimodal data, our model achieves state-of-the-art performance among discrete-representation models, outperforming UniAR and Emu3-Chat by 22.8% and 59.2%, respectively, on a nine-metric average. To our knowledge, it is also the first discrete-representation model competitive with strong continuous-representation models such as Qwen3-VL, including on fine-grained OCR tasks. We further identify the key principles and a practical recipe enabling this competitiveness. These results and insights support the potential of discrete visual representations to underpin large-scale native multimodal foundation models. Code and models are available at https://github.com/hustvl/FVQ
