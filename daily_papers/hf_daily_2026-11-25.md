## 1. Nemotron-Labs-VisualCreation: Pixel as Code for Agentic Visual Creation
**Upvotes:** 👍 39 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.31358`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31358) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31358.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31358)

### Abstract:
> Visual creation demands expressive imagery together with precise control over text, layout, and structure. We introduce Nemotron-Labs-VisualCreation, an agentic framework that connects these capabilities through Pixel as Code. Raster images are represented by learned discrete visual tokens, while structured artifacts use explicit programs. A shared generation-and-rendering interface connects these representations to visual feedback across images, vector graphics, webpages, and documents. A Nemotron-native harness coordinates planning and raster candidate selection, with targeted repair for editable structured and hybrid artifacts. To support this interface, we develop Nemotron-Labs-Image-Tokenizer, the autoregressive raster generator Nemotron-Labs-Image-30B-A3B, and the structured-code model family Nemotron-Labs-VisionCode. On approximately 178K images spanning six visual domains, our tokenizer improves reconstruction PSNR from 26.08 to 29.57 dB over Emu3.5 IBQ. It also provides 7.13x faster encoding and 10.93x faster decoding at batch size one on A100. In standalone evaluation, one Nemotron-Labs-VisionCode variant surpasses Kimi K3.1 Pro on the aggregate across Chart2Code, MMSVG, Design2Code, and Image2Struct. At the system level, the Nemotron-native configuration raises GenEval2 Soft-TIFA GM from 31.71 to 52.56 using the same raster generator. With our best setting, Nemotron-Labs-VisualCreation achieves performance competitive with GPT Image 2 and Nano Banana Pro across most benchmarks with available comparisons.

## 2. Rewirable Circuits in Transformers with Loom Attention
**Upvotes:** 👍 27 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.30634`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30634) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30634.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30634)

### Abstract:
> We propose loom attention, which allows a Transformer to dynamically rewire its circuits at every token by interweaving a head's vertical attention over the outputs of earlier heads (to obtain its query, key and value) with its horizontal attention over tokens. We instantiate vertical attention efficiently with normalized and gated linear attention over a per-token matrix memory, which stores the compressed head outputs and serves as a second residual stream. This yields LoomFormer, a dual-stream architecture that splits control and content between the vector and matrix streams. Across model sizes and training token budgets in language modeling and downstream tasks, LoomFormer matches Transformers trained with over 3x compute and recent residual-stream baselines (HC/mHC, MUDDFormer, AttnRes) trained with ~1.7-2.8x. For example, trained on the same 300B tokens with only 2% extra FLOPs, LoomPythia-2.8B achieves ~1.6x the average downstream gain of mHC-Pythia-2.8B over Pythia-2.8B, and matches (0-shot) or surpasses (5-shot) Pythia-12B. Code is available at https://github.com/Caiyun-AI/LoomFormer

## 3. ACAP: Asynchronous Coding Agents for Efficient Robot Control
**Upvotes:** 👍 26 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.31205`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31205) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31205.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31205)

### Abstract:
> Coding agents can construct and revise robot behavior during execution, but using one agent for the whole robot can delay otherwise independent work. We introduce Asynchronous Coding Agents as Policy (ACAP), an asynchronous framework that organizes one robot as a team of persistent agents. Each member reasons and acts for a configurable control group, such as an arm with its gripper or the torso and chassis, enabling asynchronous reasoning and execution. However, different robot components often have physical dependencies that require coordination. To address this challenge, ACAP introduces a hyper-agent that assigns tasks, coordinates dependencies, and dynamically allocates control between the lead and member agents. This allows independent work to progress concurrently while supporting tightly coupled manipulation when necessary. Across 72 real-world rollouts covering 25 task/platform settings, ACAP reduces task-balanced time cost by 28.2% compared with a single-agent Direct Policy, with improvements across Fully Parallel, Parallel-Sequential, and Collaborative tasks. Across all eight Collaborative settings, ACAP also achieves lower mean time cost than Fixed Delegation, where only member agents can control their assigned groups. An additional 90-rollout study shows lower mean 30-minute-capped time cost than Direct on all six tasks. We further demonstrate mobile manipulation using the same member interface for chassis and torso control. We present the framework and a real-world rollout dataset containing observations, generated programs, actions, and feedback to support research on how coding agents reason and act in the physical world.

## 4. S-Mind: Internalizing Multi-View Geometry for Spatial Intelligence
**Upvotes:** 👍 23 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.28649`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28649) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28649.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28649)

### Abstract:
> Spatial intelligence, the ability to reason about 3D space from visual observations, becomes essential as multimodal large language models (MLLMs) move into the physical world. Beyond perceiving each view, it rests on two cornerstones of human cognition: fusing multiple views into one coherent 3D scene and mentally transforming that scene to new viewpoints as reasoning unfolds. Yet our spatial probes reveal that MLLMs largely lack both capabilities, even after spatial fine-tuning. We therefore present S-Mind, which internalizes multi-view geometry within the MLLM through a compact set of spatial tokens. It supervises these tokens to hold the fused 3D scene and re-emits them to re-express this scene from every viewpoint the reasoning adopts. Across four spatial benchmarks, S-Mind improves its Qwen3.5 backbones by up to 22.26 points and surpasses frontier general models such as GPT-6-Astra (+0.45), Kimi-K3.1-Pro (+1.97), and GLM-5.3-Flash (+7.80) in average accuracy. Moreover, the same probes confirm that it acquires both capabilities, reaching fusion and transformation accuracies of 65.11% and 82.17%, against at most 16.02% and 37.28% for the baselines. At inference, S-Mind also runs about 100x faster than a tool-based agent on the same backbone. Instilled through these spatial tokens during training, spatial capability becomes intrinsic to the backbone and persists even when the tokens are withheld, offering a promising path toward spatial intelligence in MLLMs.

## 5. Interpretable Prototype-Conditioned Adaptation for Weather Forecasting
**Upvotes:** 👍 21 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.25848`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25848) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25848.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.25848)

### Abstract:
> Data-driven weather forecasting requires distinguishing local states and their temporal evolution. However, learned patterns remain implicit in neural forecasters, making their influence difficult to interpret. Failing to distinguish local evolution can lead to similar forecasts for locations that develop differently, increasing prediction error. This motivates explicit references for distinguishing and interpreting local patterns. Prototypes provide explicit pattern references, but sample- or task-level conditioning leaves open how to adapt to distinct locations and variable groups. To deal with this problem, we design ProtoCast, which uses group-specific prototype banks to adapt local features while accounting for cross-variable dependencies. Specifically, (1) we combine each group's historical features and temporal change with forecast context from cross-variable attention for evolution-aware prototype matching. (2) Sparse mixtures of prototype-specific low-rank operators tailor corrections to each group and location. A gated attention block then exchanges the corrected features across groups before prediction. Each group's correction decomposes into explicit prototype contributions before this fusion. (3) We jointly train the forecasting network and operators from scratch and refit each group's prototypes before every training epoch. Slot tracking preserves prototype-operator associations, making each prototype's contribution to local corrections more interpretable. Experiments on GlobalForecast and TyphoonTracking show improved forecasting accuracy, while prototype deletion links local feature contributions to changes in prediction error. This work advances the development of more accurate and interpretable data-driven weather forecasting models. Project page: https://zhixuanli.github.io/projects/li2026protocast

## 6. Antom 3-in-1 Transformer: A Payment Foundation Model
**Upvotes:** 👍 21 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.25403`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25403) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25403.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.25403)

### Abstract:
> We report the development of Antom 3-in-1 Transformer, a payment foundation model that integrates sequence, tabular, and graph representations for payment risk detection. The model contains approximately 7B parameters, with approximately 1.7B activated per task route. Antom 3-in-1 Transformer is pretrained to learn reusable behavioral and relational representations, primarily on Ant International's internal payment data. In a cross-business transfer experiment, pretraining improves the target business metric by 34.5% over the same architecture trained from scratch. With only 25% of the training labels, the pretrained model outperforms the full-label scratch baseline in Average Precision (AP) and Recall@0.5% on different merchant domains. In a four-month online experiment on Ant International's billion-scale payment platform, the model reduced the overall loss rate by 37.88% relative to XGB-Online.

## 7. KMed-31B Technical Report
**Upvotes:** 👍 17 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.28719`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28719) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28719.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28719)

### Abstract:
> We present KMed-31B, a medical large language model post-trained from an open-weight model for on-premises deployment with limited hardware. Post-training a model for diverse medical tasks remains difficult, because improving one kind of task can lower performance on another. We attribute this to differences between tasks in expected output and available feedback. Medical question answering and diagnosis prediction have reference answers, whereas clinical inquiry has no single correct response and is judged with rubrics. We therefore divide medical training tasks into four categories by these two properties, train one specialist per category with a reward that its feedback supports, and integrate the specialists into a single student with Multi-teacher On-policy Delta Distillation (MOPD^2). For clinical inquiry, we build Synthetic Health Dialogue, a simulated corpus of consultations with lay users and clinicians whose patient cases span common clinical presentations in primary care. Relative to its base model, KMed-31B raises HealthBench Hard from 22.95 to 41.87 and HealthBench Professional from 37.92 to 50.20 in non-thinking mode, and from 23.80 to 51.48 and from 41.97 to 59.18 in thinking mode, while its scores on four multiple-choice medical benchmarks are maintained or slightly improved. KMed-31B has the highest unadjusted HealthBench Hard score among the compared models, including proprietary ones, and scores higher on HealthBench Professional than every open-weight model we compare, including models with more than ten times as many total parameters. Applied to another base model, Qwen3.8-27B, the same training recipe also raises both HealthBench scores. We describe the training recipe, data construction, and settings in detail so that institutions requiring on-premises deployment can develop medical models from open-weight foundations.

## 8. Innata-0: A Native Embodied World System with Model-Agent Co-Design
**Upvotes:** 👍 17 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.30254`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30254) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30254.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30254)

### Abstract:
> The physical world is open-ended, dynamic, and partially observable. While foundation models have advanced embodied intelligence, standalone models struggle with long-horizon execution, and simply integrating models with agents may lead to inconsistencies between planning and physical control. To support reliable and adaptive behaviors in the physical world, we introduce Innata-0, a native embodied world system. Innata-0 jointly designs an embodied world-action model and an agent to combine their complementary strengths. The model, Innata-0-WAM, adopts a tri-tower Mixture-of-Transformers architecture that integrates understanding, prediction, and action generation. The agent, Innata-0-Agent, coordinates long-horizon execution through memory, verification, and recovery. A co-design protocol is further proposed to align model training with agent execution and leverage validated experiences to support subsequent adaptation and model improvement. Extensive evaluations demonstrate strong embodied capabilities across understanding, world prediction, and robotic manipulation. Innata-0 represents our first step toward physical intelligence, and we see it as the beginning of a much longer journey.

## 9. PartAgent: Agentic 3D Part Segmentation via Expert Integration and Closed-Loop Refinement
**Upvotes:** 👍 16 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.32554`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32554) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32554.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.32554)

### Abstract:
> Segmenting a 3D object into semantically meaningful parts requires joint reasoning about geometric structure and semantic context. Pretrained 3D experts provide spatially grounded region proposals, while 2D experts provide semantic cues for identifying object parts. The challenge is to integrate these complementary capabilities into a framework that can both produce meaningful part segmentations and progressively refine them. We present PartAgent, a training-free agentic framework for 3D part segmentation built upon two complementary modules: (i) Structural-Semantic Expert Integration combines regions predicted by the 3D expert with multiview semantic evidence from the 2D expert through image-to-surface correspondences, constructing compositional part partitions at multiple granularities. (ii) Observation-Driven Closed-Loop Partition Refinement observes the current partition and its supporting evidence to select targeted inspection, evidence repair, composition, or reselection actions. It verifies proposed changes and uses the updated state and action outcomes to guide subsequent decisions. On PartObjaverse-Tiny-WT, PartAgent achieves 68.41 mIoU, outperforming the strongest competing method by 8.53 points. Across 3DCoMPaT200, PartNeXt, PartNetE, and PartObjaverse-Tiny, PartAgent achieves the highest mIoU on each dataset, averaging 69.36 across the four datasets, 7.28 points above the highest mean among competing methods.

## 10. Beam Model Technical Report
**Upvotes:** 👍 14 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.26358`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26358) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26358.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26358)

### Abstract:
> Beam is a sparse Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) model with 501.3B total parameters and 23.4B active parameters, specifically built and optimized for coding, reasoning, and agentic workloads. Beam delivers frontier level reasoning, while requiring 3-4x less inference compute than many models of its class. Pretrained on 23.8T diverse, curated, high-quality tokens, Beam features a 1M-token context window achieved through a novel two-stage midtraining phase, built on verifiable, correctness-anchored data. Post-training leveraged a large-scale, fully asynchronous reinforcement learning (RL) campaign across 10.5K GB300 GPUs, generating over 100M rollouts over 4 weeks of training. We believe this is one of the largest scale RL runs conducted by any open lab to date. Across our evaluation suite, capabilities continued to improve as we increased RL compute, with no sign of a plateau. Supported by deep hardware-model co-design, FP8/NVFP4 quantization, and a 1.93B-parameter speculative decoding drafter (DFlash 2), Beam maximizes serving throughput with efficient, flexible deployment. Averaged across conversation turns and harm categories, Beam refuses or pivots on 88.9% of harmful requests while complying with 81.5% of harmless requests, achieving the highest refusal/pivot rate on harmful requests among open-weight models on the Pareto frontier.
