## 1. cmHC: Balancing Stream Mixing in Hyper-Connections via Geometric Contraction
**Upvotes:** 👍 94 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.27193`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27193) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27193.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27193)

### Abstract:
> Manifold-constrained Hyper-Connections (mHC) extends residual connections to multiple streams and stabilizes residual propagation by constraining stream-mixing residual matrices to the Birkhoff polytope, the set of doubly stochastic matrices. In this work, we investigate how to effectively mix residual streams within the Birkhoff polytope. We reveal a mixing trade-off: when under-mixed residual matrices stay close to the vertices of the Birkhoff polytope, hyper-connections will degenerate into a set of nearly independent streams; when over-mixed residual matrices are concentrated around the uniform barycenter of the polytope, streams will become less distinguishable from each other. We theoretically validate our findings by bounding the cumulative residual mixing, stream difference and gradient difference of mixing coefficients across layers. Motivated by these findings, we propose Contraction for Manifold-Constrained Hyper-Connections (cmHC), an exact doubly stochastic method which geometrically contracts the Birkhoff polytope toward its interior. This contraction mitigates under-mixing by forcing the residual matrices to explore deeper into the polytope, with a provable stream mixing bound and no new parameterization. Moreover, we apply a moderate contraction at the shallow layers to prevent over-mixing, enabling stable gradient propagation on extremely large-scale model pretraining. Extensive experiments across dense and mixture-of-experts models at various parameter scales demonstrate the effectiveness and scalability of cmHC. We also develop fused operators for cmHC on Ascend NPUs, reducing hyper-connection execution latency by up to 44.8%.

## 2. Motion Mamba 2.0: Autoregressive Diffusion Mamba for Long-Horizon Humanoid Control
**Upvotes:** 👍 77 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.29897`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29897) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29897.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29897)

### Abstract:
> Long-horizon, real-time controllable motion generation is essential for interactive animation and humanoid robotics, where an agent must respond to changing instructions and spatial goals while moving. Existing autoregressive diffusion methods generate motion incrementally, but bounded attention caches limit access to distant history, while repeated denoising introduces substantial inference overhead. We present Motion Mamba 2.0, a Mamba-based root-body diffusion architecture that integrates local attention, resumable recurrent states, and persistent content memory (PCM) for long-horizon controllable motion generation. PCM transforms compressed motion history from Mamba recurrent states, together with state changes and text-conditioned features, into dynamically updated content slots accessible to local attention. To enable efficient inference, we further develop an execution scheme tailored to this architecture, combining CUDA Graph replay, fused kernels, and execution-flow simplification while preserving the canonical history update. Evaluations on retargeted HiPHI demonstrate accurate terminal control over rollouts of up to 39.52 s, while short-horizon comparisons on BONES Seed and retargeted HumanML3D show that the model also retains strong spatial control and text-motion matching. On an RTX 4090, the optimized execution reduces median complete-window latency from 1685.35 to 276.14 ms, achieving a median paired speedup of 6.138times while preserving identical outputs and committed states in the tested rollouts. Moreover, we conduct extensive real-world evaluation on the Unitree G1, demonstrating sequential text-guided action composition, expressive motion, and interactive locomotion through keyboard and mouse control.

## 3. Non-penetrating World Models
**Upvotes:** 👍 55 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.27963`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27963) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27963.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27963)

### Abstract:
> We study a new problem of preventing object penetration in interactive video world models. To understand how existing models handle this, we introduce NoPeBench, a benchmark that evaluates how world models respond to physical obstacles. Our evaluation reveals that none of the open-source models can reliably prevent such failures. Surprisingly, however, these models already encode the information needed to predict such violations. We find that the hidden states of diffusion transformers (DiTs) can predict whether a candidate action will penetrate an obstacle. Building on this, we introduce NoPeWorld, a lightweight framework that detects and prevents penetration. Specifically, we train a small prediction head on top of a frozen DiT's hidden states to read out if a candidate action is safe or not. Unsafe actions are then replaced with safe ones that best preserve the original intent. Despite its simplicity, NoPeWorld substantially reduces the physical violation rate, averaged over six video world models, from 74% to 16%. Remarkably, our method shows zero-shot generalization to open-domain imagery, third-person viewpoints, and moving obstacles. We publicly release our code, the NoPeBench benchmark, and model weights. Project Page: https://nopeworld.github.io .

## 4. Learning Video Foresight Reasoning from Unlabeled Videos via On-Policy Self-Distillation
**Upvotes:** 👍 42 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.26954`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26954) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26954.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26954)

### Abstract:
> Video foresight reasoning requires vision-language models to anticipate future events or next actions from currently observed videos. Existing methods fit future captions, rationales, or full visual states, but face two limitations. First, they rely on annotations or external large models for supervision, limiting scalability. We address this with an on-policy self-distillation framework that turns past-future video pairs into a natural self-supervised signal without extra annotations or LLM supervision. Second, they treat the whole future clip as equally valuable, although most of it is irrelevant context information. We address this by defining a foresight score, from which we obtain a contrastive privilege advantage that drives the model to learn the most valuable future information. Since the score is intractable, we optimize a tractable lower-bound surrogate that interpolates this advantage, computed between the true and a random reference future, with a teacher-student distillation advantage; together, they amplify decision-relevant content information and suppress irrelevant context information. We call the resulting framework Future-OPSD. On open-ended prediction and closed-ended planning, Future-OPSD improves base models by up to 14.2 and 15.9 points without compromising general video understanding. Code will be available at https://jingchensun.github.io/future-opsd/

## 5. Clover-35B-A3B-Preview Technical Report
**Upvotes:** 👍 40 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.24636`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24636) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24636.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24636)

### Abstract:
> We introduce Clover-35B-A3B-Preview (Clover), a mixture-of-experts diffusion language model with 35 billion total parameters and 3 billion activated parameters, supporting thinking-mode generation. Clover adapts Gated Delta Networks to block diffusion by combining recurrent memory of previously generated context with bidirectional softmax attention inside the current block. Historical memory readout competes with current-block tokens in a shared softmax, with learnable block-size calibration and a separate, zero-initialized right-context convolution branch. Clover leads the seven evaluated agent metrics, including 73.6 on SWE-bench Verified and 51.7 on Terminal-Bench 2.1. On internal agent workloads, it reaches a reported generation speed of 680 tokens/s using one NVIDIA GB300 GPU at BF16 precision and batch size one. We organize the evaluation around model quality, drafting and verification costs, and deployment under constrained memory. We also describe a decision-model variant that generates answers to related questions in one denoising pass. On an internal set of 500 groups, joint prediction raises question accuracy from 90.37% to 93.88% and all-correct group accuracy from 65.4% to 77.0%.

## 6. 3AGameFactory-Motion: Agentic Rigging and Reliable Complex Motion Generation for Diverse 3D Assets
**Upvotes:** 👍 37 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.30013`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30013) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30013.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30013)

### Abstract:
> Animating arbitrary 3D assets from instructions requires both structural inference and motion control, yet existing motion benchmarks mostly cover conventional human or animal skeletons with simple instructions. We introduce DivDynBench, which evaluates rigging and motion generation across six morphology categories, with instructions requiring multi-phase part coordination and explicit timing, angle, repetition, and trajectory constraints. With large language models, we systematically compare three common paradigms: specialized motion generation, video generation followed by motion recovery, and direct agent-driven generation. We analyze their control capabilities, cross-morphology adaptability, and generation costs. Guided by this comparison, we propose an Agentic Motion Framework in which an agent writes compact parametric skeleton and motion programs, and a deterministic executor performs rigging, kinematic solving, and geometric correction. Compact parameter control reduces generation overhead and improves rigging reliability and motion-control accuracy, providing practical guidance for deploying complex 3D motion generation.

## 7. AutoPILOT: A Visual Agent Harness for Real-Time Gameplay
**Upvotes:** 👍 26 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.28202`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28202) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.28202.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.28202)

### Abstract:
> Multimodal foundation models can play video games well when the game waits for them, but often fail when it keeps running. We find that much of this gap lies in the harness, which decides what the model sees, what it remembers, and how its outputs become actions over time. Existing harnesses such as VISTA improve the first two, yet turn each model call into at most one action, so the agent acts only as often as its model thinks. We introduce AutoPILOT, which changes the third: in each model call, the model can send a primitive action, start an open-loop macro, or write a closed-loop controller that keeps playing from the screen without further model calls until it hands control back. To evaluate agents in real-time games, we build AFK-Bench, 120 diverse browser games grouped by how they respond to inaction and playable in real time or with the clock paused. With GPT-6.1 Sol under the same budgets, AutoPILOT reaches 68.6% real-time success against 38.3% for VISTA, and on GameWorld it keeps 80.0% in real time while VISTA drops from 84.1% to 42.9%. Its gains hold across models, and it reaches VISTA's final success rate at a fifth of its API cost. Our results suggest that how a harness turns model outputs into actions over time is a central yet underexplored part of agent design, and we hope AutoPILOT and AFK-Bench will serve as a foundation for agents in a world that does not wait.

## 8. Plan4D: Generative Plannable 4D Worlds
**Upvotes:** 👍 26 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.26516`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26516) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26516.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.26516)

### Abstract:
> Most world models for future prediction prioritize aestetic quality of generated videos, but offer only weak physical grounding and limited geometric consistency over time, due to the lack of explicit 4D modeling. In this paper, we introduce plan4d, a possible next milestone towards building a generative plannable 4D world model that predicts future RGB videos together with dense 4D geometry, including depth, cameras, point clouds, and scene flow from an observed scene context and text-specified actions. Since training data with paired actions and dense 4D annotations is inherently scarce, plan4d leverages the complementary priors of foundational video generators and 3D reconstructors. Specifically, we propose a model-stitching architecture that couples Depth Anything 3 with LongCat-Video and augments it with an auxiliary DPT head for scene-flow prediction. The resulting model allows us to plan and generate complex, long-horizon, and fine-grained actions in a dynamic 3D scenes, while substantially improving both visual fidelity and geometric accuracy of the generated future videos. Extensive experiments on both synthetic and real-world datasets demonstrate that plan4d achieves state-of-the-art performance in generative 4D modeling of environmental reactions. Project Page: https://fogradio.github.io/plan4d-project

## 9. MemOnce: A Scalable Memory Architecture for Large Language Models
**Upvotes:** 👍 23 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.27973`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27973) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27973.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.27973)

### Abstract:
> Deploying large language models (LLMs) in long-horizon and continual interactions requires maintaining reusable memory states for future inference, posing challenges for existing architectures. Standard Transformers accumulate layer-wise, token-level KV caches, incurring linear GPU memory growth and quadratic computational complexity as the context expands. In contrast, linear-time architectures compress history into fixed-size states, but struggle to retain fine-grained details. We introduce MemOnce, a foundation architecture that learns scalable memory states for language models. MemOnce is based on two core designs: (a) Memory encoder-decoder: it caches only a small subset of final-layer hidden states as compact memory representations and dynamically decodes them back into KV caches for attention layers; (b) Memory sparse attention: for each continual input, MemOnce adaptively retrieves relevant memory representations, with the sparse indices computed only once across layers. Coupling these two designs enables efficient on-the-fly memory loading and KV cache decoding, drastically reducing the GPU memory footprint and achieving linear-time complexity. We conduct extensive experiments to show that MemOnce outperforms Transformers and other architectures across varying model sizes, context lengths, and training paradigms (pretraining and post-training adaptation). In particular, adapting Qwen3.5-4B with MemOnce reduces memory state cache by 1313times and prefill latency by 6.9times at 1M context, and improves throughput by 3.5times and overall memory performance by +5.77%. Finally, MemOnce generalizes zero-shot to downstream representation tasks, achieving competitive results against specialized models.

## 10. A Glimpse of Training VLMs as Foundation Models for Robotic Manipulation
**Upvotes:** 👍 22 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.29338`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.29338) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.29338.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.29338)

### Abstract:
> Recently, GPT-6 Astra demonstrates strong generalization in robot control, yet limited disclosure of its training leaves open how such capabilities can be reproduced. We introduce nanoAstra, an approach to training vision-language models (VLMs) for generalizable manipulation that combines semantic action modeling with capability-preserving supervised fine-tuning (CP-SFT). nanoAstra represents robot behavior as semantic steps composed of reusable, parameterized action primitives, guided by explicit progress descriptions. Meanwhile, CP-SFT uses an explicit robot-specific system prompt to constrain manipulation outputs to a structured action space, aiming to focus adaptation on robot-specific contexts and promote specialization while minimizing interference with broader pretrained capabilities. With this formulation, we train nanoAstra on more than 5,000 hours of robot data together with general VQA data. On RoboTwin 2.0's randomized benchmark, nanoAstra-9B achieves a 17.8% zero-shot success rate, substantially outperforming advanced VLA and WAM baselines as well as similarly sized VLMs. With inference faster than the corresponding motion execution on a single H20 GPU, our model supports action-chunk-like streaming execution that overlaps inference with motion, reducing the robot's idle time from 35.0% to 6.9%.
