## 1. TeleChat to Xing4: Advancing Frontier Agentic Models on Domestic Chips and Frameworks
**Upvotes:** 👍 151 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.22115`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22115) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22115.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22115)

### Abstract:
> We present Xing 4, a series of large language models that extends the TeleChat lineage through coordinated advances in model architecture and training infrastructure on Ascend. This report describes the design principles and systems engineering behind the series, spanning architecture selection, large-scale pretraining, and reasoning and agentic post-training. We study how sparse activation, attention design, model depth and width, and speculative decoding balance model capacity with computation, memory access, and execution overhead. Our pretraining infrastructure builds on MindSpore HyperParallel, combining topology-aware hybrid sharding, efficient distributed optimizer execution, and coordinated management of long-sequence workloads and activation memory. CPU affinity and core isolation further reduce host scheduling interference. Together, these mechanisms support the pretraining of a 285B-parameter model on 8,192 Ascend 910C cards, providing a concrete setting for the architectural and systems studies. For post-training, we introduce TeleGym, a unified agent execution platform that connects execution, evaluation, and trajectory construction to policy optimization. Disaggregated asynchronous execution, bounded oversampling, and partial rollout accommodate heterogeneous task durations while preserving trajectory continuity across policy updates. Together, these advances establish an Ascend-oriented training foundation for the continued development of Xing 4 across model scales and increasingly demanding reasoning and agent workloads.

## 2. AscendGYM: A Cost-Aware Kernel Optimization Benchmark for Real-World Workloads on Ascend NPUs
**Upvotes:** 👍 91 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.17621`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17621) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17621.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17621)

### Abstract:
> Kernel optimization is essential for efficient large language model (LLM) training and inference on Ascend NPUs. Existing Ascend kernel benchmarks primarily focus on kernel generation, with less emphasis on the ability of agents to optimize existing implementations under input configurations drawn from real workloads. To address this requirement, we introduce AscendGYM, a benchmark whose initial suite comprises 48 kernels across three difficulty levels. Each kernel is paired with test cases whose data types and tensor shapes are collected from real-world training and inference tasks. Consequently, performance improvements on AscendGYM map directly to the corresponding workloads. In terms of usability, two evaluation modes are supported in AscendGYM: model evaluation and model-harness evaluation. The kernel optimization capabilities of both trained models and custom agents are allowed to be measured. It provides a complete execution environment that closely reproduces how Ascend kernels are invoked in practical pipelines. For model evaluation, it additionally includes a built-in harness equipped with search orchestration and experience accumulation. Finally, we propose a new metric, the Speedup-Token Score (STS), which quantifies performance gains relative to token cost. By jointly considering speedup and cost, STS enables the assessment of practical kernel optimization capability of a model or harness under an acceptable budget. AscendGYM is available at https://github.com/SLAI-AITP/AscendGYM.

## 3. Beyond Answering: Learning to Speak First and Act in Streaming Video
**Upvotes:** 👍 35 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.17048`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17048) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17048.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17048)

### Abstract:
> Most AI assistants respond only when prompted, remaining silent even when events in the video stream offer meaningful opportunities for conversation. To move beyond purely user-driven interaction, we present AURA 2.0, a streaming video large language model that introduces three core upgrades to AURA. The key advance is Model-Initiated Conversation (MIC), which enables the model to identify topics worth discussing in the evolving visual stream and initiate conversations at appropriate moments without a user query. Stream-Aware Tool Calling (SATC) lets the model act on what it sees by invoking external tools using the current visual and conversational context and incorporating returned information into the ongoing interaction. Interactive Visual Grounding (IVG) supports visual grounding in both user inputs and model outputs. The model can identify objects marked by users on video frames and produce frame-aligned bounding boxes to link its responses to specific objects and regions. To develop these capabilities, we construct a dedicated streaming interaction dataset and adopt a three-stage training scheme to integrate the new behaviors with AURA's existing streaming interaction abilities. We further incorporate Long-Horizon Interaction Memory (LHIM) to maintain contextual continuity over extended video streams. Evaluations on public streaming benchmarks show that AURA 2.0 achieves leading performance among open-source streaming models, while targeted evaluations further validate its new interaction capabilities.

## 4. FastFrames: Video Generation at Interactive Latency
**Upvotes:** 👍 24 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.23226`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23226) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23226.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23226)

### Abstract:
> Autoregressive video diffusion models generate frames based on new observations and inputs, making them a promising medium for interactive visual experiences. However, existing models are orders of magnitude too slow for fluid interaction. This paper introduces FastFrames, a family of fast autoregressive video diffusion models that achieves interactive latency without compromising quality. We profile the inference pipeline of state-of-the-art models and find that operations often treated as fixed implementation details, such as VAE decoding and KV cache updates, in fact account for over half of end-to-end inference latency. We challenge the assumption that these components are indispensable and replace them with lightweight alternatives. Our 1.3B variant runs at 36 ms per frame, or 28 responses per second (RPS), on a single B200 GPU while matching the generation quality of a state-of-the-art model that is nearly 8x slower. Our 14B variant runs at 45 ms per frame, or 22 RPS, on an 8x B200 GPU node and yields significantly better visual quality and realism. We show that FastFrames enables real-time interaction in applications such as camera-conditioned and physics-conditioned video generation.

## 5. TCP4D: A Unified Tool-Center-Point Tracking and Generation Model for Robot Manipulation
**Upvotes:** 👍 23 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.23143`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23143) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23143.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23143)

### Abstract:
> Robot manipulation videos provide a rich visual experience, yet recovering robot action and gripper-state annotations from RGB observations still remains challenging. Using the embodiment-agnostic tool-center-point (TCP) pose as the action space, we present TCP4D, a unified model in which a shared 4D scene representation both tracks observed TCP motion and generates future TCP trajectories. Given a monocular video and initial 2D TCP queries, the tracker directly recovers metric TCP poses in SE(3) and gripper states alongside scene geometry and camera parameters. The generator samples local features from the same 4D representation along the recovered TCP tracks, then combines them with robot observations and language instructions to predict future TCP states. Our new cross-embodiment augmentation pipeline converts existing RGB videos into demonstrations for alternative embodiments by retargeting the recovered motion and synthesizing the corresponding observations, without recorded source-robot states. Experiments show that the tracker achieves absolute trajectory errors of 7.60 and 16.07 mm on DROID and RoboLab and adapts to a new embodiment with only ten demonstrations. The generator achieves 69.19% mean success on RoboTwin and outperforms well-established policy models on two real-world tasks, while joint training on source and augmented demonstrations improves target-robot success by 41.5% over augmented-only training.

## 6. Heave-Ho: Decentralized Long-Horizon Multi-Humanoid Collaboration
**Upvotes:** 👍 21 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.22275`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22275) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.22275.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.22275)

### Abstract:
> Collaboration promises to extend humanoid capabilities beyond what individual robots can achieve. However, realizing this potential requires coordinated whole-body interactions, coherent long-horizon planning, and precise synchronization across robots. We introduce Heave-Ho, a decentralized framework for long-horizon collaborative multi-humanoid loco-manipulation. At the low level, we develop a multi-agent post-training framework that adapts a humanoid foundation tracking model to physically coupled interactions between humanoids, enabling coordinated decentralized control from local observations alone. At the high level, we train a vision-language planner and design a speech-based leader-follower coordination mechanism to synchronize decentralized execution without network communication. Trained with two identical humanoids, our system scales to teams of up to ten robots and generalizes to heterogeneous teams without retraining. To benchmark these capabilities, we introduce HH-Bench, comprising 12 tasks involving teams of 2 to 10 humanoids, including heterogeneous teams. Systematic evaluations show that Heave-Ho consistently outperforms baseline methods, while real-world experiments demonstrate robust decentralized collaboration across challenging tasks.

## 7. PROLA: Principal-Orthogonal Low-rank Adaptation for Predictive Spatiotemporal Weather Downscaling
**Upvotes:** 👍 20 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.11318`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11318) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11318.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.11318)

### Abstract:
> High-resolution weather prediction is important for resolving local atmospheric patterns missed by coarse global forecasts. Large pretrained weather backbones have improved global forecasting, but adapting them to produce future high-resolution fields from recent coarse states remains challenging and costly. We formulate this setting as Predictive Spatiotemporal Weather Downscaling (PSWD), a task that jointly refines spatial and temporal resolution while predicting future high-resolution trajectories. To this end, we propose a framework that decomposes prediction into spatial and temporal stages using a shared pretrained backbone, fixed numerical scaffolds, and lightweight residual heads. For backbone adaptation, we introduce PROLA (PRincipal-Orthogonal Low-rank Adaptation), which allocates a fixed low-rank budget between pretrained weights' principal directions and their orthogonal complement. PROLA further rescales rank-1 optimizer updates using gradient signals, improving adaptation without increasing trainable rank. Experiments in the PSWD setting show that PROLA outperforms representative low-rank adaptation baselines. Further experiments across diverse datasets and tasks support the broader applicability of PROLA, showing that its benefits extend beyond the primary weather downscaling setting.

## 8. VideoNexus: Video Perception Beyond the Reasoning Context
**Upvotes:** 👍 20 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.21392`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21392) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21392.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.21392)

### Abstract:
> Understanding long videos requires locating evidence, resolving visual details, and integrating observations into an answer. Even when relevant evidence is present, performing these operations within long, dense visual sequences burdens both perception and reasoning. Video agents enable selective inspection, but returning frames to the planner still accumulates visual information in its reasoning context. Delegating perception reduces this burden, yet sparse sampling can miss decisive details, and per-frame descriptions can preserve context growth. We introduce VideoNexus, a training-free framework that separates detailed video perception from evidence-driven planning and reasoning. The planner controls where to inspect, what to resolve, and how densely to sample. Perception sub-agents process native audio-video in independent contexts and return compact, temporally grounded evidence for iterative search and verification. Using Gemini 3.8 Flash for both planning and perception, VideoNexus achieves 88.6% accuracy on full LVBench, exceeding same-backbone static ingestion and built-in agentic processing by 3.6 and 1.1 points, respectively, at 44% of the latter's measured API cost. A paired LVBench subset ablation shows lower planner input and higher accuracy. With Kimi K3 as the shared backbone, VideoNexus improves over the official Video-GAIA ReAct harness by 8.5 points in our evaluation. These results support compact evidence exchange for combining detailed observation with efficient planning and reasoning.

## 9. Game2Player: Scaling Gameplay Experience for Visual Agents through Environment-Player Co-Development
**Upvotes:** 👍 16 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.24154`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24154) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24154.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.24154)

### Abstract:
> Games are a rich testbed for visual agents, demanding perception, long-horizon planning, and precise control. However, collecting human gameplay is costly, and learning from gameplay videos requires inferring missing actions. Existing datasets also cover a limited range of games, often without explicit tasks or verifiable outcomes. To address these gaps, we introduce Game2Player, a framework that co-develops game environments and players to collect diverse and verifiable gameplay experience at scale. In the Game2Player loop, a Builder agent adapts existing web games, exposes their internal state, and defines tasks with state-based progress measures and success checks. A Coding Player and a VLM Player then attempt the tasks and report problems to the Builder for repair, so that play serves as both environment validation and training data collection. With Game2Player, we build 568 verified web games with 3,436 tasks, collect 15.7K trajectories (307 hours of gameplay), and hold out 50 games as the benchmark PlayerBench. To evaluate the collected experience as training data, we train VLM agents with progress-weighted SFT, assigning greater weight to steps that advance task progress. Game2Player-9B improves progress from 14.3% to 31.2% on PlayerBench and from 30.0% to 53.1% on the external GameWorld benchmark. More broadly, our work suggests a path for visual agents to scale their gameplay experience by building, verifying, and learning from existing game worlds.

## 10. π^4: A Foundation Model for 4D Scene Geometry and Motion
**Upvotes:** 👍 13 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.23144`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23144) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.23144.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.23144)

### Abstract:
> π^4 is a unified foundation model for 4D scene perception. From diverse, uncalibrated RGB observations in the wild---a single image, an unordered collection, or a video sequence---it recovers dense geometry, camera poses, and metric scale, together with 2D/3D point tracks and moving-object masks for multi-image inputs. It couples a geometry branch with a geometry-conditioned motion branch, which predicts image-space correspondences that are lifted into 3D using reconstructed target-view geometry. Across diverse benchmarks, π^4 achieves the best overall average rank in geometry evaluation, alongside competitive performance in single- and multiview 2D/3D point tracking and moving-object segmentation. With a shared backbone, the full geometry-and-motion system contains approximately 0.5B parameters---less than half the size of most baselines. It produces joint reconstructions and tracks in one forward pass, including large-scale scenes with thousands of input images despite training with contexts of at most 64 views. We further present the data and training recipe underlying these capabilities, together with empirical studies of key design choices. Code and models are available at https://github.com/yyfz/Pi4.
