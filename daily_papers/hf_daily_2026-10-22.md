## 1. CoEvo-RSI: When Harnesses Evolve, Which Models Should Follow?
**Upvotes:** 👍 76 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.25078`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25078) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25078.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25078)

### Abstract:
> Model--harness co-evolution has emerged as a prominent paradigm for recursive self-improving (RSI) agents, jointly evolving their execution scaffolds and intrinsic capabilities. We study the persistent yet often overlooked brain--body mismatch problem in this paradigm, where an evolving harness (body) demands behaviors its model (brain) has not learned or restricts capabilities it already possesses. We introduce CoEvo-RSI, a RSI framework that advances model--harness co-evolution through agent-driven, bidirectional historical inheritance. Drawing on a shared evolutionary archive and execution feedback, a meta-agent coordinates harness development from historical designs with model checkpoint selection or merging and subsequent adaptation under the selected harness, aligning inherited capabilities with evolving execution requirements to reduce brain--body mismatch. Using only supervised fine-tuning (SFT) on its own rejection-sampled trajectories, CoEvo-RSI makes Qwen3.5-9B competitive with larger models across diverse vertical applications, including multi-agent failure attribution, role-aware text-to-SQL, and smart-home instruction following. It surpasses DeepSeek-V4-Pro on TraceElephant (+14.16 agent accuracy) and GLM-5.3 on HomeBench VM (+4.90 success rate), underscoring the need for brain--body alignment and the potential of bidirectional historical inheritance to advance agent co-evolution.

## 2. Joints, Camera, Action! Co-Generating 3D Motion and Video for Arbitrary Rigs
**Upvotes:** 👍 22 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.28632`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28632) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28632.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28632)

### Abstract:
> Video generation models can now depict remarkably realistic motion. Yet it is unclear how to use this motion beyond pixels: to animate a specific 3D asset and replay the animation in its 3D scene. For instance, a video model can show a monkey jumping over a crossbar, but it cannot return that jump as replayable 3D motion placed in the 3D scene. Here we present a model that directly co-generates the video and 3D motion for any supplied rig, along with a camera that registers this motion to the scene. Prior video-motion models either handle only human skeletons, often adding billions of parameters, or triangulate 3D motion from generated 2D pose maps. We instead keep 3D motion in a separate model that supports arbitrary skeletons, and connect it to a pretrained video model through a small set of cross-attention layers. This separation lets the motion model first learn how skeletons move from motion-only data, which is easier to obtain and augment than paired video. Additionally, our model learns to locate each joint in the video frames, which lets us recover the camera and place the 3D motion in the scene. To train across many skeletons, we use a generative video editor to place animated 3D assets in scenes suited to their actions, curating the edits so their known motion remains valid. Together with human and robot video, this yields nearly 200K training pairs. Our evaluations demonstrate that our model animates diverse rigs from text more faithfully than video-first pipelines, and its motion responds to the surrounding scene. It also turns video into rig motion, rig motion into video, and enables keyframe-based control of the 3D motion. Videos and additional results are available on our project page: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/par/projects/jca/.

## 3. SpecKCast: Spectral Kalman Forecasting for Diffusion Acceleration
**Upvotes:** 👍 22 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.26837`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.26837) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.26837.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26837)

### Abstract:
> Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) achieve high-fidelity image and video generation, but they suffer from substantial inference costs due to repeated transformer evaluations across many denoising steps. Feature caching reduces this cost by reusing features across denoising steps. Recent forecasting methods extend this idea by extrapolating features to skipped steps instead of reusing them. However, existing forecasting methods extrapolate the features they predict uniformly, even though the spatial-frequency components of these features change most at different stages of denoising. They also overlook that denoising progresses nonuniformly, so equal step intervals can correspond to very different amounts of change. These mismatches amplify forecast errors as more steps are skipped, limiting the achievable acceleration. We propose SpecKCast, a training-free method that forecasts the model output in the frequency domain. SpecKCast applies a 2D discrete cosine transform (DCT) to the output and tracks each coefficient with a lightweight Kalman filter along a band-specific time coordinate derived from the noise schedule. The filter tracks each coefficient's value and rate of change, and the transformer is evaluated only when the filter's forecast uncertainty exceeds a threshold. Experiments on FLUX.1-dev, Qwen-Image, Wan2.1-14B, and HunyuanVideo show that SpecKCast achieves a better trade-off between acceleration and fidelity than existing caching and forecasting methods, reaching up to 6x speedup with less than 0.05 GiB of additional memory.

## 4. LLM2R: From Large Language Models to 3D Reassemblers
**Upvotes:** 👍 21 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.18604`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18604) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18604.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.18604)

### Abstract:
> 3D part assembly aims to reconstruct a complete shape from a set of unassembled parts by predicting a rigid SE(3) transformation for each part. Because the placement of a part is rarely determined by its own geometry, the task demands reasoning over the entire part set, and existing methods, which rely on pairwise relations or iterative generation and verification, scale poorly to objects with many parts. We present LLM2R, which casts part assembly as sequence modeling with a pretrained decoder-only large language model (LLM): a Point-BERT encoder and a Q-Former compress each part into a block of geometric tokens, the blocks of all parts are concatenated, and a causal LLM adapted with Low-Rank Adaptation (LoRA) processes the sequence in one forward pass. Causal attention, however, lets the hidden states of a part observe only the parts that precede it. LLM2R therefore appends a block of learnable summary tokens after the last part: these tokens follow every part, so their states aggregate the whole assembly at a constant token cost, and a part-aware pose head fuses each part's states with them before predicting its rotation and translation. One architecture thus handles semantic and geometric assembly and predicts all poses in a single non-autoregressive pass. LLM2R achieves the highest part accuracy and success rate on all three PartNet categories, the best rotation error, translation error, and part accuracy on PartNeXt, and the lowest translation error and fastest inference on Breaking Bad, where only a fracture-specialized method is more accurate. Its advantage grows with the number of parts: at 100 parts it exceeds GARF by 33.5 points in part accuracy on Breaking Bad and Assembler by 29.1 points on PartNet, and it transfers zero-shot to unseen categories with more than 16 points higher part accuracy than the strongest of three baselines.

## 5. Specter: Fast MoE Inference via Quantization-Enabled Multi-Step Speculative Offloading
**Upvotes:** 👍 20 | **arXiv ID:** `2511.14102`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.14102) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.14102.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2511.14102)

### Abstract:
> Mixture-of-experts (MoE) models often exceed a single accelerator's memory capacity. Offloading experts to host memory creates a severe PCIe I/O bottleneck because data-dependent routing keeps transfers on the critical path. We present Specter, an inference system that uses quantization to combine speculative decoding with expert offloading. Its lightweight quantized draft model runs efficiently on device while preserving high-fidelity expert activation patterns. This provides accurate multi-step lookahead over future expert usage, allowing the runtime to organize expert movement and execution ahead of verification and improve both communication and execution efficiency. Under single-request inference, Specter achieves an average 1.50x (up to 1.88x) speedup in time per output token (TPOT) over the state-of-the-art system. Specter makes memory-constrained, single-GPU MoE inference more practical on commodity hardware. Our code is available at https://github.com/wwwwwf/specter_asplos.

## 6. Evolving Agent Interfaces
**Upvotes:** 👍 19 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.28601`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28601) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28601.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28601)

### Abstract:
> Computer-use agents are limited by the interfaces humans give them. An agent may type a spreadsheet cell by cell when a single line of code would do, or print all two thousand lines of a file through a shell to find one function. Both the interfaces and the knowledge of when to use each are built by hand, which cannot keep pace with new applications, workflows, and models. We argue that interface design should itself be an agent capability. We introduce Agent S4, an autonomous computer-use scientist that builds Evolving Agent Interfaces from task experience. A meta-agent examines task agents' trajectories on synthetic training tasks, without success labels, and constructs and revises interfaces and usage knowledge in a repository. Population-based evolution then selects which repositories to develop further by their performance on validation tasks the meta-agent never sees, favoring changes that generalize beyond the seen tasks. On OSWorld V2, Agent S4, with Qwen3.8-27B and the evolved repository of interfaces, sets a new state of the art in open-source computer-use agents, reaching 21.3% binary score, 3.8 times the same model with fixed interfaces. This is above both GPT-5.5 (13.0%) and Claude Opus 4.8 (20.6%), at about 4.3% and 1.5% of their respective costs. On ScienceBoard, the evolved interfaces improve the success rate from 41.4% to 58.6% with the same model. These results suggest that useful interfaces can be evolved from experience, making autonomous interface design a source of improvement complementary to advances in models and harnesses. We open-source our code at https://github.com/simular-ai/agent-s.

## 7. Epi: Towards a Persistent, Self-Improving Agent for Autonomous Research
**Upvotes:** 👍 18 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.24944`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.24944) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.24944.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.24944)

### Abstract:
> We present Épi, an autonomous research agent that sustains investigations across model sessions and evolves its methods through accumulated experience. Épi combines persistent research history, hierarchical memory, branching and independent review to maintain research continuity, learn from experiments, and explore and revisit alternative approaches. Its experiments run across session boundaries, and a fixed verifier evaluates their outputs. Épi produced 120 record-setting results, comprising 20 numerical bound records across 12 families and 100 construction records across eight families. These include an approximately 84-fold narrower interval for the n-queens constant and a more than 3.6-million-fold narrower rigorous interval for the quartic Feigenbaum attractor dimension. Exact dual certificates establish that the Cohn-Elkies linear-programming bound cannot be sharp in dimensions 10 and 11. Beyond numerical improvements, Épi gives a computer-assisted proof of Tuza's conjecture for split graphs with an eight-vertex clique part and at most three triangle-active neighborhood types, with arbitrary multiplicities. We extend this research process to algorithmic coding with Épi-turbo, a lightweight configuration that develops executable solvers for tasks including vehicle routing, scheduling and production planning. On 30 FrontierOR tasks, Épi-turbo achieves a 5.4% higher total benchmark score than Codex at 64% of its model cost, using the same underlying model and reasoning effort. Épi is designed to support human understanding by connecting computational results to explicit methods and mathematical arguments that researchers can examine and build upon.

## 8. Painting Change Map as guidance for generating painting processes
**Upvotes:** 👍 17 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.25369`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.25369) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.25369.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.25369)

### Abstract:
> Digital art generation has undergone a massive paradigm shift, moving from static image synthesis to dynamic video generation that can mimic the human painting process. While existing models can synthesize visually stunning time-lapses of paintings being created or "unpainted," they largely treat the creative process as a black box. Current conditioning mechanisms are either too rigid, relying on static geometric cues like Canny edges or depth maps, or too holistic, utilizing simple scalar progress variables that offer no localized control. Consequently, users lack the agency to steer how a painting evolves over time. In this work, we argue that true control over the video generation of artwork requires a joint understanding of two fundamental dimensions: where an artist acts, and when those actions take place. To achieve this, we introduce the Painting Change Map, a novel spatio-temporal control mechanism that explicitly tracks normalized structural changes across a 1,000-timestep continuum, a resolution sufficient to capture fine-grained stroke-level changes while remaining computationally tractable. By mapping localized visual differences into an encoded RGB space, our representation enables fine-grained guidance for both the forward painting and reverse unpainting processes. Extensive benchmarking demonstrates the efficacy of our approach. We outperform existing control baselines and achieve competitive or superior results compared to state-of-the-art painting methods. Furthermore, under oracle conditions, our method surpasses all baselines by a significant margin. Code is available at https://github.com/Markus-Pobitzer/painting-change-map.

## 9. FlowState: Execution State as Memory for Long-Horizon LLM Agents
**Upvotes:** 👍 16 | **arXiv ID:** `2609.34565`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.34565) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.34565.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.34565)

### Abstract:
> Long-horizon tasks require LLM agents to continually draw on information from earlier interactions. However, retaining the full history increases context costs, while compressing it risks losing details needed later, and the relevance of historical information often becomes apparent as the task progresses. To address these challenges, we propose FlowState, which treats execution state as memory that can be retained and revisited across requests, unifying current decision-making with the reuse of historical information. FlowState preserves semantically typed state nodes, their relations, and references to raw tool observations, separating persistent retention from on-demand access. Within a single execution loop, Incremental State Update (ISU) maintains the current state based on new inputs and feedback, while Progressive State Access (PSA) progressively reveals historical states and supporting evidence as needed during reasoning. Together, these mechanisms enable agents to reassess prior decisions in light of new information and guide subsequent actions. Compared with a full-context baseline using the same DeepSeek-V4-Flash model, FlowState improves the average success rate on MemoryArena and the average pass rate on τ^3-Bench by 4.55 and 13.95 percentage points, respectively, while reducing total token consumption by 43.2% and 40.6%. These results demonstrate the performance and efficiency advantages of FlowState on long-horizon tasks.

## 10. Event-STVG: Spatio-Temporal Video Grounding for Long-Form Event Reasoning
**Upvotes:** 👍 16 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.22444`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22444) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22444.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22444)

### Abstract:
> Spatio-Temporal Video Grounding (STVG) aims to jointly localize a target in space and time given a natural-language query. Despite continued advances, existing STVG benchmarks remain largely centered on localizing targets through instance-specific descriptions, leaving them largely decoupled from long-form reasoning increasingly emphasized in multimodal large language models (MLLMs). In this paper, we introduce Event-STVG, a task that shifts STVG from locating what is described toward event-level reasoning by unifying long-form context, repeated-event reasoning, event localization, role inference, and multi-target grounding. To facilitate research on Event-STVG, we construct a large-scale benchmark comprising 4,016 annotated event occurrences across soccer and seven additional domains. Experiments with state-of-the-art MLLMs reveal substantial difficulty, with the strongest model achieving only 5.4% m-vIoU at the 10-minute horizon. Across domains, we consistently observe confusion among repeated same-type events, spatial grounding bottlenecks, and rare success in jointly grounding all required roles. Event-STVG establishes a new challenge for visual understanding in MLLMs, calling for future models that can jointly reason about complex events across space and time. Code and annotations are available at https://github.com/THU-KEG/EventSTVG
