## 1. SO-Arena: Benchmarking the Capability Spectrum and Reliability of System One Models
**Upvotes:** 👍 82 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.15777`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15777) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15777.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.15777)

### Abstract:
> System One models promise fast, general-purpose decision-making without lengthy token generation. However, understanding their capability spectrum and decision reliability requires more than aggregate performance on current benchmarks. Existing evaluations often provide limited insight into the breadth of model capabilities and the reliability of their decisions. We introduce SO-Arena, a benchmark characterizing the capability spectrum and decision reliability of System One models across 11 domains and five capability families. For the capability spectrum, SO-Arena offers evidence-grounded evaluation through reliable sources and manual verification of reference labels against model-visible inputs; fine-grained capability assessment through domain-specific difficulty criteria; and realistic interactive evaluation, where models iteratively execute actions in sandboxes. Reasoning-intensive tasks further enable system one-two comparison. Beyond accuracy, SO-Arena assesses decision reliability through two complementary measures: decision stability under controlled option-set changes and missing-answer recognition when no valid option is available. Evaluations across 21 models reveal that the benefits of scaling and additional reasoning vary substantially across domains. More importantly, strong overall performance does not necessarily imply reliable decision-making: the best-performing System One model ranks only seventh in reliability. Together, these findings highlight the need to characterize capability spectrum and decision reliability as complementary dimensions, providing a systematic view of the strengths and limitations of System One models.

## 2. BirdDreams: Joint Perspective and BEV Video Generation for a Plausible, Reactive Driving World Model
**Upvotes:** 👍 46 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.14815`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14815) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.14815.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.14815)

### Abstract:
> Driving world models aim to simulate how a scene unfolds under different ego actions. This requires plausible motion of surrounding vehicles as well as precise ego-trajectory control. Learning vehicle motion from perspective video alone is challenging because motion is entangled with appearance and vehicles can be occluded. Prior work therefore conditions video generation on layouts that specify vehicle motion, providing control over surrounding vehicles but requiring their future trajectories as inputs. We introduce BirdDreams, an autoregressive driving world model that jointly generates perspective video and a schematic bird's-eye-view (BEV) video, making scene layout part of the predicted future. The BEV, rendered from scene annotations for training, depicts roads and vehicles as simple shapes at a fixed metric scale, providing a top-down view of their positions and motion with reduced occlusion between vehicles. Perspective video, by contrast, retains scene details that the BEV omits, such as traffic signals and object appearance. Joint attention between two video diffusion transformers, one per stream, lets each stream inform the other during generation. The predicted BEV also provides a geometric interface for ego control: we translate and rotate its latest frame along an input ego trajectory and use the resulting frames as a soft condition that shows how the scene would appear to move if only the ego vehicle moved. At inference, BirdDreams generates both videos from a single camera image without requiring an HD map or vehicle-state inputs. On PhysicalAI-AV and nuScenes, BirdDreams follows input ego trajectories more faithfully than prior driving world models and generates more plausible ego motion without ego-action conditioning. Human evaluation shows that BirdDreams outperforms a perspective-only baseline in generating realistic surrounding-vehicle behavior.

## 3. Scaling Mathematical Proof Supervision from Structured Textbooks
**Upvotes:** 👍 44 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.40394`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40394) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40394.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.40394)

### Abstract:
> Mathematical supervision for language models is typically flattened into isolated problem-solution pairs, discarding the curricular organization and evidential structure of the original sources. We introduce TOME-Math (Textbook-Oriented Mathematical Evidence), a large-scale corpus that converts 2,118 textbooks spanning the core areas of university-level mathematics subjects into approximately 380K structured proof-supervision records while preserving chapter order, proposition-proof links, dependency references, and source provenance. Leveraging these structural signals, we explore curriculum learning across multiple mathematical subjects and characterize scaling patterns with supervision volume and source breadth. At full scale, fine-tuning on TOME-Math raises the unweighted mean score across our held-out evaluations and established public benchmarks by 16.7 percentage points over the base model, while exceeding all three public instruction-data baselines evaluated in aggregate. Our findings highlight structured textbooks as both a scalable source of mathematical supervision and an empirical framework for studying the impact of data organization on mathematical reasoning. We will release the corpus to facilitate further research on structured mathematical supervision.

## 4. PhysLoop: Harnessing LLMs' Physics Awareness for Self-Improving Video Generation Models
**Upvotes:** 👍 42 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.16748`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16748) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.16748.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.16748)

### Abstract:
> Video generation models produce visually realistic videos but still struggle with physical dynamics and causal consistency. Learning from generated videos requires fine-grained physical supervision, yet reliable quality judgments, correction pairs, and controlled variations are difficult to obtain. Large language models (LLMs) possess physical knowledge, but struggle to reliably guide video generation models in producing their training videos. We present PhysLoop, a unified harness designed to support physically grounded self-improvement in video generation models. The harness guides and constrains LLMs throughout video generation, selection, and correction, distilling their physical knowledge into physically consistent videos. The framework supports controllable training-data construction and inference-time video repair. By identifying and correcting failures, PhysLoop marks a key milestone toward recursive self-improvement (RSI) in video generation models with limited human intervention. We release 100K videos spanning diverse, realistic indoor and outdoor scenes, along with generation tools and two evaluation datasets: PhysLoop-Gen and PhysLoop-Repair. We evaluate controllable generation and localized repair on these datasets, respectively, and assess physical behavior on the external Physics-IQ Verified benchmark.

## 5. G-STAR: Group-Scaled Token Capacity for Deeply Compressed Autoregressive Generation
**Upvotes:** 👍 41 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.13004`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13004) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13004.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13004)

### Abstract:
> Discrete autoregressive models offer a scalable framework for image generation. However, they face a fundamental trade-off: long token sequences preserve representation quality but slow generation, while aggressive spatial compression reduces representation capacity. Based on product quantization, we show that, under a fixed nominal information budget, group size should scale quadratically with the per-axis spatial compression factor. Building on this principle, we propose GSTAR, a spatial--group co-scaling framework that enables \(32\times\) and even effective \(64\times\) compression while preserving strong reconstruction, understanding, and generation performance. Since larger groups introduce intra-group dependencies that must be modeled efficiently, we analyze their impact on inference latency and generation quality and introduce a semi-autoregressive decoder that combines parallel feature computation with lightweight causal modeling. With these designs, our highly compressed models perform competitively with leading models across five representative text-to-image benchmarks (GenEval, DPG-Bench, PRISM, LongText-Bench, and Qwen-Image-Bench). At \(1024\times1024\) resolution, our model delivers a \(20\times\) speedup over prior discrete autoregressive models, generating each image in 2.5 seconds on a single NVIDIA H200. Together, these results show that highly compressed discrete representations can enable high-quality autoregressive generation at low latency.

## 6. Duo: A Reciprocal World Model for Full-Duplex Interaction
**Upvotes:** 👍 37 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.15131`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15131) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15131.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.15131)

### Abstract:
> In this report, we introduce the Reciprocal World Model (RWM) and present Duo v1, a full-duplex world model that continuously perceives audiovisual inputs while responding to users in real time. The RWM treats intelligence and physical reality as equally fundamental to what constitutes a world. Existing world models largely focus on simulating the latter, leaving reasoning and decision-making as external controls rather than intrinsic parts of the world itself. In RWM, we identify two equally fundamental capabilities: Intent, which provides the cognition underlying world interactions, and Realization, which generates coherent audio-visual behavior. To realize this formulation, Duo v1 introduces three key technical designs: (1) Leveraging autoregressive modeling for continuous interaction reasoning and participation decisions, rather than restricting it to instruction understanding for diffusion models. (2) Constructing chunk-specific conditions and synchronize tokens across Intent and Realization on a unified timeline. Module-specific attention masks enforce causal dependencies both within and across chunks, preventing access to future inputs. (3) Mitigating distribution shift in both modules through dual rollout distillation, training on autoregressively generated Intent tokens and synthesized audiovisual histories from Realization. Both modules continuously perceive real-time audio, video, and text, enabling full-duplex interaction with spontaneous conversations and interruptions. Beyond conversation, users can share real objects, reshape virtual scenes, guide assistants, or play audiovisual games without mouse or keyboard controls. The release of Duo v1 makes our first step, with the next version coming soon. We look forward to broader exploration of this emerging field.

## 7. SteerDistill: Quantizing LLM Agents via Closed-Loop Teacher Steering
**Upvotes:** 👍 35 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.13410`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13410) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13410.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13410)

### Abstract:
> Low-bit quantization reduces the inference cost of LLM agents but can compromise task success. Examining failed rollouts, we find that quantized agents overlook task requirements or environmental conditions, producing environment transitions that diverge from successful full-precision references. Correcting these behavioral divergences remains difficult: offline data may miss the student's failure contexts, while its own rollouts may provide few correct responses in those contexts. We introduce SteerDistill, a quantization-aware distillation method in which a full-precision teacher guides the student to revise failed responses and iteratively refines its guidance using feedback from student execution. This closed-loop teacher steering produces locally verified behavioral corrections. We internalize these corrections by training the student without guidance to match the steered student's predictions at corrected turns and the full-precision teacher's at other turns, enabling unassisted inference. Across five agentic benchmarks and three models, SteerDistill exceeds the strongest quantized baseline by 1.67--2.18 points in average score under W4A16 and stays within one point of BF16. On τ^2-Bench, it outperforms QeRL by 1.84 percentage points in success rate using 67% fewer training GPU hours and produces shorter successful trajectories than the quantized baselines.

## 8. Open Vision Banana: A Fully Open Recipe for Generative Visual Perception
**Upvotes:** 👍 30 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.15844`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15844) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15844.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.15844)

### Abstract:
> Recent work suggests that pretrained image generators encode real-world understanding beyond image synthesis. Vision Banana exploits this by framing vision tasks as conditional image editing. However, its data, weights, code, and implementation details are not public. We present Open Vision Banana (OVB), a fully open and reproducible recipe for converting pretrained generators into unified multi-task perception systems. Our analyses identify the design choices that matter most for generalization, including color encoding, prompt design, parameter adaptation, and task balancing. These choices interact, so individually helpful choices can hurt when combined with others. Starting from 4B and 9B parameter open-weight base models and using 2.1M-3.8M training pairs from public datasets, OVB surpasses Vision Banana's reported Cityscapes zero-shot performance (73.9 vs. 69.9 mIoU) and rivals state-of-the-art affine-invariant depth estimators. Its non-zero-shot variants come within five points of Vision Banana on referring and reasoning segmentation but remain further behind on instance segmentation. All models largely retain the base model's image-editing abilities. With legend ensembling, we introduce a test-time scaling method for generator-based perception. It segments an image under several class-color legends and fuses the results by majority vote, correcting unstable color-class bindings and improving mIoU by up to 8.7 points on Cityscapes and 15.0 points on COCO-Object without retraining. We release the dataset construction and processing pipeline, the training and evaluation code, as well as the final, intermediate, and ablation checkpoints. Project website: https://openvisionbanana.github.io

## 9. Actor, Judge, or Teacher? Understanding Decision Models in Long-Horizon Agents
**Upvotes:** 👍 29 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.17167`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17167) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17167.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17167)

### Abstract:
> Decision models answer a question with a typed output, such as a choice among options with a probability for each, so one model can choose an agent's actions, evaluate actions already taken, or score every action to teach a policy with reinforcement learning. Prior work has studied them mainly as actors, judging their usefulness by how well they act. We ask whether a decision model that acts poorly can still teach well. We compare three decision models and three general LLMs as actors and judges on ALFWorld, and use JEV as a teacher on ALFWorld and WebShop. As actors, all six models are pulled away from a subgoal that must come first by an easier one. As judges that see the whole trajectory, the same models credit the steps toward that subgoal, and asking whether each action helped, rather than whether the task will succeed, separates useful from useless actions. As a teacher, JEV raises a Qwen2.5-1.5B policy over GRPO from 72.8% to 82.9% success on ALFWorld and from 56.8% to 71.4% on WebShop. These results suggest that a poor actor can still teach well, because judging an action after the episode ends reveals the consequences that acting must anticipate. Code is available at https://github.com/ZJU-REAL/decision-agents.

## 10. Ropedia Xperience 2: A Gold Standard for Scaling Physical AI Environments and Behaviors
**Upvotes:** 👍 26 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.17060`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17060) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17060.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17060)

### Abstract:
> Large foundation models such as GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 have recently been shown to control robots directly from visual observations. They nevertheless remain brittle on long-horizon, contact-rich tasks in real-world environments. Further progress depends on two complementary resources: demonstrations of human behavior that supervise how tasks are performed, and interactive environments in which policies can be rolled out, evaluated, and improved in closed loop. Language models have advanced along well-characterized scaling laws; the corresponding question for physical AI is how capability scales with environments and behaviors, which first requires identifying which data to scale and how to produce and reuse such data continuously. To address this question, we introduce Ropedia Xperience 2, comprising 1,589 released metric-scale 3D scans and a collection of 4,230 egocentric recordings of 74 tasks (131 hours). We further show that each capture can be repurposed for robot learning: scenes are converted into physically interactive simulation environments, human motion is retargeted to whole-body robot trajectories, and additional robot behaviors are obtained through virtual teleoperation and automated long-horizon trajectory generation. We propose this joint capture of environments and behaviors as a gold standard for physical AI data collection, laying a data foundation for establishing scaling laws over environments and behaviors.
