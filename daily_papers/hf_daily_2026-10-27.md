## 1. Reading, Writing, Sharing, and Interacting with the World through Video
**Upvotes:** 👍 267 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.31668`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31668) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31668.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31668)

### Abstract:
> Video foundation models have advanced rapidly in understanding and generating visual sequences, while video world models seek to capture the underlying dynamics of the world they depict. Despite their different objectives, both concern the relationship between video observations and an evolving world. This survey adopts world modeling as a unifying lens for examining these developments. Viewing an observed video as a partial, lossy, and temporally ordered projection of a latent temporal world, we organize the literature around four complementary operations: reading infers world properties from observed video; writing synthesizes video from specified or learned world conditions; sharing integrates understanding and generation through common modeling mechanisms; and interacting predicts and updates world states under actions and evolving observations. This perspective distinguishes temporal coherence in observed or generated sequences from consistency in the underlying world state. Across tasks, representations, models, datasets, and benchmarks, we trace a shift in emphasis from clip-level recognition and perceptual fidelity toward persistent state, controllable evolution, physical and causal validity, and evaluation grounded in interaction and downstream decisions, and we distill the open challenges that follow.

## 2. HiSTEVE 1.0: Harnessing Human-Interface VLA Agents for Open-World Games
**Upvotes:** 👍 38 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.31162`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31162) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31162.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31162)

### Abstract:
> Building virtual embodied agents that operate in open-ended games like human players is a long-standing goal requiring both long-horizon autonomy and reliable native-interface control. As a procedurally generated open world, Minecraft provides a natural testbed for studying the integration of these capabilities. Existing agents typically provide either native visuomotor control without long-horizon autonomy or high-level planning executed through environment APIs or predefined skills. To bridge this gap, we propose HiSTEVE, a hierarchical VLA agent with a capability-aware agent harness. At the orchestration level, we equip the harness with structured memory for tracking progress and a VLM planner for selecting subtasks within the VLA's capabilities. To reliably determine subtask completion, we further introduce a policy-coupled task completion state detector that conditions on VLA policy context to signal candidate completion. At the control level, a single VLA executes all subtasks with vision-only perception and native keyboard-and-mouse actions. To mitigate interference between heterogeneous GUI and non-GUI interactions, we introduce regime-specific RMSNorm branches and separate mouse-movement token embeddings. We also train the VLA through world knowledge injection, imitation learning, and reinforcement learning with environment feedback to improve reliability and recovery. HiSTEVE achieves state-of-the-art atomic-task performance, scoring 85.0% on MineStudio Bench and a 37.4% average success rate on OpenHA, while also completing long-horizon tasks such as crafting an iron pickaxe from scratch like human players.

## 3. RSIHub: A Composable Framework for Agent-Evolution Methods
**Upvotes:** 👍 34 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.32253`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32253) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32253.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32253)

### Abstract:
> Agent-evolution methods use execution feedback to improve prompts, skills, tools, and orchestration, but their implementations often rebuild the same experimental machinery. We present RSIHub, an open-source framework for building, comparing, and extending these methods on shared infrastructure. Recipes compose reusable operators and declare mutable resources; RSIHub supplies execution, evaluation, recovery, and lineage. Researchers can focus on evolution policy while evaluation stays outside the mutable surface and results remain tied to candidates and evaluation conditions. We instantiate AHE, Hyperagents, A-Evolve, and GEPA without modifying the framework core, keeping method-specific logic in recipes and operators. We evaluate them with MiniSWE and Codex on Terminal Bench 2 and τ^3 Banking under fixed model and evaluator configurations. The largest observed overall pass@1 gain is 13.5 percentage points, while method rankings vary across settings and optimization gains do not always transfer to held-out tasks. RSIHub provides a common protocol for comparing evolution policies, inspectable candidate histories, and reusable components for developing new methods.

## 4. RynnUnify: Towards Unified Embodied Brain
**Upvotes:** 👍 33 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.32965`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32965) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32965.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32965)

### Abstract:
> We introduce RynnUnify, a 16B unified embodied model that combines strong embodied understanding with long-horizon visual planning through interleaved video--language reasoning. With capabilities spanning spatial reasoning, physical prediction, and grounding, RynnUnify connects an understanding of the physical world with visual anticipation of how it evolves across successive subtasks. Building on these capabilities, we further develop an agentic extension through reinforcement learning to selectively use video predictions for reasoning about unobserved states and interaction outcomes. We also develop a world-action extension of the full RynnUnify model, translating its understanding and prediction capabilities into robot control. To evaluate these capabilities, we introduce Rynn-VP and Rynn-GEU for long-horizon visual planning and anticipatory reasoning. Experiments show that RynnUnify achieves the highest average embodied understanding score among the evaluated unified multimodal models and improves the Rynn-VP overall score by 9.75 points over Cosmos 3 Nano. Its agentic extension improves the Rynn-GEU average by 6.0 points over direct reasoning, while the world-action extension achieves 51.5\% success on 20 selected composite RoboCasa365 tasks and 46.7\% average success across three real-robot tasks. Together, these results establish RynnUnify as a shared foundation for embodied understanding, visual planning, and robot control, taking a step toward a unified embodied brain.

## 5. WorldCast: World-Consistent Character Video Generation with Large-Angle Camera Control
**Upvotes:** 👍 27 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.33135`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33135) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33135.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33135)

### Abstract:
> Precise camera control is essential for cinematic character video generation, yet existing methods struggle to follow large-angle trajectories with combined rotation, translation, and radius variation from arbitrary initial azimuths. Such viewpoint changes also reveal character regions unseen in the initial frame, causing geometric distortions, appearance degradation, and identity drift. To systematically study these coupled challenges, we introduce CLACT, a large-scale Character-centric dataset and benchmark covering diverse Large-Angle Camera Trajectories, with accurate camera geometry and multi-view character observations for training and evaluation. We further propose WorldCast, a framework for world-consistent character video generation under diverse large-angle camera motions. Specifically, we back-project the initial frame into a 3D point cloud using estimated depth, align the point-cloud scale with the target camera trajectory, and render it along the trajectory to obtain explicit geometric guidance and coverage masks. To complement the incomplete character information, we introduce Geometry-Aligned Memory Attention (GAMA). Using relative camera poses estimated jointly from the initial frame and multi-view character anchors, we express anchor and target-view geometry in a common coordinate frame. GAMA uses this geometric alignment to guide the retrieval and injection of view-relevant character features during generation. Evaluations across diverse large-angle camera trajectories demonstrate accurate trajectory following while preserving character geometry, appearance, and identity.

## 6. Trajectory2Gym: Decision-Grounded Compositional Interaction Synthesis for Tool-Use Agents
**Upvotes:** 👍 23 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.33568`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.33568) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.33568.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.33568)

### Abstract:
> Successful tool-use trajectories are not necessarily useful supervision. Even successful interactions may underrepresent multi-step dependencies or contain actions that are unjustified by the information visible to the agent at decision time. We identify two coupled requirements for effective trajectory supervision: compositional coverage, so that important observation-action dependencies are represented, and decision grounding, so that each supervised action is supported by the learner-visible interaction history. We introduce Trajectory2Gym, which constructs executable interactions around under-supported compositional structures and derives supervision only from grounded decisions in their realized executions. Structural-support selection allocates the resulting experience, while parent-normalized supervised fine-tuning preserves the semantic interaction as the unit of training weight. This reframes trajectory generation as the deliberate construction of learnable interaction experience, rather than the collection of successful trajectories. Across five Qwen3 scales, Trajectory2Gym improves Tau1, Tau2, and BFCL at every tested scale, increasing the equal-weight three-benchmark mean by 16.9-29.8 percentage points. The gains are strongest in stateful and multi-turn interaction, where later decisions depend on earlier observations and feedback. Our results suggest that scaling tool-use agents depends not only on collecting more successful trajectories, but on constructing interactions whose dependencies are represented and whose decisions are learnable from the agent's visible history.

## 7. Every Weight Is a Path of Shared Discrete Decisions
**Upvotes:** 👍 19 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.31931`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31931) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31931.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31931)

### Abstract:
> We introduce TWIG (Tree-structured Weights with Inherited Gates), a representation in which every weight of a neural network is the end of a path of small discrete decisions: a sign, a shift by a power of two, a zero gate, an index into a shared table. Weights that belong together share the upper part of their paths, and each decision is inherited by every weight below it, so it is stored once and applied once. This simple change has three consequences. Any trained network can be written as a TWIG, and when the decisions are powers of two its weight layers become circuits made almost entirely of wires and adders, suited to chips whose weights are fixed at fabrication. Pruning, quantization, low-rank factorization, codebooks and weight sharing become edits of one object, so a single optimizer can choose among all of them for a whole network. And choosing the decisions is the ground-state problem of a higher-order Boltzmann machine whose interactions follow the paths of the tree, the problem class that annealers and quantum optimizers are built to solve. Today these questions are handled apart: each compression method brings its own format and solver, a circuit with fixed weights needs a dedicated constant multiplier for every weight, and discrete solvers reach only narrow subproblems such as rounding. Weight formats have long been moving toward shared decisions, through group scales, shared exponents, microexponents and sparse weights; TWIG takes this trend to its end, and every common format is a shallow TWIG at no extra cost. On five weight matrices of a large language model, a flat tree whose signed power-of-two terms are chosen per weight for circuit cost, with no group scales, needs 9--15\% fewer additions than GPTQ at equal layer error at 3- and 4-bit accuracy on typical matrices, and 20--55\% fewer on matrices with strong outliers.

## 8. Dual Exam: Controlled Evaluation of Multimodal Agents and World Models
**Upvotes:** 👍 17 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.31066`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31066) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31066.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.31066)

### Abstract:
> World Models (WMs) can support Multimodal Agents (MAs) through simulated experience, while Agent trajectories can inform World Model refinement. Evaluating this interaction requires separate evidence about action selection and state evolution. We introduce Dual Exam, which tests Agents under fixed environment response mechanisms and World Models under fixed action interventions across physical, digital, social, and scientific domains. The Agent track compares 10 configurations on 1,088 items in 15 reporting categories. The World Model track contains 400 cases across eight scenarios; 15 configurations have full coverage and one covers six scenarios. GPT 6 Astra leads the case-weighted Agent Index at 31.3, and GPT-Image-2 leads the full-coverage WM Overall at 21.7. These scores use different tasks and metrics and are not directly comparable. A 150-case pilot of one coupled pairing records zero task successes and motivates component-level diagnosis. Results reveal uneven task performance and failures to preserve task-relevant state. Their value for predicting coupled success or sustained reciprocal learning remains to be established.

## 9. On-Policy Self-Training: Data-Free Self-Improvement in Large Language Models
**Upvotes:** 👍 14 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.32770`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32770) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32770.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.32770)

### Abstract:
> On-policy self-distillation enables post-training foundation models without relying on stronger external teachers. However, existing approaches rely on ground-truth question-answer pairs, human-curated problem sets, or other supervised signals. In this work, we reformulate on-policy self-distillation as a self-training framework, driven by self-generated question-answer pairs and privileged reasoning demonstrations. We push supervision to the limit: guided solely by coarse, high-level topic descriptions, the model synthesizes its own curriculum of queries and privileged reasoning traces without access to any human-curated prompts or answers. We find that self-distillation on fully synthetic privileged information enables a model to improve across multiple domains while retaining its capabilities on domains it is not self-trained on. We demonstrate the generality of this framework across text and multimodal domains, achieving consistent self-improvement on standard language benchmarks and Visual Question Answering. Our findings demonstrate that foundation models possess rich latent capabilities that can be amplified without external task supervision or curated problem distributions.

## 10. TenetBench: A Clause-Anchored Russian Corpus for Evaluating Language Models against Professional Codes of Ethics
**Upvotes:** 👍 13 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.29477`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29477) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29477.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29477)

### Abstract:
> Codes of professional ethics guide and restrict specialists, who increasingly work with assistants built on large language models. Such an assistant can breach the user's code, or help the user breach it, without the user noticing. Evaluations reporting broad categories of harm cannot show how often this happens or which provision is breached. TenetBench pairs 3,433 requests conflicting with clauses of fifteen Russian-language professional codes with benign twins that differ only in the legitimacy of what is asked. An automated pipeline generates the tasks from the text of a code, so the corpus grows with every code added and a provision yields new tasks on every run. It also produced tasks and human-validated verdicts from two English codes of other jurisdictions. In human validation of the frozen pipeline, specialists rated 0.995 of the conflicting tasks plausible, and the model judge agreed with the adjudicated label at Gwet's AC1 of at least 0.894 on 1,860 elements. We evaluated fourteen model and reasoning-mode combinations on whether they produce the conflicting text, decline or warn. Eleven of the fourteen produce conflicting content on most such requests, mostly without a warning, and nearly three warnings in four accompany conflicting content. The models that conform more do so by declining while still serving the legitimate twin, and enabling reasoning halved the refusals of two models. We hope the dataset and pipeline will help evaluate how language models observe professional norms and let diagnostic datasets be built for particular professions and organisations.
