## 1. Envisioning Agentic Robotics: A New Era of Robotic Systems
**Upvotes:** 👍 116 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.18359`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18359) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18359.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18359)

### Abstract:
> The release of GPT-6 Astra marks the emergence of a new paradigm in robotics: agentic robotics. Within weeks, frontier models with agentic harnesses matched or exceeded task-specific policies on many embodied benchmarks. Instead of training a dedicated policy for each family of tasks, an agentic robotic system applies a foundation model to open-world embodied tasks. It operates in a closed loop: observing the environment, interpreting user intent, reasoning about the task, invoking skills and tools, verifying outcomes, and adapting its strategy. Despite these advances, the community has yet to converge on a shared definition, a reliable evaluation framework, or a research roadmap. We argue that Embodied System 2, realized by a foundation model together with an embodied harness, forms the decision-making core of agentic robotics. Its central challenge is to translate general-purpose reasoning into effective and verifiable actions under physical constraints such as limited reaction time, mechanical limits, unpredictable environment changes, and irreversible consequences. Motivated by this perspective, we propose an evaluation framework that measures both model capabilities and closed-loop system performance. Furthermore, we identify the principles that carry over from digital to physical agents and the challenges that must be addressed to achieve reliable physical autonomy. On top of the Embodied System 2 and the evaluation framework, we envision a research roadmap toward sustained autonomy: the ability to perform work safely and reliably with progressively broader responsibility, from executing individual primitives to fulfilling standing roles.

## 2. Gamer: A Generalist Vision-Language-Action Foundation Model for Long-Horizon Real-Time Game Agent
**Upvotes:** 👍 97 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.18397`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18397) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18397.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18397)

### Abstract:
> We introduce Gamer, a generalist agent framework built for long-horizon tasks in complex interactive environments. Its end-to-end architecture combines perception, memory management, and multi-step reasoning. A shared training and deployment pipeline supports interaction with both conventional 2D graphical interfaces and complex 3D simulated worlds. We first continue pre-training on a dataset constructed from tens of thousands of hours of device-level keyboard and mouse interaction. These trajectories span simulated games and embodied environments, allowing the model to learn environmental dynamics across both physical and virtual domains. Sparse-thinking instruction tuning then substantially reduces redundant computation while preserving the reasoning needed for complex decisions. We train the policy for long-horizon interaction with a stable multi-turn reinforcement learning method that addresses finite context windows, accumulated rollout errors, and sparse rewards. For real-time interaction, a cloud-edge inference engine serves numerous simulated game environments concurrently, supports unbounded-context execution, and keeps online inference latency low. Across multiple challenging 3D open-world games, Gamer demonstrates solid generalization and operational efficiency comparable to skilled players. It also achieves competitive results on a range of conventional 2D game benchmarks. These results suggest that even repetitive keyboard and mouse trajectories can support substantial gains from scaling data and learning algorithms. Training on these trajectories helps Gamer learn complex skills more steadily in environments with both physical and digital properties. This approach offers a path toward generalist agents that can interact across digital and physical worlds.

## 3. VibePrevisBench: Evaluating Agentic Coding for 4D Scene Generation in Blender
**Upvotes:** 👍 86 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.15789`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15789) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.15789.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.15789)

### Abstract:
> Controllable real-world video generation models can condition on a rendered graybox proxy video, which fixes layout, motion and camera. Coding agents can build such videos by reconstructing a 4D scene in Blender, yet no benchmark checks both these videos and the long, multi-shot scenes behind them. We introduce VibePrevisBench, a benchmark for 4D scene reconstruction: an agent turns a brief into an executable 4D scene and renders its proxy video. Its 242 tasks span four input kinds, including scripts, photos, continuations, and reference videos. Each delivery is scored along two evaluation dimensions. Rule-based Scene Check re-runs the code and compares the exported 4D scene with hidden ground truth facts in nine dimensions. VLM-based Screen Check asks a VLM judge whether the facts that pass can be recognized in the proxy video. We analyze the evaluation results and trajectories of 11 frontier coding agents and identify three key observations. First, multimodal understanding separates the agents, especially in understanding space and camera motion. Second, all agents seem to already master basic camera work and staging, and fail at challenging requests. Third, agents inspect their own renders but fail to correct their errors. Agents thus need better visual understanding of space and camera, explicit checks for challenging requests, and item-by-item verification of renders against the brief.

## 4. MIRIX-2.0: Multi-Agent Memory System
**Upvotes:** 👍 47 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.12068`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12068) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12068.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12068)

### Abstract:
> Long-term memory lets LLM agents personalize, stay consistent, and reuse what worked before. Parametric memories need training, consolidation can lose details for good, and keeping every record leaves a growing store to search. We present MIRIX-2.0, a multi-agent memory system designed to be practical, accurate, and efficient at once. It is the most accurate of the compared systems on four of five QA benchmarks. It reaches 91.4% on LoCoMo and 72.6% on LongMemEval-S, where it leads the strongest baseline by 14.3 points, and with AutoDream disabled it gains 31.0 and 22.0 points over HyperMem on single-hop and multi-hop document QA. As procedural memory, it yields the highest mean success among the compared memory methods on ALFWorld and WebShop and reaches 49.2% on MetaClaw-Bench, against 44.0% for MetaClaw's skill library. Six typed memory agents keep complete records as the system of record, and a provenance hypergraph indexes episodic and semantic records with n-ary frames that cite their rows. Retrieval runs under explicit budgets, so the evidence a question inspects does not grow with the history. AutoDream merges duplicate anchors outside the write path without rewriting any record, and it distills task sessions into skills. With AutoDream disabled, MIRIX-2.0 answers faster than HyperMem on all five timed benchmarks. It needs no fine-tuning, runs with closed and open models, and its code is public.

## 5. Rethinking in Spikes: Mitigating Hallucinations in MDLMs with Step-Aware Decoding
**Upvotes:** 👍 36 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.12901`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12901) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12901.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12901)

### Abstract:
> Recent advancements in multimodal diffusion language models (MDLMs) have exhibited strong global modeling capabilities through iterative refinement. We observe that mask uncertainty follows an overall downward trend during iterative denoising, while a few denoising steps exhibit localized entropy spikes closely associated with hallucinations. We argue that anomalous steps can be identified by measuring the entropy deviation of each denoising step from its neighboring temporal window. We hypothesize that discrete token commitment discards the distributional information at masked positions, limiting the continued interaction between candidate semantics and the context during entropy spikes. With this goal, we present StepRefine, a plug-and-play decoding strategy that leverages semantic context to achieve reliable denoising. The core of our method lies in a step-aware switch between discrete denoising and latent refinement. The model employs probability-weighted embeddings at entropy spike steps to preserve diverse reasoning hypotheses and performs multiple rounds of continuous refinement. Meanwhile, a refinement controller monitors distributional stability to reduce unnecessary loops and switch the model back to standard discrete denoising. Through extensive experiments, StepRefine demonstrates significant hallucination mitigation across different MDLMs on multimodal benchmarks.

## 6. MMFlash: Faster Speculative Decoding for Vision-Language Models
**Upvotes:** 👍 35 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.18482`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18482) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.18482.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.18482)

### Abstract:
> Speculative decoding accelerates generation by letting a lightweight drafter propose tokens for parallel verification by a target model. Diffusion drafters such as DFlash propose an entire token block in a single forward pass, but mainly focus on text-only tasks, leaving multimodal drafting underexplored. To study this setting, we first introduce a metric of token-level visual dependence based on the KL divergence between next-token distributions with and without visual input. We find that acceptance rates decrease from 46% in the lowest visual-dependence quartile to 22% in the highest, with this disparity persisting after multimodal training. Motivated by this observation, we propose MMFlash, a simple and effective framework that adaptively upweights visually dependent tokens that the drafter predicts poorly. For long video inputs, MMFlash further employs sparse attention to reduce redundant attention computation and improve drafting quality. Across various multimodal benchmarks, MMFlash achieves lossless decoding speedups of up to 3.58times, outperforming EAGLE-3 and DFlash by up to 195.4% and 17.1%, respectively.

## 7. V-JEPA-LM: Video-Language Models That Read Dynamics Beyond Static, Text-Aligned Representations
**Upvotes:** 👍 25 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.17831`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17831) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17831.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17831)

### Abstract:
> World-model video encoders such as V-JEPA learn how scenes change (direction, speed, order, state) from video alone. To answer questions, they are joined to a language model trained on captions and question-answer pairs. But each caption or question names only a few attributes of a clip, so the model learns to retrieve those attributes rather than to read the clip, and the dynamics the encoder carries go unused: on the same items, a linear probe reads speed from frozen V-JEPA features at 87% while the VLM answers at chance. We show where dynamics lives in a frozen encoder: in a subspace shared across videos for predictive encoders such as V-JEPA, but not as a shared component of frame-wise, text-aligned encoders such as SigLIP. We then build counterfactual supervision that makes the language model read it: pixel transformations turn public videos into answer-balanced variants whose answers are set by the transformation and differ only in the change. Trained in a single stage on 4.53M public samples with 32 visual tokens per frame, where open video LLMs use several stages, 9M to 172M samples and 64 to 256 tokens per frame on average, the resulting V-JEPA-LM models (1B to 8B, Qwen3 and Llama) lead every size class in average score across eleven video benchmarks; our 4B model scores 72.1, against 61.1 for the best open 4B and 64.8 for the best open 8B model. A controlled encoder x supervision study shows that the encoder decides what can be read and the supervision what is read: at a matched budget, counterfactual supervision adds 4.5 points on average and 38 on the speed questions of natural benchmarks. Code and models: https://github.com/kkt94/Llama-V-JEPA-LM.

## 8. DySENSE: Dynamic Spatiotemporal ENergy Synthesis and Emulation with Satellite Imagery
**Upvotes:** 👍 22 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.17878`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17878) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.17878.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.17878)

### Abstract:
> Cities account for over two-thirds of the world's energy and more than 70% of global carbon dioxide emissions. City energy is essential for achieving the 2050 net-zero target and the UN's Sustainable Development Goals 7 and 11. Although existing studies have made progress, they are limited by data and methodological gaps. Existing studies are largely predictive for existing cities rather than generative for future cities; fine-grained, aligned monthly dynamic energy data are scarce; and existing GenAI models struggle to generate dynamic urban functional monthly energy. We present DySENSE, a joint multimodal GenAI framework that synthesizes static urban satellite imagery together with spatially aligned, dynamic monthly building energy consumption maps. We decouple long-term static urban morphology from short-term dynamic energy consumption. By conditioning on static urban land use, DySENSE can generate realistic, diverse and new static urban imagery with aligned energy maps. By conditioning on dynamic monthly and climate prompts, DySENSE can jointly generate dynamic, monthly energy consumption maps while keeping the urban morphology static. Experiments across four cities (New York, Washington, Norfolk, Busan) demonstrated that DySENSE can generate annotated high-fidelity synthetic energy data using less than 25% labelled data, boosting downstream prediction performance by 8% mIoU, and achieving strong energy dynamic consistency. Compared to existing methods, DySENSE significantly improved prediction performance (8.7% accuracy) and reduced prediction error (9%-18% CVRMSE, ASHRAE). DySENSE offers an effective foundation for energy-efficient cities, peak-load analysis, building performance simulation and dynamic demand response for city, energy, and building science. The data and code: https://huggingface.co/datasets/skl24/MUSE2 and https://github.com/kailaisun/DySENSE

## 9. Loopback Transformer: Towards a Unified View of Residual Looped Transformers
**Upvotes:** 👍 21 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.07226`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07226) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.07226.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.07226)

### Abstract:
> Looped Transformers reduce parameter count by repeatedly applying shared blocks, but parameter sharing alone does not determine how the results of repeated computation are reused. We take the depth-memory view of residual connections and analyze which representation is stored after each block application and how later reads weight it. A full block output contains both its input and its new update, so weighting that output also reweights the history already carried by the input. Reading individual updates instead, with the shared stack's input kept on a separate path, gives separate controls over input strength, total update weight, and allocation across updates. The Loopback Transformer implements these controls: geometric decay sets the total update weight and supplies a recency prior, and attention learns how to allocate that weight. On ClimbMix, Loopback reaches lower validation loss than standard Transformer baselines with 40-75% of their Transformer parameters (excluding token embeddings and the output head) at reference scales from 191M to 3B, with training tokens and executed depth matched. It also has higher mean accuracy over thirteen downstream tasks at 1.4B and 3B. At 191M, 374M, and 778M, it has the lowest validation loss and the highest thirteen-task mean among the standard Transformer and seven looped residual designs.

## 10. Mobile AI Factory: Building Specialized Language Models for Your Mobile Device
**Upvotes:** 👍 19 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.13509`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13509) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.13509.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.13509)

### Abstract:
> Small language models (SLMs) running on mobile devices can keep user data private and work without connectivity. But they perform well only when fine-tuned for the target task and sized for the target device, a process that today requires repeated expert tuning. We present {\it Mobile AI Factory}, an agentic system that builds a fine-tuned, quantized SLM for a given task and phone. Given a task specification and a target phone, an orchestrator LLM chooses whether to tune hyperparameters, mine real data, or generate synthetic examples, while fixed rules roll back changes that hurt accuracy. The factory searches from small to large models across base models and quantizations, adapts its accuracy goal as it searches, and uses real measurements of the target phone across inference engines and backends. We evaluate Mobile AI Factory on ten tasks and four Android phones. On an unseen testset, its on-device models outperform two five-shot frontier models on five tasks, by up to 0.34, and match them on three more. Its search comes within 0.03 of exhaustively training all 27 model variants on an entity extraction task, while using 19times less GPU time. Phone measurements also matter: the best configuration changes across phones. Without phone measurements, Claude Opus 5.5, given the full hardware and model specifications, misses the app's requirements in 25\% of easy and moderate cases and in 60\% of hard ones. Code is available at: https://github.com/evanly-gh/SLM_Factory.
