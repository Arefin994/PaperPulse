## 1. Learning to Think and Explore in 3D World
**Upvotes:** 👍 146 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.21783`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21783) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21783.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21783)

### Abstract:
> We study how vision-language models (VLMs) can learn to think and actively explore in 3D space. Instead of providing multi-view images, videos, or 3D representations to the model, we preserve the native visual encoding of VLMs, i.e., a single image, one at a time, just like humans, and treat the 3D representation only as an environment with which the model can interact through actions (e.g., move left) and observations (rendered images). Each rollout begins from an initial first-person view and a visual question. The model can then iteratively reason, navigate, and reorient its viewpoint before committing to a final answer. Following an initial trajectory-supervised finetuning, we apply reinforcement learning (RL) with rewards driven primarily by final-answer correctness. Crucially, RL provides no direct supervision over which viewpoints the model should visit or which exploration actions it should take. Instead, the model must learn an effective exploration policy solely from the extent to which its interactions improve performance. Across six 3D benchmarks, our model ACT3D achieves an average accuracy of 53.2% that improves its base Qwen model by 22.0% and surpasses proprietary Gemini 3.6 Flash. ACT3D also outperforms existing open-source 3D methods while using 50-70% fewer input frames, and generalizes to unseen domains with an average improvement of 5.9% over its supervised initialization. Further analysis reveals adaptive, question-conditioned exploration. Performance gains over the base model increase from 0.6% on questions answerable from the initial view to 16.3% on those requiring extensive exploration. ACT3D searches for occluded targets, avoids unnecessary exploration, and acquires complementary viewpoints for spatial comparisons. These findings demonstrate that outcome-based RL enables VLMs not only to reason over visual evidence, but also to learn when and how to acquire it.

## 2. LEV: Thinking Fast and Deep with Looped Decision Models
**Upvotes:** 👍 132 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.20951`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20951) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20951.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.20951)

### Abstract:
> Decision models answer typed questions about an input with one probability per option, computed in a single forward pass without generating text. Like the fast System 1 of dual-process theory, they answer at once, but their depth is fixed at training time, so they spend the same computation on easy and hard questions. Models that reason in generated text can think longer, but they pay for every extra step in decoded tokens. We present LEV, a decision model that thinks deeper by rerunning its layers in a loop instead of generating tokens. LEV builds on a language model pretrained as a loop, reads the options after every loop step and trains all steps jointly, so one checkpoint can answer at any depth. On three looped backbones, accuracy rises with depth on the same weights, mostly on hard questions, and a 2.6B LEV goes from 63.2% at depth one to 76.2% at depth four on JevBench. On-demand depth, set per question by a small stopper with a threshold fitted on unlabelled requests, saves 15% to 24% of the computation at a cost of under one accuracy point, level with or slightly above a fixed mix of depths. On a locked test set, the 1.4B and 3B models are 5.4 to 8.8 points more accurate than dense (non-looped) models and cascades of the same latency below 50 ms.

## 3. LiveMACE: Process-Aware Evaluation of LLM Agent Capabilities in Evolving Markets
**Upvotes:** 👍 112 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.09872`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09872) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.09872.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.09872)

### Abstract:
> Evaluating agents by outcomes alone can obscure the capabilities that produce them. This problem is especially pronounced in evolving environments, where outcomes reflect a closed-loop interaction between agent behavior and changing external conditions. We introduce LiveMACEBench, a process-aware benchmark that uses live financial markets as a naturally evolving testbed for persistent LLM agents. Five frontier LLMs operate along continuous trajectories under matched Tool Use, Persistent Memory, Rule Following, and Multi-Agent Collaboration configurations. We evaluate them through both realized outcomes and mechanism-specific diagnostics derived from complete decision traces. Across 30 days of live evaluation, we find a pronounced outcome-capability gap: realized returns often diverge from capability-specific measurements, and similar outcomes can arise from markedly different patterns of mechanism use. Trace-level diagnostics further expose distinct bottlenecks across capabilities, demonstrating that mechanism access, effective mechanism use, and downstream performance are not interchangeable measures of agent capability. LiveMACEBench makes this distinction measurable, turning live markets from a performance leaderboard into a diagnostic environment for agent capability

## 4. Scene-Designer: Learning Design Intelligence for Agentic 3D Scene Generation
**Upvotes:** 👍 49 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.23121`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23121) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23121.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23121)

### Abstract:
> Generating complete, coherent, and usable 3D indoor scenes from natural language requires strong design intelligence: the ability to design high-quality scenes and critique their quality. However, existing approaches either rely on general-purpose models lacking domain-specific design experience or learn from static scenes. In this work, we introduce Scene-Designer, an agentic framework that learns design intelligence through complementary design and critique experiences. We construct two datasets: Scene-OTA-35K, which provides Observation--Thought--Action supervision for learning scene design and efficient error correction, and Scene-OTC-15K, which provides Observation--Thought--Critic supervision for learning scene quality critique across physical validity, functional correctness, and layout coherence. Based on these experiences, we propose Scene-GRPO, a Critic-guided GRPO framework that improves design decisions and reduces unnecessary trial-and-error corrections. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Scene-Designer achieves superior performance in physical validity, functional correctness, and layout coherence, while the learned Scene-Critic significantly outperforms general-purpose models in scene critique. Moreover, test-time Critic guidance further improves scene generation, showing that the Critic can provide useful guidance beyond training. Additional analysis suggests a trend of mutual enhancement between scene design and critique abilities.

## 5. PremiseCheck: Efficient Fact-Checking of Human and AI Peer Reviews
**Upvotes:** 👍 49 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.21046`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21046) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21046.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21046)

### Abstract:
> Peer review is under strain from the growth of submissions, and review comments written by humans or AI can misstate what a paper says. We introduce ReviewFact, a benchmark that labels such factual errors in review comments under one definition across NLP, machine learning, and the natural sciences, and that measures cost and latency alongside accuracy. A frontier LLM checks comments well but must read the whole paper for each check, while cheap decision models, which score whether a statement holds in a text, fall far short on whole comments that mix several implicit assertions. PremiseCheck is a framework that combines the two. An LLM rewrites each comment as explicit premises without reading the paper, and a decision model checks each premise against the paper. When each comment is checked on its own, PremiseCheck reaches accuracy comparable to GPT-5.5 across all three fields at about half its cost, and on NLP reviews at a quarter of its cost and less than half its latency.

## 6. BridgeWAM: Bridging Video and Action Experts via Latent Bridge Queries
**Upvotes:** 👍 36 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.14746`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14746) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14746.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.14746)

### Abstract:
> World Action Models (WAMs) commonly couple video and action experts through dense, layer-wise Mixture-of-Transformers (MoT) attention, exposing action prediction to irrelevant visual features while enforcing matched expert depths. This coupling hinders selective visual-feature use and introduces redundant computation. We introduce Bridge-of-Experts (BoE), instantiated as BridgeWAM, which aggregates visual, language, and proprioceptive priors through 32 Latent Bridge Queries (LBQs) into a shared, limited latent space. Joint video-action supervision lets action gradients guide the Video Expert toward representations better suited for action prediction, while the Action Expert learns to selectively retrieve task-relevant information through cross-attention. Decoupling expert depths and reusing LBQs during action denoising yield a 5.06B model with a 30-layer Video Expert and a two-layer Action Expert, achieving 98.25% success on LIBERO and 72.24% pooled success on LIBERO-Plus, demonstrating effective task execution and strong generalization with an ultralight action head. Ablations support joint representation adaptation and unified LBQ conditioning. Relative to Fast-WAM, BridgeWAM reduces single-sample inference FLOPs by 28.72% in simulation profiling and real-robot latency to 77.1 ms (a 2.47-fold speedup). Qualitative experimental results further indicate preferential attention to target objects and task destinations across perturbations.

## 7. Lexical Reference Frames: Interpreting LM Residual Streams in Native Semantics
**Upvotes:** 👍 32 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.22169`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22169) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22169.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22169)

### Abstract:
> The vocabulary of a language model is its native semantics: the embedding and the unembedding fix the meaning of a state at the two ends of the residual stream. Between them, existing methods either reuse a fixed lens or treat the states as a point cloud, and neither reads a state the way the model does. We argue that every hidden state has a native reference frame, induced by the layers that read it next and adaptive to the context. Lexical reference frames approximate it by reading a state through the model's own remaining layers with a stated number of rounds of attention between positions and a typical attention output for the rest, without fitting to the model's output. No lens, with fixed directions per layer, can exceed the square root of the native frame's coherence, 0.30 and 0.35 at mid-depth of SmolLM3-3B and OLMo3-7B, whereas the first-order frame, LexFrame[1], reaches 0.97 and 0.97 and reads a state within 0.16 nats of the model's output. Its coefficients attribute predictions more faithfully than attribution patching, its lexical basis steers a prediction while sparing other tokens, and its confident readings would let an early exit skip 0.45 and 0.42 of the layers, where lenses skip almost none. In this way, lexical reference frames make every hidden state of the model readable, attributable and steerable.

## 8. Xiaomi-Lens-0 Technical Report
**Upvotes:** 👍 27 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.23279`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23279) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23279.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23279)

### Abstract:
> Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) answer questions about whole images well, yet they still struggle with fine-grained region understanding. A key reason is that text-only supervision never specifies which regions support an answer. To close this gap, we present Xiaomi-Lens-0, a 4B MLLM built on Qwen3.5 that predicts masks from its own visual representations. Specifically, a lightweight decoder combines vision-encoder patch features with visual latents, the language model's mask-token states, so mask supervision reaches the shared backbone without an additional segmentation encoder. Because each latent decodes to a mask, the model can interleave reasoning with region-specific latents and thus think with visual latents, while grounded reasoning traces supervise the regions it visits during chain-of-thought. As a result, across all eight RefCOCO, RefCOCO+, and RefCOCOg splits, Xiaomi-Lens-0 gains 5.6 points on average over Qwen3.5 in referring comprehension and achieves the highest segmentation cIoU among compared models. Moreover, it surpasses Qwen3.5 across all perception benchmarks. Homepage: https://seerray-lab.github.io/Xiaomi-Lens-0{this https URL}

## 9. RAP: A Dataset for Paper-to-Poster Generation via Declarative Markup from Human-Crafted Exemplars
**Upvotes:** 👍 23 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.23230`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23230) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23230.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23230)

### Abstract:
> Making a conference poster is visual coding in miniature: an entire paper must be compressed onto a single page, which exercises summarization, visual design, and code implementation at once, using nothing beyond the paper itself. Yet benchmarks for this task in the context of visual coding remain scarce: existing resources are small and annotated only with layout boxes, so evaluation must fall back on VLM-as-judge or human checklists rather than objective render-and-compare, and the generation methods they support design layouts from scratch, leaving human-crafted posters unusable as either a retrievable design library or as verified supervision. We introduce RAP (Reconstruct A Poster), a dataset and benchmark for paper-to-poster generation as a visual coding task. RAP contains 25K+ verified paper-poster pairs from various machine learning conferences held between 2022 and 2026, annotated with a three-level content taxonomy, plus a held-out benchmark of 150 test papers each paired with its human poster. A fully automated derendering pipeline turns each poster into styled HTML/CSS of typed, refillable slots, so every sample is a paper-poster-markup triplet verifiable by rendering against the source poster. The corpus serves both inference and training: PosterRAG, a training-free multi-agent pipeline, retrieves an exemplar and refills its slots without touching layout or styling, while an SFT+RL recipe trains a dedicated model from the same triplets. An online demo is available at https://reconstruct-a-poster.github.io/.

## 10. Execution Before Memory: Diagnosing Memory Failures in Video World Models
**Upvotes:** 👍 23 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.23542`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23542) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.23542.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.23542)

### Abstract:
> A memory test for a video world model should say whether the scene was set, whether the asked change was carried out, and whether the last frame shows the result. Current tests fold these into one number, so a low score is easy to read as forgetting. When the change is not carried out, the hidden state transition cannot appear, so the test is not really a test of memory. We introduce MIST, an evaluation that separates the change from the memory it should leave behind. The model first sets the scene while the change stays hidden, and only later is asked to show it, with leakage before that point kept under control. This separation locates the bottleneck: the model sets the scene but does not carry out the instructed change. The same gap appears in general memory evaluations. On that basis we propose M3-Agent (Make the Missing Move), which recursively revises the details of the input instruction so that the model successfully carries out the requested instruction. Relative to the baseline, it more than triples the MIST reveal score. On the benchmarks, WRBench's score T rises by 26.1% and MBench-T's score U by 52.7%. The method meets the problem from the execution side: it shows what the frozen model can do once the move is stated, and it sets a stricter baseline.
