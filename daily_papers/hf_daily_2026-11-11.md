## 1. Learning Heterogeneous Tactile Representation for General Dexterous Manipulation
**Upvotes:** 👍 94 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.12806`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12806) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12806.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12806)

### Abstract:
> Tactile sensing is an indispensable modality for contact-rich dexterous manipulation, a capability that hints at the ultimate frontiers of embodied intelligence. However, commercially available tactile sensors are still at a relatively early stage of development, with each type of sensor offering irreplaceable strengths alongside its own drawbacks. A key challenge is thus to free tactile understanding from sensor-specific physics, so that embodied models can effectively leverage heterogeneous tactile data. To this end, we develop LingBot-Touch, a tactile foundation model characterized by the following properties. (i) Our model yields a unified representation across different tactile sensors (i.e., piezoresistive, magnetic, and visuotactile sensors), and is easily extensible to new sensor types. (ii) The resulting tactile tokens are plug-and-play: they can be seamlessly integrated into policy models across paradigms, from diffusion policy to vision-language-action (VLA) models such as π_{0.5} and LingBot-VLA, consistently enhancing manipulation dexterity. (iii) Extensive experiments, spanning simulation and real-robot deployments across multiple hardware platforms, demonstrate that our tactile representation substantially benefits complex manipulation tasks while maintaining strong computational efficiency. We believe the findings in this work demonstrate the potential of leveraging cross-sensor data to scale tactile learning, and offer encouraging insights for the design of future hardware and embodied models.

## 2. WorldFoundry: Towards Unified Infrastructure for World Intelligence
**Upvotes:** 👍 54 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.10771`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10771) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10771.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10771)

### Abstract:
> Visual generative models increasingly blur the line between synthesizing observations and simulating environments. Yet visual plausibility alone does not constitute successful world modeling: compelling outputs may lack persistent state, physical dynamics, or causally consistent responses to interactions. We define a generative world model as a predictive dynamical system that maintains a persistent, history-conditioned representation of an environment, updates that representation in response to actions or interventions, and generates temporally coherent trajectories that reflect outcomes under observed and counterfactual interventions. From this definition, we derive an intelligence-oriented framework spanning perceptual grounding, persistent state modeling, controllable transformation, dynamics-aware prediction, and closed-loop interaction. We present WorldFoundry, open-source infrastructure for reproducible inference and evaluation across video, 3D and 4D, embodied action, and interactive world systems. WorldFoundry unifies model and benchmark discovery, runtime and asset preparation, execution orchestration, artifact management, and interactive visualization, while modality-specific adapters preserve native inputs, execution semantics, output representations, and evaluation protocols. Explicit manifests, durable artifact contracts, and evidence-aware scorecards separate execution readiness from evaluative claims, making each claim traceable to its supporting artifacts. The catalog contains 270 model entries, 74 benchmarks, and 62 registered metric entries, with readiness and execution evidence recorded separately. WorldFoundry enables reproducible comparison of persistent, controllable dynamics across heterogeneous systems, advancing the field from perceptual synthesis toward measurable world intelligence.

## 3. Sage-Taiyi: A Metacognitive Architecture for Scientific Discovery
**Upvotes:** 👍 53 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.12762`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12762) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12762.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12762)

### Abstract:
> We introduce Sage-Taiyi, a metacognition-inspired architecture that uses accumulated scientific understanding to guide what to investigate next and how to investigate it. Through explicit monitoring and control, the system revises its understanding of evidence, assumptions, and unresolved questions, then uses these assessments to adapt research targets and select reusable reasoning methods. On Taiyi-Bench, an AI co-scientist ideation benchmark comprising 265 tasks from 50 papers across ten disciplines, Sage-Taiyi ranks first on all eight metrics of historical rediscovery and open-ended ideation, achieving an Overall score of 54.0 compared with 47.9 for the strongest baseline, AI Scientist-v2. It also performs well on two external scientific ideation benchmarks. Five expert-guided collaborations further connect its proposals to experimental and computational validation across chemistry, materials science, and engineering. In chemistry, reaction-condition optimization raises the yield of cobalt-catalyzed carbonylation from trace amounts to 84% and photocatalytic allylic amination from 13% to 69%. These results support metacognitive regulation as an approach to scientific ideation and its progression toward practical validation.

## 4. Toward the Era of Agentic Embodied Intelligence
**Upvotes:** 👍 49 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.09124`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09124) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09124.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.09124)

### Abstract:
> Foundation models increasingly guide robot actions, yet sustained autonomy requires efficient control and failure recovery. We introduce A2-MEAS, an embodied multi-agent framework built around code as a scaling interface. Learned correction codes combine corrective language, spatial evidence, execution history, and action proposals. MetaBrain provides training supervision and runtime guidance. A correction encoder and adapter absorb this knowledge into policy weights during training, while fresh codes adapt behavior with frozen weights at deployment. The action model, A2-WLA, adds motion-aware history and a latent world model to a vision-language-action policy. Surprisingly, this simple design with action pretraining and task specialization reaches 0.5755 Q-score on the 50-task BEHAVIOR 2026 public subset before online correction, 2.21x the 2025 winner's reported 0.2605 across different benchmark editions and training configurations. On all 100 BEHAVIOR 2026 public tasks, A2-MEAS achieves 0.6411 Q-score and 45.40% task success. On RoboTwin 2.0, A2-WLA achieves 93.98% average success, rising to 95.05% with the complete system. Across 12 real-robot tasks in five household settings, A2-WLA achieves 57.92% average success versus 26.67% for pi0.5. In long-horizon pour-over coffee preparation, a history-conditioned adapter raises post-failure completion at two filter-handling steps from 14% to 94% and from 8% to 82%, compared with A2-WLA retries. Our training infrastructure delivers 427.1 samples per second, 4.03x the throughput of a pi0.5 reference implementation on matched hardware. We will release the system, models, training scripts, and infrastructure to support efficient embodied learning.

## 5. PCBRouteAgent: How Close Are LLM Agents to Becoming PCB Routing Engineers?
**Upvotes:** 👍 41 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.08175`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08175) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08175.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08175)

### Abstract:
> Automating printed circuit board (PCB) routing requires coordinating board-level connectivity decisions with geometric execution under strict design rules. Although recent agents can interact with native routing tools, reliable routing on complex boards remains challenging. We present PCBRouteAgent, a multimodal PCB routing agent inspired by how human engineers coordinate global strategy with local execution. A Global Routing Planner combines board images and structured electrical and geometric information to specify connection topology, routing order, corridors, and layer transitions. A Detailed Routing Executor realizes these guides through KiCad's native routing engine and performs bounded local recovery. Unresolved failures are returned as structured feedback, enabling targeted guide revisions while preserving accepted routes. We evaluate PCBRouteAgent on 247 synthetic and real-world boards from four PCBWorld-Bench test splits. It achieves clean-pass rates of 100%, 94.9%, 100%, and 40% on D2, D3-A, D3-B, and D3-C, respectively. On the three real-world subsets, PCBRouteAgent outperforms the best evaluated baseline in clean-pass rate by 13.9, 36, and 10 percentage points, respectively.

## 6. Reframe: Observation-Aligned Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Visual Reasoning
**Upvotes:** 👍 38 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.12886`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12886) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12886.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.12886)

### Abstract:
> Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) offer complementary routes to post-training. For visual agents, however, tool use presents two related challenges. Models may overlook useful tool-returned observations, while poorly grounded actions can limit the evidence available for subsequent distillation. We introduce Reframe, a post-training framework that treats the observation state, rather than only the output distribution, as an object of alignment. First, Ground-Drop-GRPO (GD-GRPO) trains spatial and temporal specialists with evidence-aware rewards and stochastic removal of the original visual input, encouraging them to acquire grounded evidence and use the returned observations. Second, Observation-Gated-MOPD (OG-MOPD) replaces deficient tool actions with canonical ones mid-rollout and resumes distillation from the repaired observation. The two stages complement each other, as learning to rely on tool-returned observations can help the policy benefit from observation repair. Neither stage requires a task-specific SFT cold start. Extensive experiments over 14 image and video benchmarks demonstrate that Reframe outperforms both Mix-RL and vanilla MOPD. Ablation results show that GD-GRPO increases observation dependence and tool gain compared with outcome-based GRPO.

## 7. Flide: Accelerating Decision Inference with Exact State Forking
**Upvotes:** 👍 32 | **arXiv ID:** `2610.42148`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42148) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.42148.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.42148)

### Abstract:
> Decision models such as Jev have emerged as a highly promising paradigm for LLM agent systems, providing lightweight and reliable categorical reasoning for rapid System One execution. However, existing generation-oriented inference engines fail to support these workloads effectively, suffering from severe prefix recomputation overheads or distorted output probabilities. To address this challenge, we propose Flide, an efficient and exact inference engine that decouples the logical tree structure of a decision request from its physical execution within a single flattened token stream. Through ForkAttention, Flide navigates request dependencies using GPU-resident indices across both standard attention and recurrent Gated DeltaNet layers, guaranteeing in exact arithmetic that every query branch evaluates identically to an isolated forward pass. By expressing branch topologies directly in GPU-resident metadata, Flide accommodates diverse request mixtures with a compact set of pre-recorded execution graphs while enabling expanding agent histories to resume from checkpoints saved in earlier passes. Extensive evaluations on public hybrid and decision models demonstrate that Flide answers queries up to 9.5 times faster than vLLM and SGLang while staying closest to a float32 reference in total variation distance. By unifying physical execution throughput with mathematical exactness, Flide establishes a principled foundation for scalable, latency-critical decision intelligence in agent systems.

## 8. ReproBridge: Building Reproducibility into ML Research Artifacts
**Upvotes:** 👍 30 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.08452`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08452) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08452.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.08452)

### Abstract:
> Reproducing machine learning experiments requires reconstructing procedures from information scattered across papers, released code, and supporting artifacts. For coding agents, reproduction success can therefore depend on their ability to fill in missing details, rather than on the released artifacts alone. We ask whether preserving recovered procedures and decisions improves subsequent independent reproduction. We define a standardized artifact design comprising a refined codebase, environment setup, experiment launchers, and a reproduction guide, and introduce ReproBridge, an agent workflow for constructing these refined reproducible artifacts. ReproBridge-Bench provides 1,029 tasks from 387 experiments across 36 papers, anchored by five human-verified reference scores per task. Agents attempt tasks in fresh sessions with raw code or refined artifacts; a separate Auditor checks execution evidence. Across four evaluated models, refined artifacts raise mean reproduction success from 76.4% to 92.6% and reduce downstream API spending by 19.7% on average. The cross-model success-rate spread narrows from 21.6 to 3.9 percentage points, with all four models achieving mean success rates above 90%. Removing any component reduces success in the ablation study, with the largest losses from code and environment removal. These results suggest that preserving execution knowledge helps even models with lower raw-code success reproduce experiments more reliably and at lower API cost.

## 9. Longtail-SWE: High-Quality SWE Tasks for Underrepresented Languages
**Upvotes:** 👍 20 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.10304`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10304) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.10304.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.10304)

### Abstract:
> Challenging repository-level benchmarks remain limited for many programming language ecosystems, where filtering scarce candidates with ambiguous descriptions or defective tests can discard valuable engineering problems. We introduce Longtail-SWE, a benchmark of 202 issue-resolution tasks across 13 languages with limited coverage in existing repository-level benchmarks: Clojure, Dart, Elixir, Erlang, F#, Haskell, Julia, Kotlin, Lua, OCaml, R, Scala, and Swift. Its construction uses a solver--judge--repairer pipeline: a solver attempts each candidate task, and a judge reviews the task together with the solver's trajectory and test results to guide revisions to tests and task descriptions. These revisions aim to correct tests that reject valid alternative implementations or accept incorrect solutions while retaining challenging tasks and preserving their intended requirements. We evaluate nine model configurations, including frontier models, and analyze task-solving performance, consistency across repeated attempts, and resource usage. We release all benchmark instances, executable environments, complete construction and evaluation tooling, and agent trajectories, together with an additional set of easier tasks to support evaluation of smaller models.

## 10. InfraRSI: Harnessing Recursive Self-Improvement for Training Infrastructure
**Upvotes:** 👍 17 | **arXiv ID:** `2611.11558`

**Links:** [arXiv Page](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11558) | [Download PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11558.pdf) | [Hugging Face Discussion](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.11558)

### Abstract:
> While Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI) accelerates isolated algorithm and kernel synthesis, distributed LLM training depends on tightly coupled launch settings, operator kernels, and communication schedules, where single edits often hit out-of-memory (OOM) boundaries, compiler conflicts, measurement jitter, or silent numerical drift. We present InfraRSI, the agentic harness that turns open-ended human commands into verified training optimizations via a five-stage workflow: init locks editable scope and a fixed task contract over workload, loss, and timing invariants to prevent reward hacking, doctor profiles baseline phases and calibrates cluster noise, run co-optimizes launch knobs and code patches before re-verifying top candidates against the baseline and logging findings to a cross-hardware experience bank, and report and apply audit source paths, track failed-trial costs, and commit promoted changes. Rather than stepping greedily from a single incumbent, InfraRSI initializes from diverse experience-seeded configurations and fits global execution-time and censored peak-memory models across all trials, including OOMs. Using these models, it prices per-setting memory costs, revises miscalibrated LLM priors from live runs, and pairs memory-hungry optimizations with memory savers and verified code patches. Across four single- and multi-node dense and Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) LLM workloads, InfraRSI achieves a speedup of up to 2.75times and lifts mean Model FLOPs Utilization (MFU) from 17.4% to 30.0%, outperforming existing tuners and coding agents while transferring experience across GPUs. As it improves training infrastructure efficiency, InfraRSI forms a two-level Recursive Self-Improvement loop---revising priors online within runs and distilling experience across clusters---to push foundation model scaling.
